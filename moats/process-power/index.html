Process Power

When your way of working is better and can't be copied quickly, the process itself is the moat.

Contents

Overview

A process-power moat exists when a company's embedded, hard-won way of operating produces better or cheaper output, and that process is so complex and tacit that rivals cannot copy it quickly even if they can see it. It is earned slowly, through long organisational learning, and it resists imitation because it lives in the whole system, not any one document.

Unlike a patent, no single thing can be bought or licensed. The advantage is the accumulated, interlocking set of practices that took years to develop and would take a rival years to reproduce.

How it works

Step 1

The company refines its operations over a long time, accumulating tacit know-how across the organisation.

Step 2

The resulting process yields superior cost, quality, or speed that competitors can observe but not replicate.

Step 3

Because the know-how is distributed and tacit, copying it requires the same years of learning — the barrier.

Where you see it

Toyota Production System

Decades of refinement produced a way of manufacturing rivals studied for years yet struggled to match.

Amazon operations

Deeply embedded logistics and operational practice that competitors can see but not easily copy.

How to build it

  • Commit to continuous improvement over years; process power can't be bought, only accumulated.
  • Embed know-how across the organisation so it doesn't walk out with any one person.
  • Make the process a system of interlocking practices, hard to copy piecemeal.

Pitfalls

  • Process power is the slowest moat to build — there are no shortcuts.
  • It can ossify into rigidity if the process stops adapting to a changing world.
  • If the advantage rests on a few documented steps, it's copyable and isn't really this moat.

Frequently asked questions

How is process power different from just being well-run?

Being well-run can be copied by hiring good managers. Process power is a system so complex and tacit that rivals can't replicate it quickly even when they can observe it.

Why is it considered the hardest moat to build?

Because it can only be accumulated through years of organisational learning — there's no asset to buy and no single step to copy.
