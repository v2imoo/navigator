Pareto Principle (80/20)

Roughly 80% of results come from 20% of causes — find and focus on the vital few.

Contents

Overview

The Pareto Principle observes that outcomes are usually unevenly distributed: a small share of inputs produces most of the output. A minority of customers drive most revenue; a few bugs cause most crashes; a handful of habits shape most of your results. The exact numbers vary, but the shape — a vital few and a trivial many — recurs everywhere.

The model is a prioritisation lens. Find the 20% that matters and concentrate there, instead of spreading effort evenly across everything.

When to use it

Prioritising effort where a few inputs drive most of the results.

How to apply it

Step 1

Measure the distribution

Look at where results actually come from — which inputs, customers, or causes.

Step 2

Identify the vital few

Find the small share producing most of the output.

Step 3

Concentrate effort there

Direct your best resources at the high-leverage minority.

Step 4

Manage or drop the trivial many

Spend less on the low-impact majority, or cut it.

Common pitfalls

  • Treating 80/20 as an exact law rather than a rough, recurring pattern.
  • Neglecting the 'trivial many' entirely when some of it still matters.
  • Applying it without actually measuring where results come from.

Frequently asked questions

Is it always exactly 80/20?

No — the ratio varies (it might be 90/10 or 70/30). What's reliable is the shape: a small share of causes drives most of the effect.

How do I use it?

Measure where your results actually come from, then concentrate effort on the vital few inputs instead of spreading it evenly.
