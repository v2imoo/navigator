The Map Is Not the Territory

Every model is a simplification of reality — never mistake it for the real thing.

Contents

Overview

This model reminds us that our representations of reality — maps, plans, metrics, mental models themselves — are simplifications, not reality itself. A map is useful precisely because it leaves things out, but that means it can be wrong, outdated, or misleading. Confusing the map for the territory leads us to trust the model when the world has moved on.

The lesson is to hold models lightly: use them, but keep checking them against the messy reality they're meant to describe.

When to use it

Relying on a model, plan, report, or metric to understand reality.

How to apply it

Step 1

Recognise you're using a map

Notice when you're relying on a model, metric, or plan rather than direct reality.

Step 2

Ask what it leaves out

Every simplification omits things — identify what yours ignores.

Step 3

Check it against reality

Compare the map to the actual territory, especially when stakes are high.

Step 4

Update the map

Revise the model when the world changes; don't defend an outdated one.

Common pitfalls

  • Trusting a model or metric so completely you stop observing reality directly.
  • Forgetting that a useful map is useful because it omits detail — and can mislead.
  • Clinging to an outdated map after the territory has changed.

Frequently asked questions

What does 'the map is not the territory' mean?

That our models, plans and metrics are simplified representations of reality, not reality itself — useful, but always incomplete and potentially wrong.

How do I avoid the trap?

Keep checking your models against direct reality, ask what they leave out, and update them when the world changes.
