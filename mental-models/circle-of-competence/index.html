Circle of Competence

Know the boundary of what you truly understand — and stay inside it.

Contents

Overview

Your circle of competence is the set of things you genuinely understand well enough to judge. The model's power isn't the size of the circle but knowing exactly where its edge is. Buffett's point was that a person who knows the limits of their knowledge has an enormous advantage over a smarter person who doesn't.

Trouble comes not from what you don't know, but from confidently acting on what you only think you know. The discipline is to operate inside the circle, and to be honest and humble at its boundary.

When to use it

Deciding whether you actually understand something well enough to act on it.

How to apply it

Step 1

Map your circle

Be honest about what you truly understand versus what you're merely familiar with.

Step 2

Mark the edge

The most important part — know where your real competence ends.

Step 3

Stay inside it for decisions

Make consequential bets only where you have a real edge.

Step 4

Expand it deliberately

Grow the circle over time through genuine study, not overconfidence.

Common pitfalls

  • Mistaking familiarity for competence — having heard of something isn't understanding it.
  • Letting ego push you to act outside the circle to seem knowledgeable.
  • Never expanding the circle, so it shrinks in relevance over time.

Frequently asked questions

Does a bigger circle make you better?

Not necessarily — knowing the edge matters more than the size. A small, well-understood circle beats a large, fuzzy one.

How do I expand my circle?

Through deliberate, honest study over time — not by convincing yourself you already understand something you don't.
