How to Study a Great Person

Turn admiration into transferable principles you can actually use.

Contents

Overview

Reading about a remarkable person is entertaining; learning from them is different. The goal is to extract the transferable principles behind their success, separate from the luck and the circumstances you can't replicate.

This guide gives you a method.

The steps

Step 1

Read the story first

Understand the arc and the context before the lessons — start with a full profile like Steve Jobs or Warren Buffett.

Step 2

Extract the playbook

Pull out the repeatable principles, not the one-off events — every profile's playbook does this for you.

Step 3

Separate skill from luck

Use base rates and regression to the mean to avoid over-crediting outcomes that involved chance.

Step 4

Trace the models they used

Connect their decisions to the underlying [mental models](mental-models) — Buffett and margin of safety, Jobs and first principles.

Step 5

Include the flaws

Study the failures and the stains too — Coco Chanel and John D. Rockefeller both carry them. A sanitised model teaches the wrong lessons.

Key takeaways

  • Extract transferable principles, not one-off events.
  • Separate genuine skill from luck and circumstance.
  • Connect their decisions to underlying mental models.
  • Study the flaws — a sanitised story misleads.

Frequently asked questions

Why not just copy what successful people did?

Because much of any single success involves luck and specific circumstances you can't replicate. Extract the principles, and weigh them against base rates.

Why study someone's failures?

Because a sanitised, all-triumphs account teaches the wrong lessons. The mistakes and even the moral failures are part of an honest, useful model.
