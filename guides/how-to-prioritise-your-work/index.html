How to Prioritise Your Work

Spend your limited time where it actually moves the needle.

Contents

Overview

Productivity isn't doing more things; it's doing the right things. When everything feels urgent, a few simple tools cut through and point you at the work that matters.

This guide combines them into a prioritisation routine.

The steps

Step 1

Separate important from urgent

Use the Eisenhower matrix so important-but-not-urgent work stops losing to noisy, urgent tasks.

Step 2

Weigh impact against effort

Plot tasks on an impact-effort matrix to find the quick wins and avoid the money pits.

Step 3

Find the vital few

Apply the Pareto principle: the 20% of tasks driving most of the results deserve most of your time.

Step 4

Account for opportunity cost

Every yes is a no to something else — judge each task against its opportunity cost, not against zero.

Step 5

Know when to stop

Per diminishing returns, stop polishing once extra effort stops paying, and move to the next high-value task.

Key takeaways

  • Protect the important from the merely urgent.
  • Chase high-impact, low-effort wins first.
  • Judge tasks against their opportunity cost, not against nothing.
  • Stop when returns diminish — don't over-polish.

Frequently asked questions

What's the difference between important and urgent?

Urgent means time-sensitive; important means consequential. Many urgent things don't matter, and the most important work often has no deadline — which is why it gets neglected.

How do I stop over-polishing?

Watch for diminishing returns — when another hour adds little, ship it and move to higher-value work.
