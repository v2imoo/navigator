Larry Page

The co-founder whose relentless focus on big, audacious bets reshaped how a technology company thinks about ambition.

4 min read · 1998–present

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Larry Page, with Sergey Brin, co-founded Google and served as its driving product and engineering force, co-inventing the PageRank algorithm that made search dramatically better. But Page's distinctive contribution to how companies think is his insistence on 'moonshots' — pursuing ideas ten times better, not ten percent better, and tackling problems so large that most companies wouldn't attempt them. He pushed Google into self-driving cars, life sciences, and other audacious bets.

Page championed what he called a 'toothbrush test' for acquisitions — does it get used daily and improve lives? — and restructured Google into Alphabet in 2015 to let its ambitious long-term bets operate as separate companies. His philosophy that incremental improvement is a trap, and that the biggest opportunities lie in thinking an order of magnitude bigger, has shaped a generation of technologists. He is a study in audacious ambition and the discipline of thinking 10x.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Think 10x, not 10%

Page pushed for ideas an order of magnitude better, not incremental gains.

Do this: Aim for 10x improvement; incrementalism is a trap that caps your ceiling.
Principle 2

Pursue moonshots

He funded audacious bets — self-driving cars, life sciences — most firms wouldn't attempt.

Do this: Take on problems so large most competitors won't dare — that's where the biggest wins are.
Principle 3

Structure for long-term bets

Alphabet let ambitious projects operate as separate companies.

Do this: Structure the organisation so bold long-term bets aren't strangled by the core business.

In their words

Always deliver more than expected.
If you're changing the world, you're working on important things.
It's often easier to make progress on mega-ambitious goals than small ones.

Frequently asked questions

What is Larry Page's '10x' philosophy?

That companies should pursue ideas ten times better, not ten percent better — because incremental improvement is a trap and the biggest opportunities require thinking an order of magnitude bigger.

Why did Page create Alphabet?

To restructure Google in 2015 so its audacious long-term 'moonshot' bets — self-driving cars, life sciences and more — could operate as separate companies rather than be constrained by the core business.
