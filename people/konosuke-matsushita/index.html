Konosuke Matsushita

The impoverished apprentice who built Panasonic — and a philosophy of business as service to society.

4 min read · 1918–1989

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Konosuke Matsushita was born into poverty, sent to work as an apprentice at nine after his family was ruined, and with almost no formal schooling built Matsushita Electric — maker of Panasonic and National brands — into one of the world's largest consumer-electronics companies. Revered in Japan as 'the god of management', he became as influential for his management philosophy as for his products.

Matsushita believed the ultimate purpose of business was to serve society — to overcome poverty by producing an abundance of quality goods at affordable prices, as plentiful and cheap as tap water (his 'tap water philosophy'). He pioneered enlightened labour practices, a long-term orientation, and a sense of mission that put social contribution above short-term profit, while building a rigorous, decentralised management system. He is a study in rising from nothing, in business as service to society, and in a long-term, mission-driven philosophy of management.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Treat business as service to society

Matsushita's purpose was overcoming poverty through abundant, affordable goods.

Do this: Anchor the business in genuine service to society, not short-term profit alone.
Principle 2

Think in the very long term

He prized mission and durability over quarterly results.

Do this: Orient around a long-term mission; it outlasts and outperforms short-termism.
Principle 3

Rise from nothing through learning

With no schooling, he educated himself and built an empire.

Do this: Lack of formal education is no barrier if you commit to lifelong learning.

In their words

The purpose of business is to contribute to society.
Business should produce goods as abundant and cheap as tap water.
Failure is simply the opportunity to begin again, this time more intelligently.

Frequently asked questions

Who was Konosuke Matsushita?

The founder of Matsushita Electric (Panasonic), revered in Japan as 'the god of management' — who rose from poverty with almost no schooling to build one of the world's largest electronics companies.

What was Matsushita's 'tap water philosophy'?

His belief that business should overcome poverty by producing an abundance of quality goods at prices as affordable as tap water — part of his conviction that the ultimate purpose of business is to serve society.
