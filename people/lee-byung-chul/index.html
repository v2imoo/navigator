Lee Byung-chul

The founder who built Samsung from a small trading company into the foundation of a Korean industrial empire.

4 min read · 20th century

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Lee Byung-chul founded Samsung in 1938 as a small trading company in Korea, dealing in groceries and dried goods, and over the following decades built it into one of the country's largest and most diversified industrial groups. Through the turbulent mid-twentieth century, as Korea industrialised, he expanded Samsung into textiles, insurance, retail, and eventually electronics — laying the foundations of the sprawling conglomerate that his successors would turn into a global technology giant.

His great strategic conviction was in scale and diversification, and in building industrial capability in a developing nation — pursuing large-scale manufacturing and entering industry after industry as Korea's economy grew. His entry into electronics in the late 1960s, though Samsung was then a modest player, planted the seed of what would become the company's defining business. He ran the group with a strong, centralised hand characteristic of the Korean chaebol he helped pioneer.

His legacy is the foundation of the Samsung empire and, more broadly, a model of the diversified Korean industrial group — built on scale, ambition and the drive to build national industrial capability. The transformation of Samsung from his diversified foundation into a world leader in electronics and components fell to his son, Lee Kun-hee.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Pursue scale and diversification together

Lee built Samsung by entering industry after industry at scale as Korea industrialised.

Do this: In a growing economy, pursuing scale and diversification together can build an enterprise of enormous breadth and resilience.
Principle 2

Plant seeds whose payoff comes later

His modest entry into electronics in the 1960s planted the seed of Samsung's defining business decades later.

Do this: Planting seeds in new industries, even as a small player, can position you for a future you can't yet fully see.
Principle 3

Build national industrial capability

Lee's drive to build large-scale industry in a developing Korea aligned his enterprise with his nation's growth.

Do this: Building genuine capability where a nation needs it can create both business success and lasting significance.

In their words

Build the business the nation needs.
Think in terms of scale.

What was his strategic approach?
