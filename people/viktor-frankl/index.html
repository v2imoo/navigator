Viktor Frankl

He survived the concentration camps and emerged with one conviction: that the search for meaning is what keeps us alive.

Part I — The story

The story

Viktor Frankl was a Viennese psychiatrist who survived years in Nazi concentration camps, where he lost his wife, parents and brother. From that experience he drew the central insight of his life's work: that even in the most dehumanising suffering, human beings can choose their attitude, and that the deepest human drive is not pleasure or power but the search for meaning. His book Man's Search for Meaning, written in nine days, has sold tens of millions of copies.

Frankl founded 'logotherapy', a form of therapy built around helping people find meaning in their lives. He is a study in the one freedom that can never be taken — the choice of one's response — in meaning as the engine of resilience, and in transforming the worst of experiences into something that helps others endure.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Choose your response

Frankl held that the last freedom is how we respond to what happens.

Do this: Between what happens to you and what you do lies a space; your freedom and growth live in that choice.
Principle 2

Anchor to meaning

He found that a sense of meaning sustains people through suffering.

Do this: In hardship, a compelling 'why' is the strongest fuel for endurance; find or create the meaning that carries you.
Principle 3

Turn suffering into service

He converted his ordeal into work that helps others.

Do this: Where you can, transform painful experience into something that aids others; meaning often grows from contribution.

In their words

When we are no longer able to change a situation, we are challenged to change ourselves.
Those who have a 'why' to live can bear with almost any 'how'.
Everything can be taken from a man but one thing: the freedom to choose one's attitude.

Frequently asked questions

Who was Viktor Frankl?

A Viennese psychiatrist and Holocaust survivor who, from his experience in Nazi camps, developed the conviction that the search for meaning is humanity's deepest drive — set out in his book Man's Search for Meaning.

What is logotherapy?

The form of psychotherapy Frankl founded, centred on helping people find meaning in their lives as the primary path to psychological health and resilience.
