Kiran Mazumdar-Shaw

The brewer-turned-biotech founder who built India's largest biopharma company from a garage — against every barrier.

4 min read · 1978–present

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Kiran Mazumdar-Shaw trained as a brewer — unable to find a brewmaster job in 1970s India because of her gender — and instead founded Biocon in 1978 from a garage with a tiny sum, initially making industrial enzymes. Facing barriers at every turn as a woman entrepreneur in a field that barely existed in India, with little access to capital or skilled talent, she built Biocon into the country's largest biopharmaceutical company and a global player in affordable medicines.

Mazumdar-Shaw's strategic bet was on affordable innovation: using India's cost advantages to develop and manufacture biosimilars and insulin for the price-sensitive markets of the developing world, making advanced medicines accessible to millions who could never afford Western prices. She is a study in persistence against systemic barriers, in building an industry where none existed, and in the strategy of affordable innovation — serving vast underserved markets that the incumbents' pricing ignored.
