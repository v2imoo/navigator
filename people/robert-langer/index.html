Part I — The story

The story

Robert Langer is one of the most prolific inventors and scientist-entrepreneurs in history — an MIT professor sometimes called the 'Edison of Medicine', with over a thousand patents and a lab that has spun out dozens of biotech companies. His pioneering work on controlled drug delivery and tissue engineering reshaped medicine, and he was a co-founder of Moderna, whose mRNA platform produced one of the COVID-19 vaccines.

Early in his career his grant proposals were repeatedly rejected and his ideas dismissed; he persisted until they transformed fields. He is a study in bridging deep science and company-building, in the enormous leverage of a research lab that systematically turns discoveries into companies, and in persistence through early rejection.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Bridge science and company-building

Langer turns lab discoveries into companies.

Do this: Pair deep research with the will to commercialise it; the discovery only changes the world when someone builds on it.
Principle 2

Build a discovery engine

His lab systematically spins out companies.

Do this: Create a repeatable system for turning research into ventures, rather than hoping for one-off breakthroughs.
Principle 3

Persist through rejection

His early proposals were repeatedly turned down.

Do this: Foundational ideas are often dismissed first; persistence through early rejection is part of doing original work.
