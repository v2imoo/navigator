Julian Robertson

He built one of the great hedge funds — and seeded a whole generation of star managers who carried his name.

4 min read · 1980s–2000s

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Julian Robertson founded Tiger Management in 1980 and grew it into one of the largest and most successful hedge funds of its era, compounding around 25% a year for most of two decades through rigorous, research-driven long-short investing: buying the best businesses and shorting the worst. He closed the fund in 2000 after refusing to join the dot-com mania — a stance that looked wrong for a year and then spectacularly right.

Robertson's greatest legacy may be human: he trained and seeded dozens of protégés, the 'Tiger Cubs', who went on to run many of the most important hedge funds in the world. He is a study in disciplined fundamental research, in the courage to sit out a bubble you don't understand, and in compounding influence by developing people, not just returns.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Pair the best longs with the worst shorts

Robertson ran rigorous long-short books.

Do this: Express conviction on both sides: own the best businesses and bet against the worst, hedging market risk.
Principle 2

Sit out what you don't understand

He refused the dot-com bubble even as it soared.

Do this: It's better to miss a mania you can't justify than to abandon your discipline to chase it.
Principle 3

Compound through people

His Tiger Cubs multiplied his influence across the industry.

Do this: Developing and backing talented people extends your impact far beyond what you could do alone.

In their words

Our mandate is to find the 200 best companies in the world and invest in them, and find the 200 worst and short them.
Hear a story, analyse it, and buy aggressively if it feels right.

Who was Julian Robertson?

The founder of Tiger Management (1980), one of the great hedge funds, who compounded around 25% a year through long-short investing and mentored the 'Tiger Cubs', a generation of star fund managers.

Why did Robertson close Tiger Management?

He shut the fund in 2000 after refusing to join the dot-com mania; his value discipline looked wrong for a year, then proved spectacularly right when the bubble burst.
