Vijay Shekhar Sharma

The small-town founder who barely spoke English — and built the app that helped put a billion Indians into digital payments.

4 min read · 2010–present

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Vijay Shekhar Sharma grew up in a small town in Uttar Pradesh, the son of a schoolteacher, and struggled through an English-medium engineering college where he could barely follow lectures — teaching himself by reading. He founded Paytm and built it into one of India's largest digital-payments and fintech platforms, riding and helping drive India's leap into a cashless, mobile-first economy.

Paytm's breakthrough came when India's 2016 demonetisation suddenly made digital payments essential overnight, and Paytm was positioned to capture the surge — a case of relentless preparation meeting a sudden opening. Sharma's story, from small-town outsider with poor English to building a company used by hundreds of millions, is one of the defining entrepreneurial journeys of modern India. He is a study in self-education against disadvantage, in being positioned to seize a sudden market shift, and in building for the mass market of an emerging economy.
