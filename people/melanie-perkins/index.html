Melanie Perkins

The founder who made professional design possible for everyone — after more than a hundred investor rejections.

4 min read · 2013–present

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Melanie Perkins co-founded Canva to solve a problem she saw teaching students design software: the professional tools were powerful but impossibly complicated for ordinary people. Canva made graphic design simple enough that anyone could create polished visuals in a browser — democratising a skill that had required expensive software and training.

Her path was famously hard: she pitched more than a hundred investors and was rejected before finally raising money, persistence that became part of her story. Canva grew explosively on a freemium model — free for most users, with paid tiers for teams and advanced features — reaching hundreds of millions of users and becoming one of the most valuable software companies founded outside Silicon Valley.

Perkins's success is a case study in radical simplification — making a hard, expert task easy for everyone — and in the freemium model's power to reach enormous scale before converting the users who need more.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Radically simplify the hard task

Canva made professional design easy enough for anyone.

Do this: Take a task that needs expertise and make it simple for ordinary people.
Principle 2

Persist through rejection

Perkins pitched 100+ investors before her first raise.

Do this: Treat rejection as feedback and persistence as part of the job.
Principle 3

Grow with freemium

A generous free tier drove scale; paid tiers converted teams.

Do this: Use a free core to reach scale, then convert users who need more.

In their words

There's nothing quite like a good challenge to bring out the best in people.
The world's problems are opportunities in disguise.
