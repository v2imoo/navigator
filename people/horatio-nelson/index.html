Horatio Nelson

The admiral who won by breaking the rules — boldness, initiative, and trusting his captains.

4 min read · 1777–1805

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Horatio Nelson was the greatest naval commander of his age, whose victories — culminating in the Battle of Trafalgar in 1805 — secured British naval dominance for a century. His genius lay in boldness and unconventional tactics: he repeatedly broke with the rigid rulebook of naval warfare, taking daring risks that caught enemies off guard and produced decisive, not merely marginal, victories.

Nelson's leadership style, dubbed 'the Nelson touch', combined personal courage that inspired fierce devotion with a radical trust in his captains — he shared his intent and empowered them to use their initiative rather than await orders in the chaos of battle. At Trafalgar he broke the enemy line in an audacious manoeuvre and won a crushing victory, though he was killed in the moment of triumph. His combination of boldness, clarity of intent, and decentralised trust is a timeless model of leadership.

Nelson exemplifies bold, decisive leadership and the power of decentralised trust — sharing clear intent, empowering capable people to use their initiative, and taking daring risks for decisive rather than marginal outcomes. His 'touch' is a model for leading in fast-moving, chaotic conditions.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Share intent, empower initiative

Nelson trusted his captains to act on his intent, not await orders.

Do this: Communicate clear intent, then empower capable people to use their own judgement.
Principle 2

Be bold for decisive wins

He broke convention to win crushingly, not marginally.

Do this: Take calculated bold action aimed at decisive outcomes, not safe half-measures.
Principle 3

Lead from the front

His personal courage inspired fierce devotion.

Do this: Lead visibly and share the risk; it earns devotion and drives performance.

Frequently asked questions

What made Nelson a great naval commander?

Boldness and unconventional tactics — breaking the rigid naval rulebook to take daring risks for decisive victories — combined with 'the Nelson touch': inspiring courage and radical trust that empowered his captains' initiative.

What is 'the Nelson touch'?

His leadership style of sharing his intent clearly and empowering his captains to use their own initiative in the chaos of battle, rather than requiring them to await orders — decentralised trust that produced decisive wins.
