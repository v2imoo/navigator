Amadeo Giannini

The banker who served the people the banks ignored — and built the largest bank in the world on their loyalty.

4 min read · 1904–1949

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Amadeo Pietro Giannini founded the Bank of Italy in 1904 (later Bank of America) on a radical idea: banking for ordinary people. In an era when banks served only the wealthy and businesses, he opened his doors to the working class, immigrants, and small farmers — the people other banks turned away — pioneering small loans to individuals and lending based on character and hard work rather than only collateral.

After the 1906 San Francisco earthquake, while other banks' vaults were sealed shut, Giannini set up a plank-and-barrel desk on the docks and lent to people to rebuild on little more than a handshake — building fierce loyalty that fuelled his bank's rise. He pioneered branch banking across California and grew Bank of America into, for a time, the largest bank in the world. He is a study in serving an underserved market the incumbents ignored, in trust and relationship as the foundation of lending, and in building scale through loyalty.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Serve the market incumbents ignore

Giannini banked the working class and immigrants others turned away.

Do this: Serve the large, underserved market the incumbents dismiss — that's your opening.
Principle 2

Build on trust and relationship

He lent on character and handshakes, earning fierce loyalty.

Do this: Base the relationship on trust and genuine service; loyalty becomes your growth engine.
Principle 3

Show up when others retreat

After the earthquake he lent while rivals' vaults stayed shut.

Do this: Be there for customers in a crisis when others retreat; it earns lifelong loyalty.

In their words

I want to serve the people nobody else wants to serve.
Be a servant to the people and the people will reward you.
It is no disgrace to be poor, but it is no great honor either — so help people rise.
