Tenzing Norgay

On his seventh Everest expedition he reached the top — a Sherpa who became a symbol of his people's place in the mountains.

4 min read · 1930s–1986

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Tenzing Norgay was the most renowned of the Sherpas, the Himalayan people whose strength, skill and knowledge of the mountains made high-altitude expeditions possible. Everest was his life's obsession: he joined expedition after expedition — the 1953 ascent with Edmund Hillary was his seventh attempt — accumulating unmatched high-altitude experience before finally standing on the summit. His success made him a hero across Nepal, India and the Sherpa community, a symbol of recognition for people long treated only as porters.

Tenzing embodied persistence across years of failure and the dignity of mastery. He is a study in relentless persistence toward a single goal, in the deep expertise the 'support' roles truly hold, and in an achievement that lifted the standing of a whole people.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Persist across repeated failure

Tenzing summited on his seventh attempt.

Do this: A single great goal can be worth many failed attempts; persistence accumulates the experience that finally succeeds.
Principle 2

Own the deep expertise of your role

The Sherpas' skill made the climbs possible.

Do this: The so-called support roles often hold the deepest expertise; master yours and your value becomes undeniable.
Principle 3

Lift your people with you

His success elevated the whole Sherpa community.

Do this: A personal achievement can change how an entire group is seen; carry your community's dignity with you to the summit.

Frequently asked questions

Who was Tenzing Norgay?

The most renowned of the Sherpas, who with Edmund Hillary made the first confirmed ascent of Everest in 1953 — on his seventh attempt — becoming a hero across Nepal and India and a symbol of recognition for the Sherpa people.

Why is Tenzing Norgay significant beyond the summit?

His achievement lifted the standing of the Sherpa community, long treated only as porters, and embodied persistence — he joined expedition after expedition, accumulating unmatched high-altitude experience over years.
