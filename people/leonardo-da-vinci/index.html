Leonardo da Vinci

The insatiably curious genius who dissolved the line between art and science.

4 min read · 1472–1519

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Leonardo da Vinci is the archetype of the Renaissance polymath — painter of the Mona Lisa and The Last Supper, but also anatomist, engineer, inventor, and scientist whose notebooks brim with ideas centuries ahead of their time. His defining trait was an insatiable, boundless curiosity: he wanted to understand everything, and saw no boundary between art and science.

Leonardo's genius flowed from cross-disciplinary observation — studying anatomy to paint bodies, physics to depict water, optics to master light. He observed the world with relentless attention and recorded everything, connecting insights across fields others kept separate. He was also a notorious procrastinator who left much unfinished, a reminder that even the greatest minds are human. His method — curiosity, observation, and connecting knowledge across domains — is a model for creative thinking itself.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Stay insatiably curious

Leonardo wanted to understand everything, without limit.

Do this: Cultivate boundless curiosity; follow your questions wherever they lead.
Principle 2

Connect across disciplines

He drew insight from anatomy, physics and optics to paint.

Do this: Combine knowledge from different fields; the connections spark originality.
Principle 3

Observe relentlessly

He watched the world with extraordinary attention and recorded it.

Do this: Observe closely and record what you see; deep observation fuels insight.

In their words

Learning never exhausts the mind.
Simplicity is the ultimate sophistication.
Study the science of art and the art of science.
