Jacinda Ardern

She led New Zealand through a massacre and a pandemic with a new model of leadership — and left on her own terms.

4 min read · 2010s–2023

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Jacinda Ardern became New Zealand's prime minister in 2017 and drew global attention for a distinctive style of leadership — combining decisiveness with visible empathy and compassion. She was praised worldwide for her response to the 2019 Christchurch mosque shootings, uniting the country and swiftly reforming gun laws, and for New Zealand's early, science-led handling of the COVID-19 pandemic. In 2023 she resigned, saying she no longer had 'enough in the tank' to do the job justice — a rare, candid act in politics.

Ardern modelled that strength and kindness are not opposites in leadership. She is a study in empathetic yet decisive crisis leadership, in uniting rather than dividing in the aftermath of tragedy, and in the self-awareness to step down honestly when depleted.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Lead with empathy and decisiveness

Ardern paired compassion with swift action.

Do this: Kindness and strength aren't opposites; visible empathy plus decisive action is a powerful leadership combination.
Principle 2

Unite after tragedy

Her Christchurch response brought the country together.

Do this: In the aftermath of tragedy, a leader's choice to unite rather than divide shapes how a nation heals.
Principle 3

Know when to step down

She resigned honestly when depleted.

Do this: Self-awareness about your own capacity is a form of integrity; leaving well can serve the role better than clinging on.

Who is Jacinda Ardern?

New Zealand's prime minister from 2017 to 2023, known worldwide for empathetic yet decisive leadership — especially her response to the 2019 Christchurch shootings and the COVID-19 pandemic — before resigning candidly in 2023.

Why was Ardern's resignation notable?

She stepped down saying she no longer had 'enough in the tank' to do the job justice — a rare, candid act of self-awareness in politics, modelling that leaving well can be its own form of integrity.
