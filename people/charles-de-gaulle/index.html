Charles de Gaulle

The general who embodied a defeated France until it believed in itself again — a master of will, symbolism, and national resolve.

4 min read · 1940–1969

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

When France fell to Germany in 1940, Charles de Gaulle — then a relatively junior general — refused to accept defeat, fled to London, and declared himself the leader of Free France in a radio broadcast to a nation that had surrendered. He had almost no power, few followers, and no mandate, yet by sheer force of conviction and a genius for symbolism, he made himself the embodiment of French resistance and legitimacy, and eventually led France's provisional government after liberation.

De Gaulle returned to power in 1958 to resolve a national crisis, founded the Fifth Republic that still governs France, and restored the country's sense of itself as a great power. He understood that leadership is often about projecting a vision and a certainty that does not yet exist until others come to believe it — that a leader can will legitimacy into being through conviction and symbol. Aloof, stubborn, and supremely self-assured, he is a study in leadership as the embodiment of an idea.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Project the reality you want to create

With no power in 1940, de Gaulle acted as the leader of France until it became true.

Do this: Project the vision and certainty you want, and conviction can will it into being.
Principle 2

Refuse to accept defeat

When France surrendered, he simply did not — and that refusal seeded resistance.

Do this: A leader's refusal to accept defeat can itself become the seed of recovery.
Principle 3

Master symbolism

He made himself the embodiment of French honour and resolve.

Do this: Leadership is often the carrying of an idea — embody what you want others to believe.

Frequently asked questions

What did de Gaulle do in 1940?

With almost no power or followers, he fled to London and declared himself leader of Free France, and by sheer conviction and symbolism made himself the embodiment of French resistance and legitimacy.

What's the leadership lesson of de Gaulle?

That leadership can be the embodiment of an idea — projecting a vision and certainty that doesn't yet exist until others come to believe it, and restoring a people's belief in itself.
