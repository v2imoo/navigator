William James

He founded modern psychology and taught that truth is what works — and that we can act our way into a new way of feeling.

4 min read · 1870s–1910

Part I — The story

The story

William James is called the father of American psychology. His monumental Principles of Psychology established the field in the English-speaking world, treating the mind as a dynamic 'stream of consciousness' shaped by habit and attention. He was also a founder of pragmatism, the distinctly American philosophy holding that the meaning and truth of an idea lie in its practical consequences — 'the cash-value of an idea' is what it does in the world.

James wrote vividly on habit, attention, will and belief, arguing that we can often change our feelings by changing our actions, and that belief itself can be a legitimate, outcome-shaping choice. He is a study in judging ideas by their practical fruits, in the power of habit and attention to shape a life, and in the agency we have over our own inner states.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Judge ideas by their fruits

James's pragmatism measures truth by practical consequence.

Do this: Ask what difference an idea makes if it's true; its practical consequences are the test of its worth.
Principle 2

Act your way into feeling

He argued action can change emotion, not only the reverse.

Do this: When you can't think your way into a better state, act as if — behaviour often pulls feeling along behind it.
Principle 3

Master habit and attention

He saw these as the architecture of a life.

Do this: What you repeatedly attend to and do becomes who you are; manage habit and attention deliberately.

In their words

The greatest discovery of my generation is that a human being can alter his life by altering his attitude.
Act as if what you do makes a difference. It does.
We are mere bundles of habits.

Frequently asked questions

Who was William James?

The father of American psychology, author of the field-founding Principles of Psychology, and a founder of pragmatism — the philosophy that an idea's meaning and truth lie in its practical consequences.

What is pragmatism?

The philosophy, co-founded by William James, holding that the truth and meaning of an idea are determined by its practical effects and what it helps us do in the world, rather than by abstract correspondence.
