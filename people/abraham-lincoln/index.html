Abraham Lincoln

The leader who held a nation together through its darkest hour with resolve, humility, and a team of rivals.

4 min read · 1834–1865

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Abraham Lincoln led the United States through its gravest crisis, the Civil War, preserving the Union and ending slavery — and did it through a leadership style studied ever since. Largely self-educated, rising from frontier poverty, he combined unshakeable resolve on core principles with remarkable humility, patience and emotional intelligence in how he handled people.

Famously, Lincoln appointed his political rivals — men who had opposed and even scorned him — to his cabinet, absorbing their talents and ambitions rather than surrounding himself with yes-men. He led through persuasion and storytelling, absorbed blame while sharing credit, and held firm to a long-term vision through staggering setbacks. His restraint and magnanimity, even toward defeated enemies, defined his greatness.

Lincoln is a foundational study in leadership: holding firm on principle while remaining humble and adaptable with people, building a team stronger than your own ego, and leading through persuasion and long-term vision amid relentless adversity.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Build a team of rivals

Lincoln put talented opponents in his cabinet rather than yes-men.

Do this: Surround yourself with the most capable people, even those who challenge you.
Principle 2

Hold principle, flex on method

He was immovable on core goals but patient and adaptable in tactics.

Do this: Be firm on your core principles and flexible on how you get there.
Principle 3

Absorb blame, share credit

Lincoln took responsibility and gave others the credit.

Do this: Take the blame yourself and give the credit away; loyalty follows.

In their words

Whatever you are, be a good one.
I am a slow walker, but I never walk back.
With malice toward none, with charity for all.

Frequently asked questions

What was Lincoln's 'team of rivals'?

His decision to appoint his political opponents — men who had scorned him — to his cabinet, absorbing their talents and ambitions rather than surrounding himself with agreeable subordinates.

What defined Lincoln's leadership?

Firm resolve on core principles combined with humility, patience and emotional intelligence in handling people — leading through persuasion, absorbing blame, sharing credit, and magnanimity toward enemies.
