Mother Teresa

She devoted her life to the dying and destitute of Kolkata, building a global order around radical service.

4 min read · 1950s–1997

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Mother Teresa, born in what is now North Macedonia, moved to India as a young nun and in 1950 founded the Missionaries of Charity in Kolkata to serve 'the poorest of the poor' — the dying, the destitute, lepers and orphans whom society had abandoned. Her order grew into a global organisation with thousands of members running homes and clinics across many countries. She won the Nobel Peace Prize in 1979 and became, for many, a symbol of selfless compassion; she was canonised as a saint in 2016.

Her work and methods have also drawn scrutiny and debate. In fair presentation, admirers revere her as an icon of charity and humility, while some critics have questioned aspects of her care and finances. She is a study in a life of radical service, in building an enduring institution around a mission of compassion, and in a legacy held in deep reverence and some dispute.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Serve those everyone else ignores

She devoted herself to the abandoned.

Do this: The greatest need — and the deepest service — is often among those the world has forgotten; go to them.
Principle 2

Build an institution around the mission

Her order outlived and outgrew her.

Do this: To make compassion scale and endure, build the organisation that carries it beyond any one person.
Principle 3

Lead by doing the humblest work

Her authority came from the work itself.

Do this: Moral authority is earned by doing the hard, humble work yourself, not by directing it from a distance.

In their words

Not all of us can do great things. But we can do small things with great love.
If you can't feed a hundred people, then feed just one.
Peace begins with a smile.

Frequently asked questions

Who was Mother Teresa?

A nun who founded the Missionaries of Charity in Kolkata in 1950 to serve 'the poorest of the poor', building a global humanitarian order; she won the 1979 Nobel Peace Prize and was canonised a saint in 2016.

Is Mother Teresa's legacy debated?

Yes — admirers revere her as an icon of selfless charity and humility, while some critics have questioned aspects of her care and finances; her legacy is held in both deep reverence and some dispute.
