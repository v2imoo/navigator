Socrates

The philosopher who taught the world to think by relentlessly asking questions.

4 min read · c.450–399 BC

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Socrates is a founding figure of Western philosophy, though he wrote nothing himself — we know him through his students, chiefly Plato. His enduring contribution is a method of thinking: the relentless asking of probing questions to expose assumptions, test beliefs, and pursue truth. Rather than lecturing answers, he interrogated, forcing people to examine what they thought they knew and confront their own ignorance.

The 'Socratic method' — questioning your way to clarity — remains one of the most powerful tools for thinking well, used in law, education and problem-solving to this day. Socrates prized intellectual humility ('I know that I know nothing'), the examined life, and following reason wherever it led, even to his death: condemned by Athens, he chose to die rather than abandon his principles.

Socrates gave the world a discipline of thinking — questioning assumptions relentlessly, embracing intellectual humility, and following reason honestly — that underpins clear thinking in every field. To think well about any decision is, in part, to think like Socrates.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Question your assumptions

Socrates exposed hidden assumptions by relentless questioning.

Do this: Interrogate your own assumptions with hard questions before acting on them.
Principle 2

Embrace intellectual humility

He held that knowing your ignorance is the start of wisdom.

Do this: Start from what you don't know; humility opens the way to learning.
Principle 3

Follow reason honestly

He pursued truth wherever it led, even at great cost.

Do this: Follow the logic and evidence honestly, even when it's inconvenient.

In their words

Frequently asked questions

What is the Socratic method?

A way of thinking by relentlessly asking probing questions to expose assumptions, test beliefs and pursue truth — forcing you to examine what you think you know, still used in law, education and problem-solving.

Why is Socrates important if he wrote nothing?

His method of questioning and his example of intellectual humility and the examined life — preserved through students like Plato — became foundational to Western philosophy and to clear thinking in every field.
