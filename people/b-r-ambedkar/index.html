B. R. Ambedkar

Born into the lowest rung of a rigid hierarchy, he rose to write the rulebook of the world's largest democracy.

4 min read · 1920s–1956

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

B. R. Ambedkar was born into a community treated as 'untouchable' under India's caste hierarchy, faced relentless discrimination, and through extraordinary determination earned doctorates at Columbia and the London School of Economics — becoming one of the most educated people of his generation. As chairman of the drafting committee, he was the principal architect of the Constitution of India, embedding equality, fundamental rights and protections for the marginalised into the foundation of the republic.

He devoted his life to dismantling caste oppression and to the dignity of the downtrodden, and near the end of his life led a mass conversion to Buddhism. He is a study in rising against systemic injustice through education, in writing your values into durable institutions so they outlast you, and in the lifelong fight for the dignity of the excluded.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Educate your way to power

Ambedkar overcame systemic exclusion through relentless learning.

Do this: Where systems are stacked against you, education is among the few levers that can move you past the barrier.
Principle 2

Write your values into institutions

He embedded rights and equality into the Constitution.

Do this: To make values last, encode them in the rules and institutions that will govern long after you're gone.
Principle 3

Fight for the excluded

His life's work was the dignity of the marginalised.

Do this: Use whatever power you gain to open the door for those still shut out; durable change lifts the bottom.

Who was B. R. Ambedkar?

A jurist, economist and social reformer born into an 'untouchable' community who, despite severe discrimination, became the principal architect of India's Constitution and a lifelong champion of equality and the rights of the oppressed.

What was Ambedkar's role in the Constitution?
