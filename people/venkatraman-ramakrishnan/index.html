Part I — The story

The story

Venkatraman 'Venki' Ramakrishnan shared the 2009 Nobel Prize in Chemistry for determining the detailed atomic structure of the ribosome — the vast molecular machine that reads genetic instructions and assembles them into proteins in every living cell. Understanding its structure, atom by atom, was a monumental achievement that also illuminated how many antibiotics work, by jamming bacterial ribosomes. Born in India and trained across physics and biology, he later served as President of the Royal Society, Britain's premier scientific body.

He is candid about the role of luck, persistence and collaboration in science, and sceptical of its mythology of lone genius. He is a study in crossing disciplines to crack a problem, in patient structural work on a machine central to all life, and in honest reflection on how science actually gets done.
