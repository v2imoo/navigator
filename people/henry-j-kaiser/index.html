Henry J. Kaiser

The industrialist who built ships faster than anyone thought possible — and invented modern managed healthcare along the way.

4 min read · 1914–1967

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Henry J. Kaiser was one of America's great industrialists, famous above all for the shipyards that, during World War II, built cargo ships at a pace the world had never seen — applying mass-production and prefabrication techniques to shipbuilding so that a 'Liberty Ship' could be assembled in days rather than months. He had earlier helped build roads, dams (including work on the Hoover Dam), and whole infrastructure projects, always through process innovation and relentless speed.

Kaiser's lasting second legacy was healthcare: to care for his vast workforce, he built a prepaid medical system that became Kaiser Permanente, one of the pioneering models of managed care still serving millions. He is a study in process power — radically rethinking how something is produced to achieve step-change speed and cost — and in how solving your own operational problem (keeping workers healthy and productive) can create an entirely new enterprise.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Rethink how it's produced

Kaiser applied prefabrication and mass production to collapse shipbuilding time.

Do this: Achieve step-change speed and cost by rethinking the production process itself.
Principle 2

Compete on speed

His yards out-built everyone by making the process radically faster.

Do this: Make process speed a weapon competitors with old methods can't match.
Principle 3

Turn your own problem into a business

Solving worker healthcare created Kaiser Permanente.

Do this: An operational problem you solve for yourself can become a whole new enterprise.

In their words

Find a need and fill it.
Problems are only opportunities in work clothes.
When your work speaks for itself, don't interrupt.

Frequently asked questions

What was Henry Kaiser famous for?

His World War II shipyards, which built Liberty Ships in days rather than months by applying mass-production and prefabrication — and for founding Kaiser Permanente, a pioneering managed-healthcare system.

How did Kaiser Permanente start?

Kaiser built a prepaid medical system to care for his vast wartime workforce — solving his own operational problem of keeping workers healthy, which grew into one of the pioneering models of managed care.
