M. F. Husain

From painting cinema hoardings to global galleries, the 'Picasso of India' blazed a bold, contested modern vision.

4 min read · 1940s–2011

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Maqbool Fida Husain rose from painting cinema billboards in Bombay to become India's most celebrated and highest-profile modern artist — sometimes called the 'Picasso of India'. A founding member of the Progressive Artists' Group, he forged a bold, vigorous style fusing modernist technique with Indian themes, mythology and everyday life, producing a vast, prolific body of work over decades. His late career was marked by fierce controversy over some of his depictions of Hindu deities, which led to legal cases and threats that drove him into self-exile.

In fair presentation, Husain is celebrated as a pioneering modernist and criticised by others for works some found offensive. He is a study in rising from humble commercial art to the heights of fine art, in a distinctly Indian modernism, and in the clashes that can arise where artistic freedom meets religious sentiment.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Rise from the commercial to the fine

Husain went from billboards to global galleries.

Do this: Commercial craft can be the training ground for great art; don't disdain the humble start that builds your skill.
Principle 2

Forge a modernism rooted in your culture

He fused modernist technique with Indian themes.

Do this: Blend global technique with your own cultural material to create something distinctly yours, not derivative.
Principle 3

Reckon with the cost of artistic freedom

His work drew both acclaim and fierce backlash.

Do this: Bold expression can collide with deeply held sentiment; artistic freedom and its consequences are both real.

Frequently asked questions

Who was M. F. Husain?

India's most famous modern artist, the 'Picasso of India', who rose from painting cinema billboards to global galleries, co-founded the Progressive Artists' Group, and forged a bold modernism fusing technique with Indian themes.

Why was Husain controversial?

Some of his depictions of Hindu deities drew fierce objection, legal cases and threats that led him into self-exile late in life — a case where artistic freedom collided with religious sentiment, viewed very differently by his admirers and critics.
