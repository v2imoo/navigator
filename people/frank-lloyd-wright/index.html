Frank Lloyd Wright

The architect who reinvented how buildings relate to people and nature — across a seven-decade career.

4 min read · 1887–1959

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Frank Lloyd Wright was the most influential American architect in history, designing over a thousand structures across a career of seventy years and pioneering a distinctly American architecture. His philosophy of 'organic architecture' held that buildings should be in harmony with humanity and their environment — growing naturally from their site rather than imposed upon it — exemplified by Fallingwater, the house he built over a waterfall.

Wright rejected the imported European styles of his day to develop something original and rooted in the American landscape, from his low, horizontal Prairie houses to the spiralling Guggenheim Museum. Fiercely independent and famously difficult, he reinvented his work repeatedly and produced some of his greatest designs in his seventies and beyond. His insistence on a coherent, original vision — sustained over a lifetime — is a model of creative conviction.
