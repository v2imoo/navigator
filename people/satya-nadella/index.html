Satya Nadella

The executive who revived Microsoft by trading its know-it-all culture for a learn-it-all one — and betting on the cloud.

4 min read · 1992–present

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Satya Nadella took over as Microsoft's CEO in 2014, when the company was seen as a fading giant that had missed mobile and search, and engineered one of the great corporate turnarounds by changing its culture and its bets. He shifted Microsoft from defending Windows at all costs to embracing the cloud, subscriptions and even rival platforms.

Nadella reframed the culture around a 'growth mindset' — becoming, in his phrase, a learn-it-all rather than a know-it-all company — and made Azure cloud computing and Office 365 subscriptions the engines of growth. He also positioned Microsoft at the centre of the AI wave through a deep partnership with OpenAI, restoring it to the ranks of the most valuable companies on earth.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Change the culture to change the strategy

Nadella's 'learn-it-all' mindset unlocked the bets Microsoft had resisted.

Do this: Shift beliefs and culture first; new strategy follows what people think is possible.
Principle 2

Embrace recurring revenue and the cloud

He moved Microsoft to subscriptions and cloud services that compound.

Do this: Trade one-time sales for recurring, compounding cloud and subscription revenue.
Principle 3

Cooperate where you once only competed

He put Microsoft software on rival platforms, expanding reach.

Do this: Drop pride-driven rivalries when partnering serves the customer and growth.

Frequently asked questions

How did Nadella turn Microsoft around?

By changing its culture to a 'growth mindset', embracing the cloud (Azure) and subscriptions, cooperating with former rivals, and betting early on AI — multiplying the company's value.

Is Satya Nadella still Microsoft's CEO?

Yes — as of early 2026 he is chairman and CEO, having led the company since 2014.
