Christian Dior

The designer whose 'New Look' revived Paris fashion and built a house that anchors the modern luxury empire.

4 min read · 1946–1957

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Christian Dior transformed fashion almost overnight with his 1947 debut collection — nicknamed the 'New Look' — whose cinched waists and full skirts, extravagant with fabric after years of wartime austerity, restored Paris as the capital of couture and made Dior a global name in a single season.

Beyond the designs, Dior was a shrewd builder of a business. He licensed the Dior name across perfumes and accessories, understanding that a luxury house could extend its brand far beyond the garments only a few could afford. That model — couture at the pinnacle, accessible luxury products beneath — became the blueprint for the modern luxury industry.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Create a defining moment

The 'New Look' made Dior world-famous in one collection.

Do this: A single, bold statement can establish a brand faster than years of incrementalism.
Principle 2

Extend the brand beyond the elite

Licensing perfume and accessories reached customers couture couldn't.

Do this: Build accessible products beneath a prestige pinnacle to widen the brand's reach.
Principle 3

Build a house, not just a label

Dior structured a business designed to outlast him.

Do this: Design the brand and business so it can endure beyond the founder.

Frequently asked questions

What was the 'New Look'?

Christian Dior's 1947 debut collection, with cinched waists and full, fabric-rich skirts, which revived post-war Paris fashion and made Dior globally famous in a single season.

How does Dior relate to modern luxury?

Dior pioneered extending a couture house's brand into perfume and accessories, the blueprint for the modern luxury industry — and the house is now central to Arnault's LVMH.
