Dieter Rams

He designed objects so quietly perfect that decades later Apple was still learning from them — 'less, but better'.

4 min read · 1950s–present

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Dieter Rams, as chief designer at Braun for much of the twentieth century, created radios, record players, calculators and appliances of such clarity and restraint that they defined modern product design — and visibly inspired Apple's Jony Ive decades later. His philosophy was 'Weniger, aber besser' — less, but better — stripping away everything unnecessary so that an object's function spoke for itself, without decoration or gimmickry.

Late in his career he distilled his approach into 'Ten Principles of Good Design', a creed that good design is innovative, useful, aesthetic, understandable, unobtrusive, honest, long-lasting, thorough, environmentally friendly and 'as little design as possible'. He is a study in disciplined subtraction, in letting function drive form, and in the enduring power of restraint over ornament.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Subtract until only the essential remains

Rams removed everything that didn't serve function.

Do this: Design by taking away; strip features and decoration until only what's necessary and good is left.
Principle 2

Let function lead form

His objects expressed their use without ornament.

Do this: Make what a thing does clear through its form; honesty about function beats decoration every time.
Principle 3

Design for the long term

He prized longevity over fashion.

Do this: Build things meant to last and age well, not to chase trends; durability is a design value.

Frequently asked questions

Who is Dieter Rams?

The German industrial designer who, as chief designer at Braun, defined modern product design with radical simplicity — visibly influencing Apple — under his credo 'less, but better'.

What are Rams's principles of good design?

His ten principles hold that good design is innovative, useful, aesthetic, understandable, unobtrusive, honest, long-lasting, thorough, environmentally friendly, and 'as little design as possible'.
