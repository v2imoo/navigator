Barry Marshall

When no one believed bacteria caused ulcers, he drank a flask of them to prove it — and won the Nobel.

4 min read · 1980s–present

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Medical orthodoxy held that stomach ulcers were caused by stress and acid, and that no bacterium could survive the stomach's acid. Barry Marshall and Robin Warren proposed instead that a bacterium, Helicobacter pylori, caused most ulcers — and were dismissed and ridiculed for years. Unable to test on humans ethically and tired of waiting, Marshall in 1984 drank a flask of the bacteria himself, developed gastritis, and cured it with antibiotics — dramatically proving his case. The treatment of ulcers was transformed, and he and Warren won the 2005 Nobel Prize.

Marshall staked his own body on a theory the establishment rejected. He is a study in trusting rigorous evidence against entrenched consensus, in the willingness to bear personal risk to prove a point, and in how a dramatic demonstration can finally break through institutional resistance.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Trust evidence over consensus

Marshall held to his theory against the establishment.

Do this: When your rigorous evidence contradicts the consensus, the consensus may simply be wrong; follow the data.
Principle 2

Stake yourself on the proof

He infected himself to demonstrate the cause.

Do this: Sometimes conviction means bearing the risk yourself — though only where it endangers no one else; a decisive demonstration can break years of denial.
Principle 3

Make the breakthrough undeniable

His self-experiment was impossible to dismiss.

Do this: A dramatic, clean demonstration can shatter institutional resistance faster than any amount of argument.

In their words

Everyone was against me, but I knew I was right.
If you are not prepared to be wrong, you'll never come up with anything original.
Sometimes you have to do the experiment on yourself.

Frequently asked questions

What did Barry Marshall prove?

That most stomach ulcers are caused by the bacterium Helicobacter pylori, not stress or acid — famously by drinking a flask of the bacteria himself in 1984 to demonstrate it, overturning medical orthodoxy and winning the 2005 Nobel Prize.

Why did Marshall experiment on himself?

Unable to test ethically on humans and tired of years of ridicule from an establishment that rejected his theory, he infected himself to produce a dramatic, undeniable proof that bacteria cause ulcers.
