Paul Volcker

He took the pain no one wanted to, crushed double-digit inflation, and restored faith in the dollar.

4 min read · 1970s–2010s

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Paul Volcker became chairman of the US Federal Reserve in 1979, when inflation was running in double digits and eroding faith in the dollar and the economy. He did what was deeply unpopular but necessary: he raised interest rates to punishing levels — the prime rate topped 20% — deliberately inducing a severe recession to wring inflation out of the system. It caused real pain and fierce political backlash, but it worked, breaking inflation's back and setting up decades of stability.

Volcker was known for his towering integrity, independence and willingness to be hated in the short term for the long-term good. He later chaired a commission on financial reform and lent his name to the 'Volcker Rule'. He is a study in the courage to absorb short-term pain for a durable gain, in institutional independence under political pressure, and in integrity as the foundation of credibility.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Accept short-term pain for long-term gain

Volcker induced a recession to kill inflation for good.

Do this: Sometimes the right move is deeply painful now and clearly right later; have the nerve to take it.
Principle 2

Protect your institution's independence

He resisted intense political pressure.

Do this: Guard the independence that lets you do the unpopular-but-correct thing; credibility depends on it.
Principle 3

Let integrity anchor credibility

His reputation for honesty made his tough medicine trusted.

Do this: People will follow hard decisions from someone they trust; integrity is the foundation of real authority.

In their words

Frequently asked questions

Who was Paul Volcker?

The chairman of the US Federal Reserve (1979–87) who broke the double-digit inflation of the era by raising interest rates to punishing levels, inducing a severe recession but restoring long-term economic stability.

Why was Volcker's inflation fight significant?

He absorbed intense short-term pain and political backlash to wring entrenched inflation out of the US economy, setting up decades of stability — a defining example of courage and central-bank independence.
