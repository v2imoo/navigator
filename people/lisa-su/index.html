Lisa Su

The engineer-CEO who took a near-bankrupt chipmaker and made it a genuine rival to the giants.

4 min read · 2014–present

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Lisa Su took over as CEO of AMD in 2014, when the chipmaker was near bankruptcy and written off against far larger rivals, and engineered one of the most dramatic turnarounds in technology. A trained electrical engineer with a doctorate from MIT, she bet the company on a bold new processor architecture, Zen, and on disciplined long-term execution.

Her strategy was patient and technical: focus resources on a genuinely competitive product roadmap, rebuild trust with customers, and exploit rivals' stumbles. Under Su, AMD went from an also-ran to taking significant share in CPUs and, increasingly, in the AI-accelerator market, its stock rising many times over.

Su's turnaround is a study in focus and execution: choosing a small number of bets that mattered, executing them with engineering discipline over years, and letting a superior roadmap compound into market share.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Focus resources on what matters

Su concentrated AMD's limited resources on a genuinely competitive roadmap.

Do this: When resources are scarce, focus everything on the few bets that decide the war.
Principle 2

Execute patiently over years

The turnaround came from disciplined, multi-year delivery, not a quick fix.

Do this: Commit to a multi-year roadmap and execute it relentlessly.
Principle 3

Rebuild trust through delivery

Consistently shipping competitive products won back customers.

Do this: Win back trust by delivering, repeatedly, on what you promised.

In their words

Focus on the big problems, the hard problems.
People want to work on things that matter.
You have to bet on yourself.

Frequently asked questions

What did Lisa Su do at AMD?

She took over a near-bankrupt company in 2014 and, by focusing on the Zen architecture and disciplined execution, turned it into a genuine rival to far larger chipmakers.

Why is her turnaround notable?

It shows the power of focus and patient execution — concentrating scarce resources on a few decisive bets and delivering them over years until a superior roadmap won market share.
