Ratan Tata

The chairman who took India's most trusted house global — with bold acquisitions and unbending integrity.

4 min read · 1991–2012

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Ratan Tata took the chairmanship of the Tata group in 1991, just as India began to open its economy, and over two decades transformed a sprawling, inward-looking Indian conglomerate into a global enterprise. He consolidated the group's control, drove it to compete worldwide, and made a series of landmark international acquisitions — Tetley, Corus steel, and most famously Jaguar Land Rover — that announced Indian business had arrived on the global stage.

Tata paired boldness with a reputation for deep integrity and ethics that made the Tata name a byword for trust in India. He championed the Nano, an attempt to build a car affordable to the Indian masses — a commercial disappointment but a statement of purpose — and directed much of the group's profits, through its charitable trusts, to philanthropy. He is a study in transforming a legacy institution for a new era, in the value-building power of integrity and trust, and in balancing bold ambition with enduring values.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Transform the legacy institution

Tata turned an inward Indian conglomerate into a global competitor.

Do this: Modernise and globalise a legacy institution rather than merely preserving it.
Principle 2

Make trust the deepest asset

The Tata name's integrity was its most durable competitive advantage.

Do this: Build a reputation for integrity; trust compounds into a moat competitors can't buy.
Principle 3

Pair bold bets with values

He made daring acquisitions while directing profits to philanthropy.

Do this: Combine bold ambition with enduring values and purpose, not one at the other's expense.
