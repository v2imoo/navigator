Anita Roddick

The founder who proved a business could campaign for its values — and built a global brand with activism as marketing.

4 min read · 1976–2007

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Anita Roddick founded The Body Shop in 1976 to support her family while her husband was away, and built it into a global cosmetics brand that pioneered ethical, activist business decades before it became fashionable. She opposed animal testing, championed fair trade with developing-world suppliers, used natural ingredients and refillable containers, and turned her shops into platforms for social and environmental campaigns — making values not a cost but the core of the brand.

Roddick spent almost nothing on conventional advertising; instead, the company's activism and distinctive stance were its marketing, attracting customers who shared its values and generating word of mouth and press no ad budget could buy. She proved that a business could be a force for activism and still thrive commercially. She is a study in building a brand on authentic values, in activism as marketing, and in pioneering ethical business before the market demanded it.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Make values the core, not a veneer

Roddick built The Body Shop's whole identity on authentic ethics.

Do this: Build genuine values into the core of the business; customers reward authenticity.
Principle 2

Use activism as marketing

Her campaigns attracted aligned customers without ad spend.

Do this: A distinctive, authentic stance can be more powerful marketing than any ad budget.
Principle 3

Lead the market, don't follow it

She pioneered ethical business before it was fashionable.

Do this: Lead on values ahead of the market; being early and authentic builds a loyal base.
