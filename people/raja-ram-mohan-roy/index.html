Raja Ram Mohan Roy

He fought to abolish sati, championed women's rights and modern education — the first great reformer of modern India.

4 min read · 1800s–1833

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Raja Ram Mohan Roy is called the father of the Indian Renaissance and a maker of modern India. A scholar of many languages and traditions, he campaigned against social evils — above all the practice of sati (widow burning), whose abolition he helped bring about — and for women's rights, property rights, press freedom and modern, scientific education. He founded the Brahmo Samaj, a reform movement that sought a rational, monotheistic form of Hinduism stripped of superstition and ritual.

Roy combined deep respect for Indian traditions with openness to reason and modern knowledge, reforming from within rather than rejecting his heritage. He is a study in reform grounded in your own tradition, in using scholarship and argument to change entrenched practice, and in the courage to confront cruelty sanctioned by custom.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Reform from within the tradition

Roy changed Hinduism from inside, not by rejecting it.

Do this: Lasting reform often comes from those who respect a tradition enough to purify it, not from outright rejection.
Principle 2

Use scholarship to change practice

His arguments drew on deep learning.

Do this: Rigorous scholarship and reasoned argument can dislodge entrenched custom; master the sources to change them.
Principle 3

Confront cruelty sanctioned by custom

He fought sati despite its sanction by tradition.

Do this: That a cruelty is customary does not make it right; have the courage to name and fight it.
