Ruth Bader Ginsburg

She dismantled legal sex discrimination case by careful case — then became a dissenting icon on the Supreme Court.

4 min read · 1970s–2020

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Ruth Bader Ginsburg faced discrimination as one of few women in her Harvard Law class, then as a lawyer who couldn't get hired despite finishing top of her class. She responded by becoming the leading legal strategist against sex discrimination, arguing a series of carefully chosen cases before the Supreme Court in the 1970s that dismantled laws treating men and women differently — sometimes using male plaintiffs to show discrimination cut both ways. Appointed to the Supreme Court in 1993, she became known for her precise opinions and, later in life, her powerful dissents, becoming a cultural icon as 'Notorious RBG'.

Her method was patient, incremental and strategic — changing the law one deliberate case at a time. She is a study in strategic, incremental change, in turning personal injustice into a career of reform, and in the long game of moving institutions through precedent.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Change the system incrementally

Ginsburg dismantled discrimination case by careful case.

Do this: Lasting legal and institutional change often comes not in one leap but through a patient, strategic sequence of wins.
Principle 2

Choose your cases strategically

She picked cases that would set the right precedent.

Do this: Pick your battles for leverage, not just merit; the right test case can shift a whole system.
Principle 3

Turn injustice into purpose

Her own discrimination fuelled her life's work.

Do this: Let the injustice you've experienced sharpen your mission to dismantle it for others.

In their words

Frequently asked questions

Why is Ruth Bader Ginsburg significant?

As a lawyer she was the leading strategist against sex discrimination, dismantling unequal laws case by case in the 1970s, then served on the US Supreme Court from 1993, becoming famous for her opinions and dissents.

What was Ginsburg's legal strategy?

Patient and incremental — arguing carefully chosen cases, sometimes with male plaintiffs to show discrimination cut both ways, to change the law one deliberate precedent at a time.
