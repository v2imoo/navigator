Ted Turner

The founder who invented the 24-hour news channel when everyone said no one would watch.

4 min read · 1970–2006

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Ted Turner reshaped television by seeing opportunities the established networks dismissed. He turned a small Atlanta station into a national 'superstation' beamed by satellite to cable systems, and then, in 1980, launched CNN — the first 24-hour news channel — against near-universal scepticism that anyone would watch news around the clock.

Turner understood that cable and satellite were changing the economics of television, creating room for niche, always-on channels the big three networks couldn't serve. CNN's live, continuous coverage redefined news, especially during major events, and made Turner a pioneer of the modern media landscape.

A flamboyant risk-taker, Turner also became one of America's largest landowners and a major philanthropist. His bet on 24-hour news is a classic case of spotting how a technology shift opens space incumbents refuse to see.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Ride the technology shift

Turner used satellite and cable to reach audiences the networks couldn't.

Do this: Exploit a technology shift that changes your industry's economics.
Principle 2

Serve what incumbents dismiss

Everyone said 24-hour news would fail; he built it anyway.

Do this: Pursue the opportunity established players are too complacent to take.
Principle 3

Commit fully to the bet

Turner staked his company on CNN when the outcome was far from certain.

Do this: When conviction is high, commit hard rather than hedging into mediocrity.

Frequently asked questions

What did Ted Turner pioneer?

The 24-hour news channel — CNN, launched in 1980 — and the satellite cable 'superstation', both by exploiting how cable and satellite changed television's economics.

Why was CNN considered a risky bet?

Because almost no one believed audiences would watch news around the clock; Turner built it anyway and redefined the industry.
