Terry Gou

He built the vast, invisible factory floor where much of the world's electronics — including the iPhone — is actually made.

4 min read · 1970s–present

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Terry Gou founded Hon Hai Precision — known globally as Foxconn — in 1974 with a small sum, starting with plastic parts and building it into the world's largest contract electronics manufacturer. Foxconn assembles products for nearly every major technology brand, most famously Apple's iPhone, operating enormous factory complexes employing hundreds of thousands of workers. Gou built an operation of extraordinary scale, speed and manufacturing precision that became indispensable to the global electronics industry.

His scale and labour practices have drawn both awe and scrutiny over working conditions. He is a study in the under-seen power of manufacturing at massive scale, in building the operational backbone that brands depend on, and in the relentless execution required to make complex products reliably, by the hundred million.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Own the operational backbone

Gou built the factory the whole industry relies on.

Do this: Being the indispensable operations partner behind many brands is a powerful, if invisible, position.
Principle 2

Compete on scale and execution

Foxconn wins on size, speed and precision.

Do this: In manufacturing, relentless operational excellence at scale is itself the moat; execution is the product.
Principle 3

Make the complex reliable at volume

He delivers intricate products by the hundred million.

Do this: The hard, valuable skill is producing something complex consistently at enormous volume — master that and brands need you.

Frequently asked questions

Who is Terry Gou?

The founder of Foxconn (Hon Hai), the world's largest contract electronics manufacturer, which assembles products — most famously Apple's iPhone — for nearly every major technology brand at enormous scale.

Why is Foxconn important?

It is the vast, largely invisible manufacturing backbone of the global electronics industry; its scale, speed and precision make it indispensable to the brands whose products it assembles.
