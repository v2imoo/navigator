Maulana Abul Kalam Azad

A scholar who opposed Partition and built independent India's education system — champion of a united, pluralist nation.

4 min read · 1910s–1958

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Maulana Abul Kalam Azad was a distinguished Islamic scholar, journalist and senior leader of the Indian independence movement — one of the youngest ever presidents of the Congress. A passionate advocate of Hindu-Muslim unity, he opposed the Partition of India, arguing for a single, pluralist nation. After independence he became India's first Minister of Education, laying the foundations of the country's modern education system, including institutions like the IITs and the UGC.

His commitment to education, pluralism and reasoned faith made him a towering figure; India marks National Education Day on his birthday. He is a study in uniting deep religious scholarship with modern nation-building, in standing for pluralism against the tide of division, and in building the institutions of knowledge for a new nation.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Stand for pluralism against the tide

Azad opposed Partition and championed unity.

Do this: Hold to a pluralist, unifying vision even when division is ascendant; the harder stand can be the right one.
Principle 2

Build the institutions of knowledge

He founded India's modern education framework.

Do this: Investing in educational institutions shapes a nation for generations; build the structures that outlast you.
Principle 3

Unite faith and reason

He joined deep scholarship with modern nation-building.

Do this: Religious depth and modern, rational nation-building need not conflict; the two can reinforce each other.

Frequently asked questions

Who was Maulana Azad?

A distinguished Islamic scholar and senior leader of India's independence movement who opposed Partition, championed Hindu-Muslim unity, and became independent India's first Education Minister, founding its modern education system.

What was Azad's legacy in education?

As India's first Education Minister he laid the foundations of the country's modern education system — including institutions like the IITs and the University Grants Commission — and India marks National Education Day on his birthday.
