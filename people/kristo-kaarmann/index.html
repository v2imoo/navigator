Kristo Käärmann

Stung by hidden bank fees on his own transfers, he built the company that exposed and undercut them worldwide.

4 min read · 2010s–present

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Kristo Käärmann, an Estonian living in London, grew furious at the hidden fees and poor exchange rates banks charged on international transfers — a problem he and co-founder Taavet Hinrikus first solved informally between themselves. In 2011 they founded TransferWise (later Wise) around a clever model that matched opposing currency flows to avoid moving money across borders, passing the savings to customers and showing them the true cost upfront.

Wise built its brand on radical transparency, publicly shaming the hidden fees of banks. He is a study in turning a personal frustration into a company, in exposing an industry's hidden costs as a growth strategy, and in a clever operational model that structurally undercuts incumbents.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Build from your own frustration

Käärmann solved a problem he personally suffered.

Do this: A pain you feel acutely yourself is often a real market need; your frustration is market research.
Principle 2

Expose the hidden cost

Wise publicly shamed banks' concealed fees.

Do this: Making an industry's hidden costs visible can be both a moral stance and a powerful growth strategy.
Principle 3

Find the structural workaround

Matching flows avoided costly cross-border moves.

Do this: A clever operational model that structurally lowers your costs lets you undercut incumbents sustainably, not just temporarily.
