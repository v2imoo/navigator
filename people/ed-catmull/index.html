Ed Catmull

The computer scientist who made computer animation possible — and built a culture that sustained creativity.

4 min read · 1979–2019

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Ed Catmull spent his career pursuing a single dream — to make a full-length film with computer-generated animation — and in achieving it, co-founded Pixar and helped create an entirely new art form. A computer scientist by training, he pioneered foundational graphics techniques, then built the technical and creative organisation that produced Toy Story and a run of classics.

Catmull's deeper contribution was managerial: he thought as rigorously about how to sustain creativity as about rendering pixels. He built a culture — candid feedback through the 'Braintrust', protecting the fragile early stages of ideas, and treating failure as information — that let Pixar produce hit after hit, a feat almost unheard of in creative industries.

Catmull — whose Pixar was funded and shaped by Steve Jobs — wrote Creativity, Inc. to share how he managed the tension between creative freedom and disciplined execution. He's a case study in building an organisation where creativity can reliably thrive.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Protect fragile early ideas

Catmull shielded ideas in their ugly early stages from premature judgement.

Do this: Give new ideas room to develop before subjecting them to harsh evaluation.
Principle 2

Build candid feedback into the culture

The Braintrust gave honest critique without authority over the creator.

Do this: Create a forum for candid feedback that informs without dictating.
Principle 3

Treat failure as information

He made failure a normal, useful part of the creative process.

Do this: Normalise failure as data, so people take the creative risks that produce hits.

In their words

Failure is a manifestation of learning and exploration.
If you give a good idea to a mediocre team, they will screw it up.
Getting the team right is the necessary precursor to getting the ideas right.

Frequently asked questions

What did Ed Catmull achieve?

He pioneered foundational computer-graphics techniques, co-founded Pixar, helped create the first fully computer-animated feature (Toy Story), and built a culture that produced a rare unbroken run of hits.

What is the Pixar 'Braintrust'?

Catmull's forum for candid, honest feedback on films in progress — critique that informs the creators without holding authority over them, protecting quality without crushing ownership.
