Margaret Hamilton

She led the code that landed Apollo 11 — and her error-handling design saved the mission in its final seconds.

4 min read · 1960s–present

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Margaret Hamilton led the team at MIT that wrote the onboard flight software for NASA's Apollo missions — at a time when software was so new it wasn't considered real engineering. She insisted on rigorous error detection and recovery, designing the system to prioritise critical tasks and discard low-priority ones under overload. When Apollo 11's computer became overloaded with alarms during the lunar descent, it was exactly this design that let the computer shed non-essential work and keep the landing on track.

Hamilton championed the term 'software engineering' to give the discipline the seriousness it deserved, and later built companies around rigorous systems design. She received the Presidential Medal of Freedom in 2016. She is a study in anticipating failure and designing for it, in taking a nascent craft seriously enough to make it a discipline, and in the quiet competence that holds up when everything is on the line.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Design for failure first

Hamilton built error-handling that saved the Apollo 11 landing.

Do this: Assume things will go wrong under load; design the system to degrade gracefully, not collapse.
Principle 2

Prioritise ruthlessly under overload

Her software shed low-priority tasks to protect the critical ones.

Do this: Decide in advance what gets dropped when resources run out, so the essential survives the crunch.
Principle 3

Name your discipline to dignify it

She pushed 'software engineering' to make the craft taken seriously.

Do this: Giving rigorous work a serious name changes how it's funded, taught and respected.

Frequently asked questions

What did Margaret Hamilton do for Apollo?

She led the MIT team that wrote the Apollo flight software, and her rigorous error-handling design let the Apollo 11 computer shed non-essential tasks during an overload in the final seconds of the lunar descent, saving the landing.

Did Margaret Hamilton coin 'software engineering'?

She was among those who championed the term in the 1960s to establish software as a serious engineering discipline, at a time when it was not treated as one.
