Claude C. Hopkins

He insisted every ad be measured — founding the idea that advertising is a science of results, not opinion.

4 min read · 1900s–1930s

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Claude Hopkins was one of the earliest and most rigorous advertising pioneers, insisting that advertising be treated as a science measured by results. He championed testing, tracking and coupons to know exactly which ads actually sold, pioneered techniques like sampling and reason-why copy, and famously made Schlitz beer a leader by simply describing its (industry-standard) purification process that no one else had bothered to explain. His 1923 book Scientific Advertising remains a foundational text — David Ogilvy said no one should work in advertising without reading it seven times.

Hopkins's core belief was that every advertising dollar should be accountable to measurable sales. He is a study in making marketing measurable, in testing before scaling, and in the power of being the first to simply explain what others take for granted.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Make every ad accountable

Hopkins measured advertising by actual sales.

Do this: Track and measure your marketing against real results; what you can't measure, you can't improve.
Principle 2

Test before you scale

He used coupons and tests to learn what worked.

Do this: Run small, measurable tests to find what actually sells before committing a big budget.
Principle 3

Explain what others take for granted

His Schlitz campaign described a process rivals ignored.

Do this: Sometimes the winning message is simply being first to clearly explain something your whole category assumes.

In their words

The only purpose of advertising is to make sales.
Advertising is salesmanship in print.
The time has come when advertising has in some hands reached the status of a science.

Who was Claude Hopkins?
