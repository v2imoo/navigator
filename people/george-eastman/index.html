George Eastman

The founder who made photography simple enough for everyone — and pioneered the razor-and-blades model a century early.

4 min read · 1880–1932

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

George Eastman transformed photography from a complex, expensive craft practised by professionals into something anyone could do, founding Kodak on the slogan 'You press the button, we do the rest'. By inventing roll film and a simple, affordable camera, he removed the technical barriers that had kept photography out of ordinary hands, creating a vast new mass market where none had existed — a classic case of democratising a gatekept capability.

Eastman's business model was prophetic: he sold cameras relatively cheaply to get them into people's hands, then made his profits on the film and processing they bought repeatedly — the 'razor-and-blades' model, decades before Gillette made it famous. He was also a pioneering philanthropist who gave away most of his fortune. He is a study in radical simplification to create a mass market, and in the razor-and-blades model of locking in recurring revenue through a cheap initial product.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Remove the barriers to a mass market

Eastman made photography simple and cheap enough for anyone.

Do this: Strip out the complexity that gatekeeps a capability, and a mass market appears.
Principle 2

Use the razor-and-blades model

Cheap cameras sold the film and processing that made the real profit.

Do this: Sell the device cheaply to lock in profitable, recurring consumable revenue.
Principle 3

Sell simplicity as the product

'You press the button, we do the rest' made ease itself the promise.

Do this: Make ease and simplicity the headline promise, not the underlying technology.

In their words

You press the button, we do the rest.
Nothing is more important than our customers' trust.
