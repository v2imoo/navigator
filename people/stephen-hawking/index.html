Stephen Hawking

Told he had two years to live, he spent five decades unlocking black holes — and making the cosmos a bestseller.

4 min read · 1970s–2018

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Stephen Hawking was diagnosed with motor neurone disease at 21 and given a few years to live; he went on to become one of the most influential physicists of his age, working for decades as the disease gradually paralysed him, eventually communicating through a single cheek muscle and a speech synthesiser. His great discovery, 'Hawking radiation', showed that black holes are not entirely black but slowly emit radiation and can evaporate — a profound link between gravity, quantum mechanics and thermodynamics.

His bestseller A Brief History of Time brought cosmology to millions, and he became a global symbol of the mind's triumph over the body. He is a study in profound work done against overwhelming physical adversity, in bridging the deepest theories of physics, and in making the hardest science accessible and inspiring to everyone.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Work on regardless of adversity

Hawking did his greatest work as the disease progressed.

Do this: Extreme limitation need not stop the mind; focus your remaining capacities fiercely on what only you can do.
Principle 2

Bridge the hardest theories

His work linked gravity, quantum mechanics and thermodynamics.

Do this: The richest discoveries often sit at the junction of fields thought incompatible; work the boundaries.
Principle 3

Make hard science accessible

A Brief History of Time reached millions.

Do this: Translating the deepest ideas for a wide audience multiplies their impact and inspires the next generation.
