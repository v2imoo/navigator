Naval Ravikant

The angel investor whose aphorisms on wealth, judgement and happiness reached millions.

4 min read · 1999–present

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Naval Ravikant co-founded AngelList, which democratised startup investing and fundraising, and became one of Silicon Valley's most successful angel investors with early stakes in companies like Twitter and Uber. But he is as influential as a thinker as an investor — his aphorisms on wealth creation, judgement, and living well have reached millions and shaped how a generation of founders think.

Ravikant's core ideas center on leverage and specific knowledge: that the modern route to wealth is building or buying equity in scalable things, applying leverage through code, media and capital, and developing knowledge so specific to you it can't be trained or outsourced. He frames wealth as freedom, distinguishes it sharply from status, and treats happiness as a skill to be practised.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Seek leverage

Ravikant urges applying leverage — code, media, capital — to your effort.

Do this: Multiply your effort with leverage: code, media, or capital that scales without you.
Principle 2

Build specific knowledge

Develop knowledge so unique to you it can't be trained away.

Do this: Cultivate rare, hard-to-copy knowledge that's specific to who you are.
Principle 3

Own equity

He stresses owning a stake in scalable things, not renting your time.

Do this: Own equity in scalable assets rather than trading time for a wage.

In their words

Seek wealth, not money or status.
Play long-term games with long-term people.
A calm mind, a fit body, and a house full of love — these cannot be bought.

Frequently asked questions

What is Naval Ravikant known for?

Co-founding AngelList, successful early angel investments (Twitter, Uber), and — as much — his widely-shared aphorisms on wealth creation, leverage, judgement and happiness.

What are Naval's core ideas about wealth?

That wealth comes from owning equity in scalable things, applying leverage (code, media, capital), and building 'specific knowledge' unique to you — and that wealth means freedom, not status.
