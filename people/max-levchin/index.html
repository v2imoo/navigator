Max Levchin

The engineer behind PayPal's fraud defences who returned to reinvent lending with Affirm.

4 min read · 1990s–present

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Max Levchin emigrated from Ukraine as a teenager with little money and became the technical force behind PayPal, where he built the fraud-detection systems that let online payments actually work at scale — a problem that nearly killed the company. As a member of the famous 'PayPal Mafia', he went on to found several companies, most notably Affirm, a 'buy now, pay later' lender built on transparent terms and no hidden fees.

Levchin's thread is using hard engineering and data to solve trust problems in finance — fraud at PayPal, honest credit at Affirm. He is a study in solving the unglamorous problem that makes the whole business possible, in turning immigrant hunger into relentless drive, and in building financial products around transparency rather than hidden costs.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Solve the problem that could kill you

Levchin beat the fraud that nearly sank PayPal.

Do this: Find the unglamorous existential problem and solve it brilliantly; that's often what makes the whole business viable.
Principle 2

Use engineering to build trust

He turned data and code into financial trust.

Do this: In finance, trust is the product; rigorous engineering and transparency are how you earn it at scale.
Principle 3

Build on transparent terms

Affirm competes on honesty about costs.

Do this: Where an industry hides its costs, winning customers' trust with radical transparency can be the differentiator.

In their words

The problem worth solving is usually the one everyone else is avoiding.
Fraud is a tax on trust; eliminate it and commerce flows.
Build products you'd be proud to explain to your mother in full.
