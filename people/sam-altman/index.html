Sam Altman

The founder at the centre of the AI age, betting that artificial general intelligence can be built — and steered.

4 min read · 2005–present

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Sam Altman became the defining business figure of the artificial-intelligence era as CEO of OpenAI, the lab behind ChatGPT that pushed AI from research curiosity into a technology used by hundreds of millions. He had already shaped a generation of startups as president of Y Combinator before turning to what he considers the most important technology in history.

Altman's bet is audacious: that artificial general intelligence — systems as capable as humans across most tasks — can be built within our lifetimes, and that doing so safely and steering its benefits is one of the central challenges of the age. In late 2023 he was briefly removed by OpenAI's board and reinstated within days amid overwhelming support, an episode that underscored his centrality to the field.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Aim at the biggest problem

Altman turned from startups to what he sees as the most important technology in history.

Do this: Point your effort at the largest problem you can credibly attack.
Principle 2

Build conviction and capital together

OpenAI paired a bold mission with the resources to pursue it.

Do this: Match an ambitious mission with the funding and talent to actually deliver it.
Principle 3

Move fast to put it in people's hands

ChatGPT reached the public and improved through real use.

Do this: Get a capable product to real users early; learning compounds from usage.

In their words

The best way to predict the future is to invent it.
You get rewarded for being right, not for consensus.
Iterate on the product with real users.
