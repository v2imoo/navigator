Sergey Brin

The co-founder whose algorithm organised the web — and whose network effects made it unassailable.

4 min read · 1998–present

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Sergey Brin, with Larry Page, built Google into the company that organised the world's information, on the strength of a better idea about search: rank pages by how many other pages link to them, treating links as votes. That PageRank insight produced dramatically better results and, once users experienced it, made Google the default gateway to the web.

Brin and Page met as Stanford graduate students and built their search engine in a dorm and a garage. Google's advantage compounded through a data network effect — more searches produced more data to improve results, which drew more searches — and its advertising model turned that dominance into one of the most profitable businesses in history.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Solve the problem fundamentally better

PageRank made search so much better that users switched and stayed.

Do this: Win by being dramatically better at the core job, not marginally.
Principle 2

Compound with a data network effect

More usage produced more data, which improved the product, which drew more usage.

Do this: Design so that use generates data that makes the product better for the next user.
Principle 3

Monetise dominance efficiently

Search advertising turned Google's reach into vast, high-margin revenue.

Do this: Pair a dominant product with a monetisation model that scales with it.

Frequently asked questions

What was Google's original breakthrough?

PageRank — ranking web pages by how many other pages link to them, treating links as votes — which produced far better search results and made Google the default.

Why is Google so hard to displace in search?

A data network effect: more searches yield more data that improves results, drawing still more searches — compounding an advantage rivals struggle to match.
