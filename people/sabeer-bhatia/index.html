Sabeer Bhatia

He co-founded Hotmail, put a sign-up link at the bottom of every email, and watched the internet market itself.

4 min read · 1996

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Sabeer Bhatia moved from India to study at Caltech and Stanford, then co-founded Hotmail with Jack Smith in 1996 — one of the first free web-based email services, letting anyone check mail from any browser rather than being tied to an internet provider. The product was good, but the growth was legendary: at the suggestion of investor Tim Draper, they added a single line to the bottom of every outgoing message — 'Get your free email at Hotmail' — turning every user into an advertiser and every email into a sales pitch.

It became the textbook case of viral marketing: Hotmail reached a million users in six months and twelve million within eighteen, faster than any media product before it. Microsoft bought it in 1997 for a reported $400 million. Bhatia's lesson is that the distribution mechanism can matter as much as the product — that the best growth engine is often built into the thing itself, spreading person to person at zero marginal cost.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Build distribution into the product

Every Hotmail email advertised Hotmail.

Do this: Design your product so that using it naturally spreads it — the best marketing costs nothing per user.
Principle 2

Remove the friction to adopt

Free, browser-based email meant anyone could start in seconds.

Do this: Strip every barrier between a stranger and their first use; adoption compounds on ease.
Principle 3

Let users do the selling

One footer line turned millions of users into a salesforce.

Do this: Find the single mechanism that makes each user recruit the next — then make it automatic.

In their words

Put the invitation inside the product.
The cheapest customer is the one your last customer brought.
Make it free, make it easy, and get out of the way.

Frequently asked questions

What was Hotmail famous for?

Being one of the first free web-based email services — and for pioneering viral marketing by adding 'Get your free email at Hotmail' to the bottom of every message, turning each user into an advertiser.

How fast did Hotmail grow?

It reached one million users within six months and about twelve million within eighteen, then was acquired by Microsoft in 1997 for a reported $400 million.
