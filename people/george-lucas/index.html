George Lucas

The director who kept the licensing rights to Star Wars — and built an empire on the deal everyone else missed.

4 min read · 1971–present

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

George Lucas created Star Wars, but his most consequential move was a business decision that seemed minor at the time: in exchange for directing the first film for a lower fee, he kept the sequel and merchandising rights, which the studio happily gave away. Those rights — toys, licensing, sequels — became worth far more than the films' box office, and made Lucas a billionaire while the studio watched.

Lucas understood, earlier than Hollywood, that a beloved story universe was an intellectual-property machine, not just a movie. He built Lucasfilm and the pioneering effects house Industrial Light & Magic, funded technical revolutions in filmmaking, and ultimately sold the whole empire to Disney for around four billion dollars — a valuation built almost entirely on the IP he'd been careful to own.

Lucas is the definitive lesson in owning intellectual property. By keeping the rights everyone else dismissed, he turned a film into a multi-decade franchise empire — proof that the value is often in the ownership terms, not the headline deal.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Keep the rights that matter

Lucas traded a bigger fee for the merchandising rights — and won enormously.

Do this: Trade short-term cash for the ownership rights that compound over decades.
Principle 2

Treat a story as an IP machine

He saw Star Wars as a franchise universe, not one film.

Do this: Build creations as durable IP franchises, not one-off products.
Principle 3

Invest in the enabling technology

ILM revolutionised effects and became its own asset.

Do this: Build the technical capabilities your work depends on into owned assets.

Frequently asked questions

What was George Lucas's genius business move?

Keeping the sequel and merchandising rights to Star Wars in exchange for a lower directing fee — rights the studio dismissed but which became worth far more than the films, making him a billionaire.

How much did Lucas sell Lucasfilm for?

Around $4 billion to Disney in 2012 — a valuation built almost entirely on the Star Wars intellectual property he'd been careful to own.
