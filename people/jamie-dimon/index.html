Jamie Dimon

The banker who steered the world's most powerful bank through crisis after crisis with a 'fortress balance sheet'.

4 min read · 1998–present

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Jamie Dimon has run JPMorgan Chase since 2005 and built it into the largest and most profitable bank in the United States, widely regarded as the most capable banker of his generation. His defining principle is risk discipline — the 'fortress balance sheet', keeping the bank strong enough to withstand any shock — which let JPMorgan emerge from the 2008 financial crisis not merely intact but strengthened, acquiring failing rivals while others collapsed. He had earlier been fired from Citigroup, a setback he turned into his defining comeback.

Dimon is known for deep operational command, blunt candour, and preparing relentlessly for the next crisis even in good times — famously planning for the 'bad room' scenario. His influential annual shareholder letters range far beyond banking into the economy and society. He is a study in risk discipline as the foundation of durable institutions, in turning a firing into a comeback, and in the leadership value of preparing for disaster when everything looks fine.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Keep a fortress balance sheet

Dimon kept JPMorgan strong enough to withstand any shock — and buy when rivals failed.

Do this: Stay strong enough to survive any shock; survivors acquire the fallen.
Principle 2

Prepare for crisis in good times

He plans relentlessly for disaster even when everything looks fine.

Do this: Prepare for the bad scenario while times are good; that's when you can afford to.
Principle 3

Turn a firing into a comeback

Fired from Citigroup, he returned to build the world's top bank.

Do this: Treat a major setback as the setup for your defining comeback, not the end.
