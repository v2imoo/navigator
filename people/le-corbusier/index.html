Le Corbusier

He called a house 'a machine for living in' — and reshaped how the 20th century built cities.

4 min read · 1920s–1960s

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Le Corbusier (Charles-Édouard Jeanneret) was among the most influential architects of the twentieth century, a founder of the modern movement who declared a house 'a machine for living in'. He stripped buildings of ornament, raised them on pilotis (columns), opened floor plans and roof gardens, and set out 'Five Points' that reshaped architecture worldwide. He also designed Chandigarh, the planned capital of Punjab and Haryana in India.

His influence was vast and divisive: admirers credit him with rational, light-filled modern design, while critics blame his ideas of large-scale, functionalist urban planning for soulless housing blocks. He is a study in codifying principles that a whole field adopts, in design as a response to the machine age, and in the double-edged legacy of an idea applied at massive scale.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Codify principles others adopt

His 'Five Points' spread worldwide.

Do this: Distilling your approach into clear, teachable principles lets a whole field adopt and build on your thinking.
Principle 2

Design for your era's forces

He built for the machine age.

Do this: Great design responds to the defining forces of its time; understand the era you're building for.
Principle 3

Weigh the scale of your ideas

His planning ideas caused harm when mass-applied.

Do this: An idea that works in one building can do damage at city scale; consider the second-order effects of your principles writ large.

Frequently asked questions

Why is Le Corbusier important?

A founder of modern architecture who stripped away ornament, introduced principles like pilotis and open plans in his 'Five Points', designed the Indian city of Chandigarh, and reshaped 20th-century building worldwide.

Why is Le Corbusier's legacy divisive?

Admirers credit his rational, light-filled modernism, while critics blame his large-scale functionalist urban-planning ideas for inspiring soulless housing blocks when applied at mass scale.
