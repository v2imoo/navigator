Mohnish Pabrai

He built his fortune by shamelessly copying the best investors — and turned 'heads I win, tails I don't lose much' into a creed.

4 min read · 1990s–present

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Mohnish Pabrai is an Indian-American investor who built Pabrai Funds explicitly by 'cloning' the methods of Warren Buffett and Charlie Munger — even structuring his fund fees like Buffett's early partnership. He champions a disarmingly simple philosophy captured in his book The Dhandho Investor: seek bets where 'heads I win, tails I don't lose much', the low-risk, high-uncertainty situations where the downside is protected but the upside is large. He and Guy Spier famously paid over $650,000 at a charity auction to lunch with Buffett.

Pabrai argues that originality is overrated in investing and that intelligent imitation of proven methods is a huge edge most people are too proud to use. He is a study in the humility of cloning what works, in structuring bets for asymmetric payoffs, and in the power of a simple, repeatable framework consistently applied.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Clone what works

Pabrai built his success by copying proven investors.

Do this: Don't prize originality for its own sake; intelligently imitating a proven method is a huge, underused edge.
Principle 2

Seek asymmetric bets

His creed is 'heads I win, tails I don't lose much'.

Do this: Hunt for situations where the downside is protected but the upside is large — low risk, high uncertainty.
Principle 3

Keep the framework simple

Dhandho is a few clear rules applied repeatedly.

Do this: A simple, repeatable framework you actually follow beats a sophisticated one you can't apply consistently.

In their words

Heads I win; tails I don't lose much.
Investing is a game where you can copy the best players and still win.
Few bets, big bets, infrequent bets.

Frequently asked questions

Who is Mohnish Pabrai?

An Indian-American value investor who built Pabrai Funds by openly 'cloning' Warren Buffett and Charlie Munger's methods, and wrote The Dhandho Investor on seeking asymmetric, low-risk-high-return bets.

What is the Dhandho approach?

Pabrai's framework of 'heads I win, tails I don't lose much' — concentrating on situations where the downside is well protected but the upside is large, made with few, big, infrequent bets.
