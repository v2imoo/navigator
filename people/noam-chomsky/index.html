Noam Chomsky

He overturned how we understand language, then became the West's most relentless political dissident.

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Noam Chomsky revolutionised linguistics in the 1950s with the theory of generative grammar and 'universal grammar' — the idea that the capacity for language is innate and hard-wired into the human mind, with a deep structure common to all languages. It overturned the prevailing behaviourist view and reshaped linguistics, cognitive science and philosophy of mind. Separately, he became one of the most prominent and relentless political dissidents in the West, a fierce critic of US foreign policy, media and power, co-authoring the 'propaganda model' of mass media.

His political views are admired and fiercely contested in equal measure. In fair presentation: a towering, field-defining scholar, and a polarising political voice. He is a study in intellectual courage across two domains, in challenging consensus in both science and politics, and in a lifetime of relentless dissent.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Overturn the reigning paradigm

Chomsky toppled behaviourism in linguistics.

Do this: A field's dominant paradigm can be wrong; a rigorous challenge from first principles can remake an entire discipline.
Principle 2

Follow your rigour into other domains

He brought a critical mind from science to politics.

Do this: A disciplined, evidence-driven habit of mind can be applied far beyond your specialty; don't confine your rigour.
Principle 3

Expect to be contested when you dissent

His politics drew fierce opposition.

Do this: Sustained dissent against power invites sustained attack; relentless critics come with the territory of speaking out.
