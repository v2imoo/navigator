Ronald Wayne

The man who co-founded Apple — then sold his 10% for $800, in business history's most expensive act of caution.

4 min read · 1976

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Ronald Wayne was the third co-founder of Apple, older than Steve Jobs and Steve Wozniak, brought in to provide adult supervision and settle disputes; he drew the first Apple logo and wrote the original partnership agreement, holding a 10% stake. But just twelve days later, anxious about the financial risk — as a partnership, he could be personally liable for any debts, and he had been burned by a failed venture before — he sold his entire stake back for $800.

That stake would later have been worth tens of billions of dollars. Wayne, who lived modestly for the rest of his life, has said he doesn't regret it given what he knew and needed at the time — but his story has become the ultimate cautionary tale about risk, conviction, and the fear that makes us sell too early. He is a study in the cost of letting fear override conviction, in the asymmetry of upside and downside in a startup, and in how the most expensive decisions are often the cautious ones.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Weigh the asymmetry

Wayne fled a capped downside and forfeited an almost unlimited upside.

Do this: When downside is limited but upside is vast, don't let fear of the small loss cost you the huge gain.
Principle 2

Don't let fear override conviction

His anxiety made him sell a stake worth a fortune.

Do this: Distinguish prudent caution from fear; fear often makes us abandon the right bet too early.
Principle 3

Understand what you're giving up

He didn't grasp the magnitude of what he was selling.

Do this: Before you exit, understand the full potential of what you're walking away from.
