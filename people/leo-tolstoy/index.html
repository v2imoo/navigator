Leo Tolstoy

He wrote the two greatest novels of the realist tradition — then renounced it all for a radical moral life.

4 min read · 1850s–1910

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Leo Tolstoy, a Russian count, wrote what many consider the two greatest novels ever: War and Peace, a vast panorama of Russian society during the Napoleonic wars, and Anna Karenina, a tragedy of love, society and moral choice. His gift was a seemingly total realism — the ability to render the inner life of dozens of characters with such depth and truth that readers feel they are living inside other minds.

In mid-life Tolstoy underwent a profound spiritual crisis, renounced his wealth and his earlier work, and embraced a radical Christian philosophy of non-violence, simplicity and moral self-perfection that later influenced Gandhi. He is a study in rendering human truth with total fidelity, in the restless search for how to live, and in the courage to upend one's own life in pursuit of meaning.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Render inner life with total truth

Tolstoy makes readers inhabit other minds.

Do this: Observe and depict the inner experience of others with complete honesty; truthfulness is what makes work immortal.
Principle 2

Keep searching how to live

His greatness included a restless moral quest.

Do this: Don't stop at success; the deepest work often comes from continuing to ask how one ought to live.
Principle 3

Be willing to upend yourself

He renounced wealth and earlier work for his convictions.

Do this: Hold your own life accountable to your beliefs, even when that means dismantling what you've built.
