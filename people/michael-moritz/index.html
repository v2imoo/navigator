Michael Moritz

The journalist-turned-VC whose early bets on Google and PayPal defined a generation of venture returns.

4 min read · 1986–present

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Michael Moritz became one of the most successful venture capitalists of all time at Sequoia Capital, with an extraordinary record of early bets — Yahoo, Google, PayPal and more — that helped define the internet era and generated legendary returns. A former journalist who had written about Apple, he brought a storyteller's eye for people and narrative to identifying exceptional founders.

Moritz's edge was judgement about founders and markets at the earliest, most uncertain stages, and the discipline to back conviction heavily. He also helped lead Sequoia's evolution into a global, enduring institution — building a firm designed to compound success across generations of partners, not just ride one era.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Judge founders, not just markets

Moritz backed exceptional people at the earliest stage.

Do this: At the earliest stage, bet on the quality of the founders above all.
Principle 2

Back conviction heavily

He committed strongly when judgement and evidence aligned.

Do this: When your judgement is strong, back it with real conviction, not a token bet.
Principle 3

Build an enduring institution

He helped make Sequoia last beyond any one era or partner.

Do this: Build your firm to compound across generations, not just ride one wave.
