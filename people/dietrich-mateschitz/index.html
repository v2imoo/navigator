Dietrich Mateschitz

The marketer who sold not a drink but a feeling — and built a brand by owning a lifestyle.

4 min read · 1987–2022

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Dietrich Mateschitz built Red Bull into a global phenomenon by understanding that he wasn't really selling an energy drink — he was selling an identity around energy, adventure and adrenaline. He discovered a tonic in Thailand, reformulated it for Western tastes, and then spent the next decades building a brand through experience rather than conventional advertising.

Red Bull's genius was to become a media and events company that happened to sell a drink: sponsoring extreme sports, staging spectacles like a supersonic freefall from the edge of space, and creating content that embodied the brand. The product was almost secondary to the world it represented — marketing as the core competency.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Sell the identity, not the product

Red Bull marketed energy and adventure, not a beverage's ingredients.

Do this: Attach your product to an identity and feeling customers want to embody.
Principle 2

Market through experience and content

Events and media, not ads, built the brand.

Do this: Build a brand through experiences and content people seek out, not ads they skip.
Principle 3

Win a commodity category on brand

The drink was ordinary; the brand made it extraordinary.

Do this: In a commoditised market, make the brand the product's real differentiator.

In their words

We don't bring the product to the consumer; we bring consumers to the product.
If we only wanted money we would do things differently.
Red Bull is not a drink, it's a way of life.

Frequently asked questions

What was Mateschitz's marketing insight?

That Red Bull sold an identity — energy, adventure, adrenaline — not just a drink, so he built the brand through extreme-sports events and content rather than conventional advertising.

Why is Red Bull a branding case study?

In a commoditised category, its brand — built through experience and content — commands loyalty and margins the drink itself never could.
