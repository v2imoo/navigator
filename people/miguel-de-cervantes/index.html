Miguel de Cervantes

A maimed soldier and former captive wrote the book that invented the modern novel — a comedy about chasing impossible dreams.

4 min read · 1600s

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Miguel de Cervantes lived a life of hardship — wounded in battle, losing the use of his left hand, captured by pirates and held as a slave for five years, later jailed as a struggling tax collector. Out of that life came Don Quixote, the tale of an ageing man who reads so many chivalric romances that he loses his wits and sets out as a knight to right the world's wrongs, tilting at windmills he believes are giants. Widely called the first modern novel, it fuses comedy, pathos and a revolutionary psychological depth.

Cervantes gave the world an archetype — the idealist chasing impossible dreams — and the word 'quixotic'. He is a study in forging timeless art from a hard, failure-strewn life, in inventing a new form, and in the bittersweet nobility of pursuing an ideal the world calls madness.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Make art from a hard life

Cervantes forged a masterpiece from failure and captivity.

Do this: A difficult, failure-filled life is not a disqualification; it can be the raw material of your most enduring work.
Principle 2

Invent the form you need

He created something the world had no category for.

Do this: If existing forms can't hold your vision, build a new one; the first of a kind defines everything after it.
Principle 3

Honour the noble, impossible quest

Quixote's folly is also a kind of greatness.

Do this: There is dignity in pursuing an ideal others call impractical; the quixotic pursuit can matter more than the odds.

Frequently asked questions

Why is Don Quixote important?

Widely regarded as the first modern novel, it fused comedy, pathos and unprecedented psychological depth, gave the world the archetype of the idealist chasing impossible dreams, and the word 'quixotic'.

What was Cervantes's life like?

Hard and failure-strewn — wounded in battle, held captive by pirates for five years, later jailed — experiences from which he forged the masterpiece Don Quixote late in life.
