Patrick Collison

The founder who removed the hardest friction on the internet — making it simple for anyone to accept payments.

4 min read · 2010–present

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Patrick Collison, with his brother John, co-founded Stripe in 2010 to solve a problem that had quietly throttled internet commerce: accepting payments online was absurdly difficult, requiring developers to navigate banks, contracts, and arcane systems for weeks. Stripe reduced it to a few lines of code, letting any developer start accepting payments almost instantly. By removing that friction, Stripe became foundational infrastructure for the internet economy, powering millions of businesses.

The Collisons built Stripe with an obsessive focus on developers and on the quality of the product and documentation, understanding that in infrastructure, being the easiest and most reliable to build on is the entire game. Patrick is also known for deep intellectual curiosity, championing work on scientific progress and long-term thinking. He is a study in removing a critical point of friction, in building beloved developer infrastructure, and in the enormous value created by making a hard thing simple.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Remove the critical friction

Stripe turned weeks of payment setup into a few lines of code.

Do this: Find the painful friction everyone tolerates and remove it entirely.
Principle 2

Obsess over the builders on your platform

Stripe won through developer experience, product and docs.

Do this: In infrastructure, being the easiest and most reliable to build on is the whole game.
Principle 3

Make the hard thing simple

By simplifying payments, Stripe unlocked enormous value.

Do this: Enormous value comes from making a genuinely hard thing simple for everyone.
