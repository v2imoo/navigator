Sol Price

The retailer who invented the membership warehouse club — and quietly shaped Costco, Walmart and more.

4 min read · 1954–2009

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Sol Price invented the membership warehouse club — the model behind Costco and Sam's Club — and in doing so influenced modern retail more than almost anyone whose name the public doesn't know. With Price Club, he pioneered charging customers a membership fee to shop in no-frills warehouses stocked with a limited selection sold at razor-thin margins in bulk.

The genius of the model was that the membership fee, not the product markup, was the profit — so Price could sell goods at barely above cost, delivering genuine value that kept members loyal and renewing. His disciplined, ethical, value-obsessed approach directly inspired Costco's founders and was openly admired by Sam Walton and Jim Sinegal.

Price's warehouse-club invention — aligning the retailer's profit with delivering real value rather than marking goods up — is one of the most influential and widely-copied ideas in retail history.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Make the model align with value

Price profited from membership, so he could sell near cost.

Do this: Design your economics so you profit by giving customers genuine value.
Principle 2

Strip out every unnecessary cost

No-frills warehouses and limited selection kept prices rock-bottom.

Do this: Remove every cost that doesn't add value the customer will pay for.
Principle 3

Earn loyalty through fairness

Consistent real value kept members renewing.

Do this: Deliver such honest value that customers renew and trust you.

In their words

Get the fundamentals right, and everything else follows.
If you wouldn't do it in front of your mother, don't do it.
The pig gets fat, but the hog gets slaughtered — don't be greedy.
