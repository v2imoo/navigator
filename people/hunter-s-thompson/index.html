Hunter S. Thompson

He threw out the rule that journalists stay objective — and made himself the chaotic centre of the story.

4 min read · 1960s–2005

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Hunter S. Thompson invented 'Gonzo journalism' — a wild, subjective, first-person style that tore up the rule that reporters should be detached observers and instead made the writer a central, often chaotic participant in the story. His books, above all Fear and Loathing in Las Vegas, blurred fact and fiction, reportage and hallucination, to capture a deeper truth about America than straight journalism could. He embedded with the Hells Angels for a year, covered presidential campaigns with savage insight, and lived as recklessly as he wrote.

Thompson's method was that raw subjectivity, honestly owned, could reveal what false objectivity concealed. He is a study in breaking a form's central rule to reach a deeper truth, in a wholly original voice, and in the double edge of a life lived without limits.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Break the form's central rule

Thompson discarded journalistic objectivity.

Do this: Sometimes the deepest truth requires breaking the one rule your form treats as sacred; question what it forbids.
Principle 2

Own your subjectivity honestly

He made his own viewpoint the lens.

Do this: Honest, acknowledged subjectivity can reveal more than a false pose of neutrality; own your perspective rather than hiding it.
Principle 3

Forge a wholly original voice

Gonzo was unmistakably his.

Do this: A distinctive, uncompromising voice is itself the achievement; don't sand yours down to fit the conventional form.

Frequently asked questions

Who was Hunter S. Thompson?

The inventor of 'Gonzo journalism' — a wild, subjective, first-person style that made the writer a central participant in the story — best known for Fear and Loathing in Las Vegas.

What is Gonzo journalism?

Thompson's radical style that discarded journalistic objectivity, blending fact, fiction and the reporter's own chaotic experience to capture a deeper truth than detached reporting could.
