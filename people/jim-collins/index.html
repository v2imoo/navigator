Jim Collins

He studied why some companies leap from good to great — and found it starts with humble, fiercely willful leaders.

Part I — The story

The story

Jim Collins built his reputation on multi-year, data-driven studies of what separates enduring, great companies from merely good ones. His books — Built to Last, Good to Great, How the Mighty Fall — distilled findings into memorable concepts: 'Level 5 leadership' (the paradox of personal humility and fierce professional will), the 'Hedgehog Concept' (the intersection of what you're passionate about, what you can be best in the world at, and what drives your economic engine), 'first who, then what' (get the right people on the bus first), and the 'flywheel' of compounding momentum.

Collins insists his frameworks come from evidence, not opinion, comparing great companies against matched peers that didn't make the leap. He is a study in deriving principles from rigorous comparison, in the counterintuitive traits of great leadership, and in the discipline of simple, durable frameworks.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Get the right people first

Collins's 'first who, then what' puts people before strategy.

Do this: Assemble the right team before fixing the direction; great people will find the right 'what' together.
Principle 2

Find your Hedgehog Concept

Operate where passion, excellence and economics intersect.

Do this: Focus relentlessly on the one thing you can be best at, are passionate about, and that drives your economics.
Principle 3

Push the flywheel

Great results come from compounding consistent effort, not one big push.

Do this: Success is a flywheel: consistent pushes in one direction compound into unstoppable momentum over time.

In their words

Good is the enemy of great.
First who, then what.
Greatness is not a function of circumstance; it is a matter of conscious choice and discipline.

Frequently asked questions

Who is Jim Collins?

A management researcher and author of Good to Great and Built to Last, known for data-driven studies of what makes companies endure and excel, and concepts like Level 5 leadership, the Hedgehog Concept and the flywheel.

What is 'Level 5 leadership'?

Collins's finding that the leaders of companies that went from good to great combined deep personal humility with fierce professional will — an unflashy but relentless drive for the organisation's success.
