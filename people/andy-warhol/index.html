Andy Warhol

He turned soup cans and celebrity into high art, blurred the line between art and commerce, and foresaw fame itself.

4 min read · 1960s–1987

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Andy Warhol was the central figure of Pop Art, who scandalised and then redefined the art world by elevating the imagery of mass consumer culture — Campbell's soup cans, Coca-Cola bottles, Marilyn Monroe and Elvis — into high art. Working from his studio 'the Factory', he used silkscreen printing to mass-produce images, deliberately blurring the line between art and commerce, original and copy, fame and substance. He predicted that 'in the future, everyone will be famous for 15 minutes' — an uncanny foreshadowing of the media age.

Warhol treated celebrity, repetition and surface as his subjects, challenging what art could even be. He is a study in finding art in the overlooked everyday, in dissolving the boundary between art and commerce, and in seeing the cultural future before it arrived.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Find art in the overlooked everyday

Warhol made soup cans into high art.

Do this: The ordinary, mass-produced things everyone ignores can become profound subjects when you see them freshly.
Principle 2

Dissolve the false boundary

He blurred art and commerce, original and copy.

Do this: Question the boundaries your field treats as sacred; dissolving them can open entirely new creative territory.
Principle 3

See the cultural future

His '15 minutes' predicted the media age.

Do this: Pay attention to where culture is heading; naming the future before it arrives is its own kind of creation.

In their words

Making money is art, and working is art, and good business is the best art.
