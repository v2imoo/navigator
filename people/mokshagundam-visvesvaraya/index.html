M. Visvesvaraya

He engineered dams, flood control and a model state — India celebrates its Engineer's Day on his birthday.

4 min read · 1900s–1950s

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Mokshagundam Visvesvaraya was India's most celebrated engineer and a visionary administrator. As an engineer he designed innovative flood-protection and irrigation systems, including automatic sluice gates and the Krishna Raja Sagara dam, transforming agriculture in the region. As Diwan (chief minister) of the princely state of Mysore, he drove industrialisation, education and institution-building, helping make it one of the most progressive states in India.

Renowned for integrity, punctuality and tireless work into old age, he lived to 100, and India marks Engineer's Day on his birthday. He is a study in uniting technical mastery with nation-building administration, in engineering as a tool for development, and in discipline and integrity sustained across an extraordinarily long life of service.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Unite engineering and statecraft

Visvesvaraya built both dams and a modern state.

Do this: Technical skill applied to governance and development can transform a society; don't confine engineering to projects alone.
Principle 2

Engineer for development

His works transformed agriculture and industry.

Do this: Aim your engineering at the fundamental needs of people — water, food, industry; infrastructure is development.
Principle 3

Live your discipline

His integrity and punctuality were legendary.

Do this: Personal discipline and integrity, sustained over a lifetime, build the trust that lets you lead big undertakings.
