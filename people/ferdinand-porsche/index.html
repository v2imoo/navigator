Ferdinand Porsche

The engineer behind the people's car and a legendary sports-car marque — a study in design genius and moral complexity.

4 min read · 1900–1951

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Ferdinand Porsche was one of history's most influential automotive engineers, designing the original Volkswagen Beetle — conceived as an affordable 'people's car' that became one of the best-selling cars ever made — and founding the company that bears his name, which became synonymous with engineering excellence in sports cars. His technical innovations in car design, aerodynamics, and even early hybrid-electric powertrains were decades ahead of their time.

Porsche's legacy is also morally complex: he worked closely with the Nazi regime, and the wartime production associated with his companies used forced labour — facts that are an inseparable and condemnable part of his history. He is a study in engineering genius and the enduring power of designing for a mass market — the 'people's car' as a concept — held honestly alongside the serious moral darkness of the context in which he worked.

The playbook

Principle 1

Design for the mass market

Do this: Designing a quality product for the mass market can define an era.
