Mary Barra

The executive who rose from the factory floor to lead GM — and bet the company on an all-electric future.

4 min read · 1980–present

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Mary Barra became CEO of General Motors in 2014 — the first woman to lead a major global automaker — having started at the company as a teenager inspecting fenders, the daughter of a GM die-maker. Her deep operational roots gave her credibility as she steered the legacy giant through one of the most disruptive periods in the industry's history.

Barra took over just as a massive ignition-switch safety crisis broke, and won respect for confronting it directly. She then committed GM to a bold long-term vision — an all-electric, zero-emissions future — investing heavily to transform a century-old combustion-engine company for the age of electric and autonomous vehicles, a wrenching but necessary strategic pivot.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Confront crisis directly

Barra faced the safety crisis head-on rather than minimising it.

Do this: Meet a crisis with transparency and accountability, not deflection.
Principle 2

Commit to a bold long-term vision

She staked GM's future on going all-electric.

Do this: When the ground is shifting, commit clearly to where the future is heading.
Principle 3

Lead from deep operational roots

Her factory-floor credibility helped drive change.

Do this: Earn credibility through deep knowledge of the work you're asking others to change.
