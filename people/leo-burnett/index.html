Leo Burnett

He gave brands living characters — the Marlboro Man, Tony the Tiger, the Jolly Green Giant — that outlived him.

4 min read · 1930s–1970s

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Leo Burnett founded his Chicago agency during the Great Depression and built it by creating some of the most enduring brand characters in advertising history — the Marlboro Man, Tony the Tiger, the Jolly Green Giant, the Pillsbury Doughboy. He believed in finding the 'inherent drama' in a product and dramatising it through warm, human, emotionally resonant imagery rather than clever wordplay — an approach that gave brands a memorable personality people felt affection for.

Burnett's characters created emotional bonds that lasted for generations and are among advertising's most valuable assets. He is a study in building brand equity through character and emotion, in finding the inherent drama in what you sell, and in the long-term value of a brand personality people love.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Find the inherent drama

Burnett looked for the drama already inside a product.

Do this: Every product has an inherent story or drama; your job is to find and dramatise it, not invent a gimmick.
Principle 2

Build a brand character

His icons gave brands a personality people loved.

Do this: A memorable character or persona can create an emotional bond that outlasts any single campaign.
Principle 3

Appeal to emotion, not cleverness

He favoured warmth over wordplay.

Do this: People remember how a brand makes them feel; lead with human emotion rather than clever tricks.

Frequently asked questions

Who was Leo Burnett?

A legendary adman who built his Chicago agency by creating enduring brand characters — the Marlboro Man, Tony the Tiger, the Jolly Green Giant, the Pillsbury Doughboy — rooted in warmth and 'inherent drama'.

What was Leo Burnett's philosophy?

To find the 'inherent drama' within a product and dramatise it through warm, human, emotionally resonant imagery and memorable characters, rather than through clever wordplay.
