Aristotle Onassis

The refugee who built one of the world's great shipping fortunes by buying when everyone else was selling.

4 min read · 1930–1975

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Aristotle Onassis fled the destruction of Smyrna as a young refugee and rebuilt his life into one of the largest shipping fortunes in the world. His defining move was counter-cyclical daring: during the Great Depression he bought cargo ships for a fraction of their cost when others were desperate to sell, then rode the recovery.

Onassis pioneered the large-scale supertanker as global oil trade exploded after the war, understanding that owning the ships that moved the world's oil was as valuable as the oil itself. A flamboyant figure whose life mixed business with celebrity, he built a fleet that made him one of the era's richest men.

Onassis's career is a classic study in counter-cyclical investing and in positioning yourself to own the essential infrastructure of a growing trade — buying the picks and shovels of the oil age.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Buy when others are forced to sell

Onassis bought ships cheaply in the Depression, then rode the recovery.

Do this: Acquire hard assets counter-cyclically, when distress makes them cheap.
Principle 2

Own the infrastructure of a boom

He owned the tankers that moved oil, profiting from the whole trade.

Do this: Own the essential infrastructure a growing industry can't function without.
Principle 3

Scale ahead of demand

He built ever-larger tankers as global oil trade grew.

Do this: Invest in capacity ahead of a trend you're confident will keep growing.

Frequently asked questions

How did Onassis build his shipping fortune?

Through counter-cyclical daring — buying cargo ships cheaply during the Depression — and by pioneering large supertankers as post-war oil trade boomed, owning the infrastructure that moved the world's oil.

What's the transferable lesson?

Buy essential hard assets when distress makes them cheap, and own the infrastructure a growing trade depends on rather than just the commodity.
