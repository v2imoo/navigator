David Vélez

Frustrated by Brazil's brutal banking, he built Latin America's biggest digital bank — and banked millions for the first time.

4 min read · 2010s–present

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

David Vélez, a Colombian who experienced the high fees, bureaucracy and poor service of Brazil's highly concentrated banking system firsthand, founded Nubank in 2013 to offer a simple, low-fee, mobile-first alternative, starting with a no-fee purple credit card. Nubank grew into the largest digital bank in Latin America, serving tens of millions — many of whom had never had access to formal banking before — and became one of the region's most valuable companies.

Vélez built around customer love and financial inclusion in a market incumbents had treated as captive. He is a study in attacking a concentrated, customer-hostile industry, in financial inclusion as both mission and market, and in winning through service in a sector people had learned to hate.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Attack the customer-hostile industry

Vélez targeted banking everyone resented.

Do this: An industry customers have learned to hate is ripe for disruption; winning on service alone can be transformative.
Principle 2

Make inclusion the market

Nubank banked millions for the first time.

Do this: Serving those the incumbents ignored can be both a mission and the largest growth opportunity.
Principle 3

Win through customer love

Nubank grew heavily on word of mouth.

Do this: Delight customers enough that they recruit others; in a hated category, love is the cheapest, strongest growth engine.

In their words

Complexity is the enemy of the customer.
We built a company customers would actually recommend to their friends.
Access to good financial services is a right, not a privilege.
