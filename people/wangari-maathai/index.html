Wangari Maathai

She fought poverty, deforestation and dictatorship with the simplest of tools: planting trees, one woman at a time.

4 min read · 1970s–2011

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Wangari Maathai, the first woman in East and Central Africa to earn a doctorate, founded the Green Belt Movement in Kenya in 1977, mobilising rural women to plant trees to combat deforestation, soil erosion and poverty. What began as an environmental effort became a broad movement for community empowerment, women's rights and democracy — and tens of millions of trees were planted. In 2004 she became the first African woman to win the Nobel Peace Prize, for linking environmental sustainability, development and peace.

Maathai faced beatings and imprisonment for confronting an authoritarian government and powerful land interests, and never backed down. She is a study in how a simple, scalable act can address many problems at once, in grassroots empowerment as a force for change, and in the courage to persist against intimidation.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Find the simple act that solves many problems

Tree-planting tackled ecology, poverty and empowerment at once.

Do this: Look for one simple, scalable action that addresses several problems together; its leverage is enormous.
Principle 2

Empower at the grassroots

She mobilised ordinary rural women as the engine of change.

Do this: Lasting change is driven by the many; build movements that give ordinary people agency and ownership.
Principle 3

Refuse to be intimidated

She persisted through beatings and jail.

Do this: When powerful interests push back, steady persistence is itself a strategy; don't let intimidation set your limits.

In their words

It's the little things citizens do. That's what will make the difference.
When we plant trees, we plant the seeds of peace and hope.

Frequently asked questions

Who was Wangari Maathai?

A Kenyan environmental and political activist who founded the Green Belt Movement in 1977, mobilising women to plant tens of millions of trees, and became the first African woman to win the Nobel Peace Prize in 2004.

What was the Green Belt Movement?

Maathai's grassroots movement that organised rural women to plant trees against deforestation and poverty, growing into a broader campaign for community empowerment, women's rights and democracy.
