Nims Purja

The mountaineer who climbed all 14 of the world's highest peaks in under seven months — shattering a record thought to be impossible.

4 min read · 2019–present

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Nirmal 'Nims' Purja, a former Gurkha and British Special Forces soldier from Nepal, set out in 2019 to do something the mountaineering world considered impossible: climb all fourteen of the world's peaks above 8,000 metres in a single season. The existing record stood at nearly eight years. Nims did it in six months and six days — 'Project Possible' — an achievement so far beyond the previous mark that it redefined what people believed humanly achievable.

Nims succeeded through meticulous planning, extraordinary physical and mental resilience, and above all an unshakeable refusal to accept conventional limits — he began with no major sponsorship, remortgaging his home to fund the attempt when institutions declined to back him. He also used his platform to highlight the Nepalese Sherpas whose contributions mountaineering had long overlooked. He is a study in setting an audacious goal others call impossible, then bringing relentless preparation and belief to bear until the impossible becomes done.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Set the 'impossible' goal

Nims targeted a record so far beyond the existing mark it was dismissed outright.

Do this: Set a goal audacious enough to redefine what people believe is possible.
Principle 2

Back yourself when institutions won't

He remortgaged his home when sponsors declined.

Do this: When no one will back your conviction, be willing to back it yourself.
Principle 3

Pair boldness with meticulous rigour

The audacious goal rested on precise logistics and timing.

Do this: Support the bold bet with meticulous planning — audacity plus rigour, not just nerve.
