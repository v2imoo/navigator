Stanley Kubrick

He controlled every frame, took on every genre, and made each film a landmark — a perfectionist who answered to no one.

4 min read · 1950s–1999

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Stanley Kubrick was one of cinema's great perfectionists and mavericks, who won near-total creative control over his films and used it to make each a meticulous, genre-defining landmark — science fiction (2001: A Space Odyssey), dystopia (A Clockwork Orange), horror (The Shining), war (Full Metal Jacket), the historical epic (Barry Lyndon). He obsessed over every detail — demanding dozens or hundreds of takes, pioneering visual effects and lenses — and worked slowly, releasing only a handful of films over decades, each a deliberate masterwork.

He refused to repeat himself, mastering a new genre each time. He is a study in demanding the control that lets you realise an uncompromised vision, in obsessive attention to craft, and in the courage to reinvent rather than repeat a winning formula.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Win the control to realise your vision

Kubrick secured near-total creative control.

Do this: An uncompromised vision needs the authority to protect it; fight for the control that lets you make it yours.
Principle 2

Obsess over every detail

He was famous for his relentless perfectionism.

Do this: At the highest level, the details are the work; obsessive attention to craft is what separates landmark from merely good.
Principle 3

Reinvent rather than repeat

He mastered a new genre each film.

Do this: Refuse to coast on a winning formula; tackling a new challenge each time keeps the work alive and your range growing.

In their words

A film is — or should be — more like music than like fiction.
The truth of a thing is in the feel of it, not the think of it.
If it can be written or thought, it can be filmed.

Frequently asked questions

Who was Stanley Kubrick?

One of cinema's great perfectionists, who won near-total creative control and made each film a meticulous, genre-defining landmark — from 2001: A Space Odyssey to The Shining — never repeating himself.

What defined Kubrick's filmmaking?

Obsessive perfectionism and total creative control — demanding countless takes, pioneering techniques, working slowly, and mastering a new genre with each of his handful of deliberate masterworks.
