Nikolay Storonsky

A former trader who built a borderless money app, obsessing over speed and growth to take on the banks.

Part I — The story

The story

Nikolay Storonsky, a former derivatives trader, co-founded Revolut in 2015 to fix the pain of spending and moving money across borders — launching with cheap, fair currency exchange and expanding into a full 'super-app' of banking, cards, crypto, investing and more. Revolut grew into one of the world's largest neobanks with tens of millions of users, built on relentless speed of product shipping and aggressive growth.

Storonsky is known for an intense, demanding, metrics-driven culture and a willingness to move faster than incumbents dare. He is a study in attacking a slow, incumbent-dominated industry with speed and breadth, in building a super-app by stacking services, and in the trade-offs of a relentlessly aggressive operating style.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Attack the slow incumbent with speed

Revolut ships faster than banks can.

Do this: Against entrenched, slow competitors, velocity of product shipping is one of your sharpest weapons.
Principle 2

Stack services into a super-app

Revolut bundled many financial products together.

Do this: Once you own the customer relationship, adding adjacent services deepens it and raises switching costs.
Principle 3

Run on metrics

His culture is intensely data-driven.

Do this: Instrument everything and manage by the numbers; clear metrics drive speed and accountability — but watch the human cost.
