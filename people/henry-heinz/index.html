Henry J. Heinz

He put his food in clear glass bottles so buyers could see its purity — and made transparency a brand.

4 min read · 1870s–1919

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

In an era when processed food was often adulterated and dangerous, Henry Heinz built his company on the radical idea of purity and transparency. He sold his condiments in clear glass bottles so customers could see the quality of what they were buying, kept his factories spotless and open to tours, and championed early food-safety legislation that his competitors fought. His '57 Varieties' slogan and relentless, inventive marketing made Heinz a household name.

Heinz understood that trust was a competitive weapon — that in a market full of suspicion, being visibly honest was the strongest differentiator. He is a study in turning integrity into a brand, in transparency as a strategy, and in supporting the regulation that raises standards you already meet.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Make transparency your edge

Heinz showed his product through clear glass.

Do this: In a market full of distrust, visible honesty about your quality can be your strongest differentiator.
Principle 2

Turn integrity into brand

His reputation for purity was his marketing.

Do this: Build trust deliberately and loudly; a reputation for doing right is a durable competitive advantage.
Principle 3

Back standards you already meet

He championed food-safety laws rivals resisted.

Do this: Support the regulation that raises the bar to where you already are; it turns your quality into a moat.

In their words

Frequently asked questions

Who was Henry Heinz?

The founder of the H. J. Heinz Company, who built a food empire on purity and transparency — selling condiments in clear glass bottles, championing food safety, and making '57 Varieties' a household slogan.

Why did Heinz use clear glass bottles?

To let customers see the quality and purity of his food at a time when processed foods were often adulterated — turning visible honesty and trust into his central competitive advantage.
