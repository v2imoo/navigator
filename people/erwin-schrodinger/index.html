Erwin Schrödinger

His equation describes how quantum reality evolves — and his famous cat became the symbol of its strangeness.

4 min read · 1920s–1960s

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Erwin Schrödinger formulated the wave equation that bears his name — the central equation of quantum mechanics, describing how the quantum state of a system evolves over time. It gave the field a powerful, continuous mathematical framework complementary to Heisenberg's. To highlight how strange quantum superposition becomes at everyday scale, he devised the thought experiment of 'Schrödinger's cat' — a cat simultaneously alive and dead until observed — which became the most famous illustration of quantum weirdness.

Later, in his book What Is Life?, Schrödinger inspired a generation of biologists, including the discoverers of DNA's structure, by asking how physics could explain heredity. He is a study in giving a field its defining tool, in using a vivid thought experiment to expose a deep truth, and in cross-pollinating ideas from one science into another.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Give the field its tool

Schrödinger's equation became quantum mechanics' workhorse.

Do this: Creating the core tool others use to work is among the most durable contributions you can make.
Principle 2

Use a vivid thought experiment

His cat made an abstract truth unforgettable.

Do this: A sharp, concrete thought experiment can expose and communicate a deep idea better than any equation.
Principle 3

Carry ideas across fields

His physics seeded modern molecular biology.

Do this: Bring the questions and methods of your field to another; cross-pollination sparks breakthroughs on both sides.

Frequently asked questions

What is the Schrödinger equation?

The central equation of quantum mechanics, describing how the quantum state of a physical system evolves over time — the continuous mathematical framework at the heart of the theory.

What is 'Schrödinger's cat'?

A thought experiment Schrödinger devised in which a cat is simultaneously alive and dead until observed, dramatising how strange quantum superposition becomes when scaled up to everyday objects.
