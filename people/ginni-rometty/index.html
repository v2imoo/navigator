Ginni Rometty

She rose from engineer to run a century-old giant, steering it through a painful reinvention toward AI and cloud.

Part I — The story

The story

Ginni Rometty joined IBM as a systems engineer and rose over three decades to become, in 2012, the first woman to lead the century-old company. She steered IBM through a wrenching transition away from its declining legacy businesses toward cloud computing, data and artificial intelligence — championing Watson and acquiring Red Hat — in a period of hard choices and shrinking revenue that drew both criticism and credit for positioning the company for the future.

Rometty became a prominent advocate for 'new-collar' jobs — skills-based hiring that doesn't require a four-year degree. She is a study in rising through a huge organisation on merit, in the painful discipline of reinventing a legacy business, and in using a leadership platform to rethink who gets access to good jobs.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Reinvent the legacy business

Rometty pushed IBM toward cloud and AI despite the pain.

Do this: Steering a declining legacy business toward the future is thankless but necessary; make the hard pivots before you're forced to.
Principle 2

Rise on merit through the ranks

She went from engineer to CEO over decades.

Do this: Deep, long tenure and demonstrated results build the credibility to lead; mastery of the business earns the top job.
Principle 3

Rethink who gets access

Her 'new-collar' push opened jobs beyond degree-holders.

Do this: Use your platform to widen access — skills-based hiring taps talent that credential filters miss.
