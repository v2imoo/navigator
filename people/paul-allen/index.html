Paul Allen

The co-founder who saw the personal-computer future with Gates — then spent a fortune on curiosity.

4 min read · 1975–2018

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Paul Allen co-founded Microsoft with his childhood friend Bill Gates, and it was Allen who spotted the seminal moment — a magazine cover featuring an early personal computer — that convinced the pair to drop everything and write software for it. He was the technical visionary who saw where computing was heading; Gates was the relentless businessman. Together they built the software company that came to define the PC era.

Allen left Microsoft in 1983 after a cancer diagnosis but retained a stake that made him one of the world's wealthiest people. He spent the rest of his life as a wide-ranging investor and philanthropist — owning sports teams, funding brain science and space ventures, and backing the arts — driven by an insatiable curiosity.

Allen's role is a reminder that great companies often need both a visionary who sees the future and an operator who builds it — and that a fortune, well directed, can fund decades of curiosity and giving.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Spot the inflection early

Allen recognised the PC moment before almost anyone.

Do this: Train yourself to notice the early signal that a new era is beginning.
Principle 2

Pair vision with execution

Allen saw the future; Gates drove the business relentlessly.

Do this: Complement your strengths with a partner who supplies what you lack.
Principle 3

Direct wealth toward curiosity

He funded science, space and the arts across decades.

Do this: Deploy resources toward the questions and causes that genuinely fascinate you.
