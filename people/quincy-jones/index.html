Quincy Jones

From bebop to 'Thriller', he produced the soundtrack of modern music — and mentored generations doing it.

4 min read · 1950s–2020s

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Quincy Jones was one of the most versatile and influential figures in the history of popular music — a jazz trumpeter and arranger who became a bandleader, film composer, and record producer across seven decades. He produced Michael Jackson's Thriller, the best-selling album of all time, arranged for Frank Sinatra and Count Basie, scored films, and won 28 Grammy Awards. Few people have shaped as many corners of music as Jones did.

He was a master collaborator and talent-spotter, able to move fluidly across genres and to assemble and lead the right people for each project. He is a study in range and reinvention across a long career, in the craft of producing — drawing the best from others — and in staying curious and open to new sounds and generations for a lifetime.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Master range and reinvention

Jones moved across genres and eras for seventy years.

Do this: Cultivate breadth and keep reinventing; a long career is built on the willingness to learn new forms.
Principle 2

Draw the best from others

As a producer, his gift was bringing out great performances.

Do this: Leadership is often about assembling the right people and getting their best, not doing it all yourself.
Principle 3

Stay curious across generations

He kept embracing new sounds and young talent.

Do this: Remain open to new ideas and younger collaborators; curiosity is what keeps your work alive over decades.

Frequently asked questions

Who was Quincy Jones?

One of the most influential figures in popular music — a jazz musician, arranger, film composer and record producer across seven decades who produced Michael Jackson's Thriller and won 28 Grammy Awards.

What made Quincy Jones exceptional?

His range and longevity: he moved fluidly across jazz, pop and film, and as a producer his gift was assembling the right talent and drawing out their best performances over a seventy-year career.
