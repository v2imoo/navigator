George Orwell

He gave us the vocabulary to name tyranny — Big Brother, doublethink, thoughtcrime — and a creed of plain honest prose.

4 min read · 1930s–1950

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

George Orwell (Eric Blair) fought in the Spanish Civil War, lived among the poor to write about it, and emerged as the twentieth century's great chronicler of political power and its abuse. His two late masterpieces — Animal Farm, a fable of revolution betrayed, and Nineteen Eighty-Four, a vision of total surveillance and manipulated truth — gave the world an entire vocabulary for tyranny: Big Brother, doublethink, thoughtcrime, newspeak, 'Orwellian'.

Orwell prized clear, honest prose as a political act, arguing that the corruption of language enables the corruption of thought and power. He is a study in writing as moral witness, in the defence of truth and clear language against propaganda, and in the courage to criticise your own side when it betrays its principles.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Defend clear language

Orwell saw clear prose as a defence against tyranny.

Do this: Write and insist on plain, honest language; muddy words enable muddy thinking and manipulation.
Principle 2

Bear moral witness

He wrote from among the poor and the frontlines.

Do this: Go where the truth is, see it firsthand, and report it honestly; proximity and honesty give writing its power.
Principle 3

Criticise your own side

He attacked totalitarianism of every stripe, including allies'.

Do this: Hold your own camp to the same standard as your opponents; intellectual honesty means naming betrayal wherever it occurs.

In their words

Frequently asked questions

Why is George Orwell important?

For Nineteen Eighty-Four and Animal Farm, which gave the world its vocabulary for tyranny — Big Brother, doublethink, 'Orwellian' — and for his defence of clear, honest language as a political act.

What did Orwell believe about language?

That the corruption of language enables the corruption of thought and political power, so writing plainly and honestly is itself a defence against tyranny and propaganda.
