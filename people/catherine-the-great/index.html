Catherine the Great

The German princess who seized the Russian throne and ruled it into a great European power through intelligence, reform, and relentless will.

4 min read · 1762–1796

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Catherine II came to Russia as a minor German princess to marry an unstable heir, spoke no Russian, and had no claim to rule in her own right. Within years she had learned the language, embraced Russian culture, built alliances across the court and army, and — when her husband's brief, disastrous reign threatened everything — led a coup that made her empress. She then ruled for thirty-four years, longer than any other woman in Russian history, expanding the empire and dragging it toward the European Enlightenment.

Catherine was a formidable self-educated intellect who corresponded with Voltaire and Diderot, reformed administration and education, and expanded Russia's territory and influence enormously. She was also a pragmatist who knew the limits of her power, compromising with the nobility whose support she needed. Her reign shows how an outsider with no inherited claim can build legitimacy and power through sheer competence, cultural adaptation, and the shrewd management of the people whose backing she required.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Adapt completely to win legitimacy

Catherine learned Russian, converted faith, and embraced the culture — an outsider earning the right to lead.

Do this: When you're an outsider, adapt fully to earn the legitimacy you weren't born with.
Principle 2

Build your base before you move

She cultivated the army and court for years before seizing power.

Do this: Lay the groundwork of support quietly before making a decisive move.
Principle 3

Educate yourself relentlessly

Self-taught through voracious reading, she made intellect her advantage.

Do this: Make continuous self-education a genuine competitive advantage.

Frequently asked questions

How did Catherine take the Russian throne?

As a German princess with no claim, she learned the language and culture, built support in the army and court over years, and led a coup when her husband's reign collapsed — then ruled for 34 years.

What's the lesson of Catherine the Great?

How an outsider with no inherited claim builds power through total cultural adaptation, patient base-building, relentless self-education, and pragmatic management of those whose support they need.
