Anna Wintour

The editor who turned Vogue into the arbiter of fashion — and herself into an institution.

4 min read · 1988–present

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Anna Wintour ran American Vogue for thirty-seven years and, in the process, became the single most powerful figure in fashion — an editor whose taste could make designers and shape trends, and whose influence extended across an entire industry. She transformed Vogue's covers into cultural statements, mixed high and low fashion, and turned the magazine into the arbiter of what mattered.

Her power came from more than a magazine: she built a position as a tastemaker and kingmaker, championing new designers, steering the industry, and turning the Met Gala into fashion's biggest night. In 2025 she stepped back from day-to-day editing of US Vogue, while remaining Condé Nast's global chief content officer and Vogue's global editorial director — still a dominant force.

Wintour is a study in building personal authority into institutional power — becoming, through consistency of taste and force of will, an institution whose influence outlasts any single role.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Become the arbiter of taste

Wintour's judgement itself became the industry's standard.

Do this: Build such consistent, trusted judgement that your taste becomes the benchmark.
Principle 2

Turn a role into a platform

She made the editorship a seat of industry-wide power.

Do this: Use a position as a platform for influence far beyond its formal remit.
Principle 3

Champion the next generation

She made and elevated new designers, extending her reach.

Do this: Build power by discovering and elevating emerging talent who owe you their break.

Is Anna Wintour still at Vogue?
