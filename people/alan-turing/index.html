Alan Turing

He imagined the universal machine before any computer existed, broke the code that shortened a war, and was destroyed by the country he saved.

4 min read · 1930s–1950s

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Alan Turing laid the theoretical foundation of computing in 1936 with the idea of a universal machine that could compute anything computable — the conceptual blueprint for every computer that followed. During the Second World War he led the team at Bletchley Park that cracked the German Enigma cipher, an achievement historians estimate shortened the war by years and saved countless lives. He also posed the question of machine intelligence that still defines the field: the Turing test.

Turing was prosecuted for homosexuality in 1952, when it was criminal in Britain, and subjected to chemical treatment; he died two years later, his contributions still secret. He received a formal apology from the British government in 2009 and a royal pardon in 2013. He is a study in abstract thinking that reshapes the possible, in applying pure theory to an urgent practical crisis, and in the tragedy of genius punished by prejudice.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Abstract to the essence

Turing reduced 'computation' to a machine of startling simplicity.

Do this: Strip a problem to its most abstract form; the general solution is often simpler than any specific one.
Principle 2

Point theory at the real crisis

He turned pure logic onto breaking a wartime cipher.

Do this: The deepest theoretical skill becomes world-changing when aimed at an urgent concrete problem.
Principle 3

Ask the question that defines a field

His test reframed what 'machine intelligence' even means.

Do this: Sometimes the most valuable contribution is posing the right question, not answering it.

Frequently asked questions

What is Alan Turing famous for?

Inventing the theoretical basis of computer science (the universal Turing machine), leading the team that broke Germany's Enigma code in WWII, and defining machine intelligence through the Turing test.

How did Alan Turing die?

He died in 1954 at 41, two years after being prosecuted for homosexuality and subjected to chemical treatment; Britain issued a formal apology in 2009 and a royal pardon in 2013.
