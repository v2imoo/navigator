Antoni Gaudí

He built as nature does — in curves, color and light — leaving a cathedral still rising a century after his death.

4 min read · 1880s–1926

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Antoni Gaudí created an architecture like no one else's — organic, flowing forms inspired by nature, drenched in colour, mosaic and light, that transformed Barcelona. His masterpiece, the basilica of the Sagrada Família, is so vast and intricate that it remains under construction nearly a century after his death. He drew structural ideas directly from natural forms — branching columns like trees, catenary arches derived from hanging chains — pioneering an approach where beauty and engineering were one.

Devout and single-minded, Gaudí devoted his final years entirely to the Sagrada Família, living on site. He is a study in forging a wholly original style from a singular vision, in learning structure from nature, and in devotion to a work larger than a single lifetime.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Learn structure from nature

Gaudí derived his forms and engineering from natural shapes.

Do this: Nature has solved structural problems over eons; study its forms for both beauty and engineering insight.
Principle 2

Forge a wholly original style

His work resembles no one else's.

Do this: Don't settle for a variation on the prevailing style; a singular vision pursued fully becomes unmistakably yours.
Principle 3

Commit to work beyond a lifetime

He gave his final years to a project he'd never see finished.

Do this: Some works are worth devoting yourself to even knowing you won't see them complete; build for what outlasts you.

Frequently asked questions

Who was Antoni Gaudí?

A singular Catalan architect whose organic, nature-inspired, colour-drenched buildings transformed Barcelona — above all the Sagrada Família basilica, still under construction nearly a century after his death.

How did Gaudí use nature in his work?

He drew structural ideas directly from natural forms — branching tree-like columns, catenary arches from hanging chains — pioneering an approach in which beauty and engineering were inseparable.
