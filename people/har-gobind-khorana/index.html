Har Gobind Khorana

From a village with no electricity he rose to help decode how DNA directs the making of proteins — and won the Nobel.

4 min read · 1950s–2000s

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Har Gobind Khorana was born into a poor family in a tiny village with no electricity, the son of a village clerk, and rose through sheer ability to become one of the great molecular biologists of the century. He shared the 1968 Nobel Prize in Medicine for helping to interpret the genetic code — showing how the sequence of nucleotides in DNA and RNA specifies which amino acids are assembled into proteins. He later achieved the first chemical synthesis of a gene.

Khorana's precise, patient chemistry unlocked one of life's deepest mechanisms. He is a study in rising from the humblest origins through ability and persistence, in meticulous experimental rigour, and in work that decoded the fundamental language of life.
