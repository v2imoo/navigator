Bill Bernbach

His 'Think Small' Volkswagen ads broke every rule — and changed what advertising was allowed to be.

4 min read · 1950s–1980s

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Bill Bernbach, co-founder of the agency Doyle Dane Bernbach (DDB), led the 'Creative Revolution' that transformed advertising in the 1950s and 60s. His landmark Volkswagen campaign — 'Think Small', 'Lemon' — did the unthinkable: it was honest, witty, self-deprecating and visually spare, trusting the audience's intelligence instead of shouting claims. He paired copywriters and art directors as creative teams, a structure the whole industry adopted, and proved that intelligence and honesty could outsell hype.

Bernbach insisted advertising was an art of persuasion rooted in human truth, not a science of formulas. He is a study in breaking category conventions with honesty, in trusting the audience, and in how a fresh creative approach can redefine an entire industry.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Trust the audience's intelligence

Bernbach's VW ads respected and rewarded the reader.

Do this: Assume your audience is smart; honest, witty, understated messages can beat loud claims.
Principle 2

Break the category's conventions

'Think Small' defied every rule of car advertising.

Do this: When everyone in your category does the same thing, doing the opposite — with substance — can make you unmissable.
Principle 3

Root persuasion in human truth

He saw advertising as art grounded in truth, not formula.

Do this: The most persuasive messages connect to a genuine human truth; find it rather than relying on technique.

In their words

The most powerful element in advertising is the truth.
Advertising is fundamentally persuasion, and persuasion is an art, not a science.

Frequently asked questions

Who was Bill Bernbach?

The co-founder of Doyle Dane Bernbach who led advertising's 'Creative Revolution', famous for the witty, honest Volkswagen 'Think Small' campaign and for pairing copywriters with art directors.

Why was the VW 'Think Small' campaign revolutionary?

It broke every convention of car advertising — honest, understated, self-deprecating and visually spare — trusting the audience's intelligence and proving wit and truth could outsell hype.
