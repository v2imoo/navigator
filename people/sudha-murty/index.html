Sudha Murty

She broke a gender barrier in engineering, built a vast philanthropy, and writes stories read across India.

4 min read · 1970s–present

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Sudha Murty broke barriers early: she wrote to the chairman of the automaker TELCO protesting its men-only hiring policy and became its first woman engineer. She later gave her husband Narayana Murthy the seed money to help found Infosys, then led the Infosys Foundation, directing large-scale philanthropy into schools, libraries, healthcare, sanitation and disaster relief across India. She is also one of the country's most beloved and best-selling authors, writing simple, warm stories and children's books in English and Kannada.

Murty is admired for her humility and groundedness despite great wealth. She is a study in challenging an unjust barrier head-on, in channelling success into large-scale giving, and in keeping simplicity and service at the centre of a life of means.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Challenge the unjust barrier directly

Murty wrote to the chairman and broke the hiring policy.

Do this: When a rule unjustly excludes you, confront it directly and specifically; a well-aimed challenge can open the door.
Principle 2

Channel success into giving

She directed Infosys wealth into broad philanthropy.

Do this: Treat success as a means to large-scale good; systematic giving multiplies the impact of what you've built.
Principle 3

Keep simplicity at the centre

She stayed grounded despite great wealth.

Do this: Humility and simplicity keep you connected to the people you serve; let means expand your giving, not your ego.

In their words

Money is not the only thing that gives you happiness.
Helping others is the rent you pay for your room here on earth.
Simplicity is the ultimate sophistication in life.
