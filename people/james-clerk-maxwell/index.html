James Clerk Maxwell

His four equations united electricity, magnetism and light — the greatest synthesis in physics between Newton and Einstein.

4 min read · 1850s–1879

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

James Clerk Maxwell achieved one of the greatest unifications in the history of science. Building on Faraday's intuitions, he showed with a set of elegant equations that electricity and magnetism are two aspects of a single force — electromagnetism — and that light itself is an electromagnetic wave. His equations predicted the existence of radio waves and the whole electromagnetic spectrum, laying the foundation for radio, radar, television and modern telecommunications.

Maxwell also made fundamental contributions to thermodynamics and the kinetic theory of gases. His unification is often ranked just below Newton's and Einstein's. He is a study in the power of mathematical synthesis, in uniting phenomena that look separate, and in theory that predicts entirely new realities before they are observed.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Unify what looks separate

Maxwell showed electricity and magnetism are one force.

Do this: Look for the deeper single principle beneath phenomena treated as unrelated; unification is the highest form of insight.
Principle 2

Trust the mathematics

His equations predicted waves no one had seen.

Do this: When a rigorous theory implies something new, take the prediction seriously; mathematics can see before experiment does.
Principle 3

Build on others' intuitions

He gave Faraday's physical pictures their mathematical form.

Do this: Translating someone else's intuition into rigorous form can be a breakthrough in itself; build on the best raw ideas.

Frequently asked questions

What did James Clerk Maxwell discover?

That electricity and magnetism are aspects of a single force — electromagnetism — and that light is an electromagnetic wave; his equations predicted the entire electromagnetic spectrum, enabling radio and modern telecommunications.

Why is Maxwell ranked so highly among physicists?

His unification of electricity, magnetism and light is considered the greatest synthesis in physics between Newton and Einstein, and the foundation of virtually all electromagnetic technology.
