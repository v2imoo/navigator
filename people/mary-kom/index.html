Mary Kom

From a remote village in Manipur she boxed her way to six world titles and Olympic glory — a mother and a champion.

4 min read · 2000s–present

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Mangte Chungneijang 'Mary' Kom grew up in a poor farming family in a remote part of Manipur in northeast India and took up boxing against the expectations of her surroundings. She became one of the greatest amateur boxers in history — a six-time world champion and an Olympic medallist — competing and winning across many years, including after taking time off to have children and returning to the top, earning the nickname 'Magnificent Mary'.

Kom overcame poverty, geography and gender expectations through sheer determination. She is a study in rising from the margins to the summit of a demanding sport, in combining motherhood with elite competition, and in the discipline and resilience that let her keep winning across a long career.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Rise from the margins

Kom reached the top from a remote, poor village.

Do this: Geography and circumstance need not cap your ambition; determination can carry you from the margins to the summit.
Principle 2

Defy the expectations set for you

She boxed against her surroundings' norms.

Do this: The limits others place on you by background or gender are not yours to accept; prove them wrong through performance.
Principle 3

Come back stronger

She returned to the top after having children.

Do this: A break — even a major life change — need not end elite performance; disciplined return can bring you back to the top.
