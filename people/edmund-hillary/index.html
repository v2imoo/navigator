Edmund Hillary

The beekeeper who stood atop Everest — then spent his life repaying the Sherpa people who made it possible.

4 min read · 1950s–2008

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Edmund Hillary, a New Zealand beekeeper and mountaineer, became with Tenzing Norgay the first person confirmed to reach the summit of Mount Everest, the world's highest mountain, on 29 May 1953 — a feat that captured the world's imagination. He was always generous in sharing credit with Tenzing and consistently refused to say which of them stepped onto the summit first.

Rather than resting on the fame, Hillary devoted much of his later life to the Sherpa people of Nepal, building schools, hospitals and bridges through his Himalayan Trust. He is a study in achieving a historic first through grit and teamwork, in generosity and humility in sharing glory, and in giving back to the community that made the achievement possible.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Share the credit

Hillary never claimed to have reached the summit first.

Do this: Generously sharing credit for a shared achievement builds trust and honours the truth that few things are done alone.
Principle 2

Give back to those who made it possible

He devoted his life to the Sherpa community.

Do this: Reinvest the fruits of your success into the people and community that enabled it; gratitude made concrete is a legacy.
Principle 3

Let achievement be a beginning

He used fame as a platform for service.

Do this: Treat a great accomplishment as the start of contribution, not the end; what you do afterward can matter more.
