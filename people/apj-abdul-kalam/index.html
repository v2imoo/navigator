A. P. J. Abdul Kalam

A newspaper-selling boy who became the architect of India's missile programme — and its most loved president.

4 min read · 1960s–2015

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

A. P. J. Abdul Kalam grew up poor in a small coastal town, selling newspapers as a boy, and became one of India's most important scientists. As a leading figure in the country's space and defence research, he was the driving force behind its satellite-launch and ballistic-missile programmes — earning the name 'Missile Man' — and a key organiser of the 1998 nuclear tests. In 2002 he became India's 11th president and, through his warmth, humility and relentless focus on inspiring the young, became known as the 'People's President'.

Kalam spent his later years travelling the country urging students to dream big and build a developed India. He is a study in rising from poverty through science and discipline, in combining technical mastery with moral purpose, and in using a position of honour to light a fire under the next generation.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Let purpose outrank position

Kalam used the presidency to inspire students, not to wield power.

Do this: Treat any position you reach as a platform for a larger purpose, not an end in itself.
Principle 2

Dream, then build the ladder

He urged big dreams backed by disciplined work.

Do this: Aim high and then do the unglamorous engineering to get there; vision without execution is just a wish.
Principle 3

Invest in the next generation

His later life was devoted to the young.

Do this: The most durable legacy is the people you inspire and equip; pour into those who come after you.

In their words

Dream, dream, dream. Dreams transform into thoughts and thoughts result in action.
You have to dream before your dreams can come true.
If you want to shine like a sun, first burn like a sun.

Frequently asked questions

Who was A. P. J. Abdul Kalam?

An Indian aerospace scientist known as the 'Missile Man' for his central role in India's space and missile programmes, who became the country's 11th president (2002–07) and its beloved 'People's President'.

Why was Kalam called the 'People's President'?

For his warmth, humility and tireless dedication to inspiring young Indians to dream big and build the nation, which made him one of the most loved public figures in the country's history.
