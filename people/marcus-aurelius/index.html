Marcus Aurelius

The most powerful man in the world wrote a private notebook to himself on how to stay good — and it became a timeless guide.

4 min read · 2nd century

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Marcus Aurelius ruled the Roman Empire at its height and is remembered as the model of the philosopher-king. Throughout his reign — amid wars, plague and the crushing pressures of absolute power — he kept a private journal of Stoic reflections, never intended for publication, reminding himself to act with reason, justice and self-control. That notebook survives as Meditations, one of the most widely read works of practical philosophy ever written.

Stoicism taught him to focus only on what is within his control — his own judgments and actions — and to accept the rest with equanimity. He is a study in self-mastery under pressure, in the discipline of distinguishing what you can and cannot control, and in using private reflection to stay anchored to your values when everything around you tempts otherwise.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Focus on what you control

Marcus trained himself to let go of everything else.

Do this: Separate what's within your power — your actions and judgments — from what isn't, and invest energy only in the former.
Principle 2

Reflect in private to stay anchored

His journal kept him true to his values.

Do this: Keep a private practice of reflection; writing to yourself keeps your principles present amid pressure.
Principle 3

Act with reason, not reaction

Stoicism taught him to respond deliberately, not impulsively.

Do this: Put a beat between stimulus and response; choose your action by reason rather than being driven by emotion.

In their words

You have power over your mind — not outside events. Realise this, and you will find strength.
The happiness of your life depends upon the quality of your thoughts.
Waste no more time arguing about what a good man should be. Be one.

What is 'Meditations'?

What is the core Stoic idea Marcus practised?

The dichotomy of control: focus your energy only on what is within your power — your own judgments and actions — and accept what is not with equanimity.
