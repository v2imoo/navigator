Noubar Afeyan

He invents companies the way others invent products — 'emergent discovery' turned into Moderna and dozens more.

Part I — The story

The story

Noubar Afeyan, an Armenian born in Lebanon who emigrated to North America, built Flagship Pioneering — a firm that doesn't just invest in biotech startups but systematically invents them. His method, 'emergent discovery', starts with provocative 'what if' questions, explores them scientifically, and spins out companies from the ones that prove promising. Flagship's most famous creation is Moderna, which Afeyan co-founded and chaired, built on the then-radical bet that mRNA could be a medicine.

Afeyan treats entrepreneurship itself as a scientific, repeatable process of generating and testing bold hypotheses. He is a study in institutionalising the creation of companies, in embracing ideas that sound unreasonable, and in the immigrant's willingness to bet on the improbable.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Institutionalise invention

Afeyan built a system that reliably creates companies.

Do this: Treat the creation of new ventures as a repeatable process of generating and testing bold hypotheses, not a lucky one-off.
Principle 2

Start from provocative questions

His method begins with 'what if'.

Do this: Begin with the unreasonable question others dismiss; the best new companies often sit behind an idea that sounds absurd.
Principle 3

Embrace the improbable

Moderna rested on a bet most thought unlikely.

Do this: Be willing to pursue ideas that seem improbable if the upside is transformative; outsized returns live in unreasonable bets.

In their words

Entrepreneurs are people who choose to believe something that isn't yet true.
Start with a question so bold it sounds unreasonable.
Innovation lives at the edge of the plausible.
