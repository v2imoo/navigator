Thomas J. Watson

The salesman who built IBM's culture, sales machine, and one word: THINK.

4 min read · 1914–1956

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Thomas J. Watson Sr. took over a struggling maker of tabulating machines and, over four decades, built it into IBM — the company that would dominate business computing. He was a salesman above all, and he built IBM's legendary sales culture: sharply dressed representatives, rigorous training, company songs, and the single-word motto he made famous — THINK — posted throughout the company.

Watson understood that IBM sold not machines but solutions to business problems, and he built deep, service-oriented customer relationships that produced enormous loyalty and recurring revenue through leasing. His culture of discipline, pride and customer service made IBM one of the most admired and powerful companies of the twentieth century, setting the stage for its later computing dominance.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Sell solutions, not products

Watson positioned IBM as solving business problems, not selling machines.

Do this: Sell the outcome and solution the customer wants, not just the product.
Principle 2

Build culture as an advantage

IBM's disciplined, proud sales culture drove its success.

Do this: Invest in a strong culture; it can be your most durable competitive edge.
Principle 3

Create recurring revenue

Leasing built loyalty and predictable income.

Do this: Structure the model for recurring revenue and long-term customer relationships.

Frequently asked questions

How did Thomas Watson build IBM?

By transforming a tabulating-machine maker through a legendary, disciplined sales culture — selling business solutions rather than machines — and building loyalty and recurring revenue via leasing and service.

What was Watson's famous motto?

THINK — a one-word exhortation he posted throughout IBM, emblematic of the disciplined, thoughtful culture he built.
