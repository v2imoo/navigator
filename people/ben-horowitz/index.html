Ben Horowitz

The founder-turned-VC who wrote the honest book on the brutal reality of leading a company.

4 min read · 1995–present

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Ben Horowitz co-founded the venture firm Andreessen Horowitz with Marc Andreessen, and became one of the most respected voices in startups by telling the unvarnished truth about how hard building a company really is. Before investing, he lived it — running the company Opsware through the dot-com crash, near-death experiences and brutal decisions, before selling it for $1.6 billion.

His book The Hard Thing About Hard Things broke from the tidy success stories of most business books to describe the actual experience of leadership: the agonising layoffs, the impossible calls with no good options, the loneliness of the CEO. That honesty, plus a philosophy of backing bold technical founders, made a16z one of the most influential firms in venture capital.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Tell the truth about difficulty

Horowitz describes leadership's brutal reality, not a sanitised version.

Do this: Face and name the hard truths of a situation rather than a comforting story.
Principle 2

Make the hard call

He's known for decisions with no good options, made anyway.

Do this: When every option is bad, still decide — indecision is usually the worst choice.
Principle 3

Back bold founders

a16z supports ambitious technical founders through difficulty.

Do this: Back ambitious builders and support them through the genuinely hard stretches.

In their words

There are no silver bullets, only lead bullets.
The hard thing isn't setting a big goal. The hard thing is laying people off when you miss it.
Take care of the people, the products, and the profits — in that order.
