Robert Noyce

The 'Mayor of Silicon Valley' who co-invented the microchip and co-founded the company that defined it.

4 min read · 1957–1990

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Robert Noyce co-invented the integrated circuit — the microchip — the foundation of essentially all modern electronics, and then co-founded both Fairchild Semiconductor and Intel, the companies that turned the invention into an industry. More than almost anyone, Noyce created the technical and cultural DNA of Silicon Valley.

Charismatic and egalitarian, he pioneered a flat, informal management style — no reserved parking, open collaboration, stock options for employees — that became the Valley's template. Known as the 'Mayor of Silicon Valley', he mentored a generation, including Steve Jobs, and helped make the region the centre of the technology world.

The playbook

Frequently asked questions

What did Robert Noyce invent?

He co-invented the integrated circuit — the microchip — the foundation of virtually all modern electronics, then co-founded Fairchild Semiconductor and Intel to build the industry around it.

Why is Noyce called the 'Mayor of Silicon Valley'?

For his foundational role in the region's technology and his egalitarian, options-driven management culture, which became the Valley's template and influenced figures including Steve Jobs.
