Abraham Maslow

He turned psychology's gaze from what's broken in people to what's possible — the drive to become all you can be.

4 min read · 1940s–1970

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Abraham Maslow shifted psychology's focus from illness to human potential. He proposed the 'hierarchy of needs' — the famous pyramid in which basic physiological and safety needs must be met before people can pursue belonging, esteem and, at the top, 'self-actualisation', the realisation of one's full potential. By studying healthy, high-functioning people rather than the disturbed, he helped found humanistic psychology and reframed what the field could be about.

Maslow's ideas pervade management, education and self-development, shaping how organisations think about motivation. The strict pyramid is debated, but the core idea — that humans are drawn upward toward growth and meaning once lower needs are secured — remains influential. He is a study in studying excellence rather than only dysfunction, in the architecture of human motivation, and in the pull toward growth.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Study excellence, not just failure

Maslow learned from the healthiest people, not the sickest.

Do this: To understand what's possible, examine those thriving at it, not only those struggling; model the best.
Principle 2

Secure the base before the peak

His hierarchy puts basic needs before higher ones.

Do this: Address foundational needs — security, belonging — before expecting people (or yourself) to pursue growth.
Principle 3

Aim toward growth and meaning

He saw self-actualisation as the human pull.

Do this: Once basics are met, orient toward becoming more fully what you're capable of; growth is a need, not a luxury.

Frequently asked questions

What is Maslow's hierarchy of needs?

Abraham Maslow's model, often shown as a pyramid, in which basic physiological and safety needs must be met before people pursue belonging, esteem and finally 'self-actualisation' — realising their full potential.

Why was Maslow influential?

He shifted psychology from focusing on illness to human potential, founding humanistic psychology by studying healthy, high-functioning people, and his ideas shape how we think about motivation in work and life.
