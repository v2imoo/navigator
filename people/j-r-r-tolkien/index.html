J. R. R. Tolkien

A philology professor invented entire languages, then built a world around them — and modern fantasy was born.

4 min read · 1930s–1970s

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

J. R. R. Tolkien was an Oxford professor of philology — the study of languages — whose lifelong love of invented languages led him to build an entire world for them to live in. Over decades he created Middle-earth, with its own languages, histories, maps, mythologies and peoples, and set within it The Hobbit and The Lord of the Rings. The depth and internal consistency of his world-building essentially founded the modern fantasy genre.

Tolkien worked slowly and obsessively, drawing on his scholarship, his faith and his experience of war to give his fantasy moral weight. He is a study in building a world of total depth and consistency, in letting a deep personal passion (here, language) seed something vast, and in the patience to construct something that rewards a lifetime of attention.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Build the world deeply and consistently

Tolkien's Middle-earth has its own languages and histories.

Do this: Depth and internal consistency make an invented world believable; build the iceberg beneath what readers see.
Principle 2

Let a deep passion seed the work

His love of languages grew into an entire mythology.

Do this: Follow an obsessive personal interest far enough and it can become the foundation of something vast and original.
Principle 3

Work with patience

He built Middle-earth over decades.

Do this: Some great works can't be rushed; patient, obsessive construction produces depth that quick work never matches.

Frequently asked questions

Why is Tolkien considered the father of modern fantasy?

His Middle-earth — with its own invented languages, histories, maps and mythologies — set a standard of deep, internally consistent world-building that essentially founded the modern fantasy genre.

How did Tolkien create Middle-earth?

As an Oxford philologist, his love of inventing languages led him to build an entire world for them to inhabit, constructed slowly and obsessively over decades and drawing on his scholarship, faith and war experience.
