Marc Andreessen

The founder who built the browser that opened the web, then became one of tech's most influential investors.

4 min read · 1993–present

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Marc Andreessen co-wrote Mosaic, the browser that made the World Wide Web usable for ordinary people, and then co-founded Netscape, whose 1995 IPO effectively launched the internet boom. Few people can claim to have opened up an entire medium; Andreessen did it while still in his early twenties.

After Netscape, he built and sold more companies, then co-founded the venture firm Andreessen Horowitz (a16z), which became one of Silicon Valley's most influential investors. His essay 'Why Software Is Eating the World' captured a thesis he built a career on: that software would remake every industry, and that backing the founders building it was the great opportunity of the age.

His defining thesis

Andreessen is a rare figure who shaped technology as both a founder and a financier — first building the on-ramp to the web, then funding the companies that drove software into every corner of the economy.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Build the on-ramp to a new medium

Mosaic made the web usable, unlocking a whole industry.

Do this: Look for the tool that makes an emerging technology accessible to everyone.
Principle 2

Back the strong thesis

a16z invested behind the clear conviction that software eats the world.

Do this: Form a strong, specific thesis about the future and commit behind it.
Principle 3

Move from building to backing

Andreessen turned founder experience into investing edge.

Do this: Use hard-won operating experience to judge and support the next builders.
