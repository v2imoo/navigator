Jack Ma

The English teacher who built China's e-commerce giant by connecting the country's small businesses to the world.

4 min read · 1999–present

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Jack Ma built Alibaba into one of the world's largest e-commerce companies by solving a problem the giants ignored: connecting China's vast base of small manufacturers and merchants to buyers, first globally through Alibaba.com and then domestically through Taobao and Tmall. He won by understanding local conditions that foreign rivals like eBay did not.

A former English teacher who twice failed university entrance exams and was rejected from dozens of jobs, Ma had no technical background but an unmatched instinct for people and persuasion. He beat eBay in China by making his marketplace free for sellers and building trust mechanisms suited to Chinese commerce.

Stepped down as chairman

Ma became China's most famous entrepreneur and a global symbol of its tech rise, before stepping back in 2019 and largely withdrawing from public life. His marketplace mastered the two-sided network effect: more merchants drew more buyers, which drew more merchants.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Serve the underserved

Ma connected small merchants the giants overlooked, building a huge base others ignored.

Do this: Find the large, neglected group your bigger rivals don't bother to serve.
Principle 2

Win on local knowledge

He beat a global incumbent by tailoring the model to local trust and payment realities.

Do this: Against a bigger global rival, win with deep local understanding they lack.
Principle 3

Remove the barrier to the hard side

Making Taobao free for sellers seeded the merchant side of the network first.

Do this: Subsidise the harder side of a marketplace to get the network spinning.

In their words

Today is hard, tomorrow will be worse, but the day after tomorrow will be beautiful.
Never give up. Today is hard, tomorrow will be worse.
If you don't give up, you still have a chance.

Frequently asked questions

How did Alibaba beat eBay in China?

By making its marketplace free for sellers, building trust and payment tools suited to local commerce, and understanding Chinese conditions the foreign incumbent didn't.

Is Jack Ma still running Alibaba?

No — he stepped down as chairman in 2019 and has largely withdrawn from public life.
