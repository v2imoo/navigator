The scholar who turned strategy into a rigorous discipline — and gave managers the tools to see why industries make money.

Part I — The story

The story

Michael Porter, a Harvard economist, transformed business strategy from a vague art into a rigorous analytical discipline. His 1980 book Competitive Strategy and its 'Five Forces' framework gave managers a systematic way to understand why some industries are profitable and others are not — analysing the power of suppliers and buyers, the threat of new entrants and substitutes, and the intensity of rivalry. It became perhaps the most influential framework in all of business.

Porter's deeper argument was about the nature of strategy itself: that strategy means deliberately choosing to be different, performing different activities or the same activities differently from rivals, and that the essence of strategy is choosing what not to do. He warned that operational effectiveness is not strategy, and that trying to be all things to all customers is a path to mediocrity. He is a study in rigorous analysis of competition, in strategy as deliberate differentiation and trade-offs, and in the discipline of choosing what not to do.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Analyse the five forces

Porter's framework reveals why an industry is or isn't profitable.

Do this: Assess suppliers, buyers, entrants, substitutes and rivalry to judge an industry's economics.
Principle 2

Strategy is deliberate difference

He defined strategy as choosing to perform activities differently from rivals.

Do this: Strategy means deliberately being different, not just operating more efficiently.
Principle 3

Choose what not to do

Porter stressed that trade-offs — what you won't do — are the heart of strategy.

Do this: Decide clearly what you will NOT do; trying to do everything guarantees mediocrity.

In their words

The essence of strategy is choosing what not to do.
Operational effectiveness is not strategy.
If your goal is anything but to be the best, you've already failed.

Frequently asked questions

What are Porter's Five Forces?

A framework for analysing why an industry is profitable or not — the power of suppliers and buyers, the threat of new entrants and substitutes, and the intensity of competitive rivalry — perhaps the most influential framework in business.

What is Porter's definition of strategy?

Deliberately choosing to be different — performing different activities, or the same activities differently, from rivals — with the essence being the trade-offs: choosing what NOT to do.
