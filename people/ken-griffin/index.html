Ken Griffin

The founder who built a hedge-fund and market-making powerhouse on technology and relentless risk discipline.

4 min read · 1990–present

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Ken Griffin built Citadel into one of the world's most successful hedge funds, and Citadel Securities into a dominant market maker that handles a vast share of US stock trading. He started trading convertible bonds from his Harvard dorm room, and built his firms on a foundation of technology, quantitative rigour and disciplined risk management rather than star-manager intuition alone.

Griffin's edge is institutional: a multi-strategy machine of specialised teams, cutting-edge technology, and a famously rigorous approach to managing risk and talent. Citadel survived a near-catastrophic loss in the 2008 crisis and came back stronger, a testament to the resilience of a system built on process rather than a single genius.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Build a system, not a star

Citadel is a machine of teams and technology, not one genius.

Do this: Build an institution whose edge survives any single person leaving.
Principle 2

Invest in technology and rigour

Griffin's firms compete on technology and quantitative discipline.

Do this: Make technology and rigorous process a core competitive advantage.
Principle 3

Manage risk relentlessly

Disciplined risk management let Citadel survive and recover from crisis.

Do this: Treat risk management as central, not an afterthought — it's what lets you survive.
