Phil Knight

The founder who built Nike into a brand by selling belief and emotion, not just shoes.

4 min read · 1964–2016

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Phil Knight built Nike into the world's dominant athletic brand by understanding that people don't just buy shoes — they buy identity, aspiration and emotion. Starting by selling Japanese running shoes from the boot of his car, he and coach Bill Bowerman built a company whose real product became the feeling embodied in a swoosh and a slogan.

Knight outsourced manufacturing early to focus Nike's energy on design and marketing, and pioneered athlete endorsement as brand-building — most famously with Michael Jordan, whose Air Jordan line became a business in itself. Nike sold performance, but won on story.

Knight's memoir Shoe Dog revealed how close Nike came to failing repeatedly. His insight — that a brand built on emotion and story commands loyalty and margins a commodity never could — made Nike a case study in the power of branding.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Sell emotion, not the object

Knight built Nike on aspiration and identity, not shoe specifications.

Do this: Attach your product to a feeling and identity customers want to own.
Principle 2

Focus on brand; outsource the rest

Nike outsourced manufacturing to pour energy into design and marketing.

Do this: Concentrate on where your advantage lives; contract out the commodity parts.
Principle 3

Borrow credibility from heroes

Athlete endorsements transferred greatness onto the brand.

Do this: Associate your brand with people who embody what customers aspire to.

In their words

Frequently asked questions

What made Nike's marketing so powerful?

Knight sold identity and emotion rather than shoes, and borrowed credibility from athletes like Michael Jordan — building a brand that commands loyalty and margins a commodity can't.

How did Nike start?

Phil Knight began by selling Japanese running shoes from his car, before he and Bill Bowerman built Blue Ribbon Sports into Nike.
