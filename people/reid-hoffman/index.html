Reid Hoffman

The founder-investor who mapped how startups grow explosively — and built the professional network of the world.

4 min read · 1997–present

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Reid Hoffman co-founded LinkedIn, building the world's dominant professional network on a powerful network effect — the service becomes more valuable as more professionals join — and sold it to Microsoft for over $26 billion. An early PayPal executive and a legendarily connected investor, he backed companies including Facebook and Airbnb, becoming one of the most influential figures in Silicon Valley both as a builder and a thinker.

Hoffman articulated the concept of 'blitzscaling' — prioritising speed over efficiency to achieve massive scale and win winner-take-most markets before competitors can. His books and framework codified how the most valuable technology companies grow, arguing that in network-effect markets, being first to scale matters more than being perfect. He is a study in network effects, in the strategic logic of rapid scaling, and in the power of building a genuinely useful network that compounds in value.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Build the network effect

LinkedIn grew more valuable with every professional who joined — a compounding moat.

Do this: Design products whose value grows as more people join the network.
Principle 2

Blitzscale in winner-take-most markets

Hoffman argued speed to scale beats efficiency when network effects decide the winner.

Do this: In network-effect markets, prioritise speed to scale over early efficiency.
Principle 3

Build and invest on relationships

His vast network powered both LinkedIn and his investing.

Do this: Treat relationships as a compounding asset — they open doors capital can't.

In their words

If you're not embarrassed by the first version of your product, you've launched too late.
An entrepreneur is someone who jumps off a cliff and builds a plane on the way down.
Scale first, optimise later — in markets that take all.

Frequently asked questions

What is 'blitzscaling'?

Reid Hoffman's concept of prioritising speed over efficiency to reach massive scale quickly — because in winner-take-most, network-effect markets, being first to scale matters more than being perfect.

What did Reid Hoffman build?

LinkedIn, the world's dominant professional network, built on a network effect and sold to Microsoft for over $26 billion — alongside a prolific investing career backing Facebook, Airbnb and others.
