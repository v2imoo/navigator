Joseph Schumpeter

He saw capitalism's engine clearly: the relentless storm of innovation that destroys the old to build the new.

4 min read · 1910s–1950

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Joseph Schumpeter put the entrepreneur and innovation at the centre of economics. His most enduring idea is 'creative destruction': the process by which new technologies, products and business models constantly destroy the old ones, driving capitalism forward in waves. Progress, he argued, is not smooth equilibrium but a perpetual storm of disruption in which entrepreneurs are the restless agents of change — the people who carry out 'new combinations'.

Schumpeter saw innovation, not price competition, as the true engine of growth and the real threat to any incumbent. His framework anticipates modern thinking on disruption and dynamic competition. He is a study in seeing markets as dynamic rather than static, in the central role of the entrepreneur, and in understanding that every dominant position is temporary in the face of the next innovation.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Expect creative destruction

Schumpeter saw every incumbent as vulnerable to the next innovation.

Do this: Assume your current advantage is temporary; the real threat is the new combination, not today's rivals.
Principle 2

Compete on innovation, not just price

He saw innovation as the true engine of growth.

Do this: Win by changing the game with new products and models, not only by undercutting on price within the old one.
Principle 3

Be the one making new combinations

His entrepreneur recombines resources in novel ways.

Do this: Innovation is often recombination — connect existing elements in a new way that creates disproportionate value.

In their words

Creative destruction is the essential fact about capitalism.
The problem is not how to keep the old; it is how to produce the new.
Entrepreneurs are the pivot on which everything turns.

Frequently asked questions

What is 'creative destruction'?

Joseph Schumpeter's idea that capitalism advances through a perpetual storm of innovation in which new technologies, products and business models constantly destroy and replace the old ones.

Why is Schumpeter relevant to business?

His focus on innovation and the entrepreneur as the engine of growth — and the vulnerability of every incumbent to the next disruption — directly anticipates modern thinking on disruptive innovation and dynamic competition.
