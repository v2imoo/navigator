King Camp Gillette

The salesman who gave away razors to sell blades forever — and named a business model that still runs the world.

4 min read · 1901–1932

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

King Camp Gillette invented the disposable safety razor — a cheap blade thrown away when dull, instead of an expensive one laboriously sharpened — and in doing so gave the world not just a product but one of the most enduring business models in commerce. A travelling salesman who had long searched for a product people would buy, use up, and buy again, he found it in the razor blade: a consumable that generates repeat purchases forever.

The model that bears his name — 'razor-and-blades' — is to sell the durable device cheaply, even at a loss, to lock customers into buying the profitable consumable repeatedly. It underpins printers and ink, consoles and games, coffee pods, and countless other businesses today. Gillette is a study in designing for recurring revenue, in the power of the consumable over the one-off sale, and in finding the product that customers must keep buying rather than buy once.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Sell the consumable, not the device

Gillette made money on blades bought forever, not the razor bought once.

Do this: Build around a consumable customers repurchase, not a one-off sale.
Principle 2

Give away the device to lock in the stream

He sold razors cheaply to capture profitable repeat blade sales.

Do this: Subsidise the durable product to lock in the recurring, profitable consumable.
Principle 3

Find what customers must rebuy

He searched deliberately for a product people use up and buy again.

Do this: Seek the offering customers must keep buying, not just buy once.

In their words

There is no new thing under the sun, but there are new ways of looking at things.
Make the razor cheap and sell the blades.
Find the product people use once and throw away — then sell it to them forever.

Frequently asked questions

What is the 'razor-and-blades' model?

Selling a durable device cheaply, even at a loss, to lock customers into repeatedly buying a profitable consumable — named for Gillette, who sold razors cheaply to sell blades forever. It underpins printers/ink, consoles/games and more.

What did King Gillette invent?

The disposable safety razor — a cheap blade thrown away when dull — and, with it, the razor-and-blades business model built on recurring consumable sales.
