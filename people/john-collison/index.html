John Collison

With his brother he reduced accepting payments online to a few lines of code — and wired the internet's economy.

Part I — The story

The story

John Collison, with his older brother Patrick, co-founded Stripe in 2010 to solve a problem that was absurdly hard at the time: accepting payments on the internet. Their insight was that developers, not finance teams, were the real customers, and that payments should be reducible to a few lines of clean code. Stripe became the payments infrastructure for a huge share of the internet economy, from startups to giants, and one of the most valuable private companies in the world.

The Collisons are known for obsessive attention to developer experience, elegant design, and a long-term, intellectually curious culture. He is a study in finding the customer everyone overlooked (developers), in making a brutally hard thing feel simple, and in building infrastructure that quietly powers an entire economy.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Serve the overlooked customer

The Collisons built for developers, not finance teams.

Do this: Identify who really adopts your product and build for them; the overlooked user is often the true buyer.
Principle 2

Make the brutally hard feel simple

Stripe hid enormous complexity behind clean code.

Do this: The deepest value is absorbing complexity so your users don't have to; simplicity on the surface is hard-won engineering.
Principle 3

Build the infrastructure layer

Stripe powers others' businesses rather than competing.

Do this: Being the rails others build on is durable, high-leverage positioning; enable the ecosystem and grow with it.

In their words

We want to increase the GDP of the internet.
Pay obsessive attention to the details users touch.
The hard part is making it simple.
