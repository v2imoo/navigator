Tobi Lütke

The programmer who couldn't find good software to sell snowboards, so he built the platform that powers millions of stores.

4 min read · 2004–present

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Tobi Lütke built Shopify, the platform that lets anyone start and run an online store, out of his own frustration: trying to sell snowboards online, he found the available e-commerce software so poor that he wrote his own — and realised the tool was a bigger opportunity than the snowboards. Shopify became the arms dealer of e-commerce, empowering millions of merchants to compete with giants like Amazon.

A programmer by background, Lütke made Shopify a platform: a core commerce engine surrounded by an ecosystem of apps, themes and developers, so the product grew more capable without Shopify building everything itself. He positioned it explicitly on the side of independent merchants — arming the rebels rather than becoming the empire.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Solve your own problem

Lütke built Shopify because existing tools failed him.

Do this: The tool you build to solve your own problem may be the real business.
Principle 2

Build a platform, not just a product

An ecosystem of apps and developers extended Shopify's reach.

Do this: Let an ecosystem build atop your core so it grows beyond what you can build.
Principle 3

Pick a side

Shopify positioned itself with independent merchants against the giants.

Do this: Stand clearly for a group of customers underserved by the incumbents.

In their words

Building a business is a search for a problem worth solving.
Arm the rebels.
Entrepreneurship is a leap of faith paired with relentless iteration.
