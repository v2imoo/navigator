Stan Lee

The writer who built the most valuable story universe in the world, one flawed hero at a time.

4 min read · 1939–2018

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Stan Lee co-created a huge share of the Marvel Universe — Spider-Man, the X-Men, Iron Man, the Hulk, the Fantastic Four and more — building what became the most valuable collection of story characters in the world. His innovation was to make superheroes human: flawed, anxious, struggling with money and relationships, which made them relatable in a way comics had never been.

Lee understood, decades early, that he was building not just stories but a connected universe of intellectual property — characters who crossed over into each other's stories, creating a web far more valuable than any single hero. That interconnected IP, cultivated over decades, became the foundation of the Marvel film empire, one of the most successful franchises in entertainment history.

Lee is a case study in building intellectual property as a compounding asset — an interconnected universe of characters worth vastly more together than apart. Like Walt Disney, he understood that beloved characters, cultivated over time, become one of the most durable and valuable assets a company can own.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Make it relatable

Lee's flawed, human heroes connected with audiences as perfect ones couldn't.

Do this: Give your creations genuine humanity and flaws people can relate to.
Principle 2

Build a connected universe

Interlinked characters were worth far more than the sum of parts.

Do this: Build interconnected assets that reinforce and cross-promote each other.
Principle 3

Cultivate IP as a long-term asset

Marvel's value compounded over decades of storytelling.

Do this: Treat intellectual property as an asset that compounds in value over time.

What made Marvel's characters special?
