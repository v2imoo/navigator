Bill Ackman

He makes a handful of huge, public bets a year — and isn't afraid to be loudly, expensively wrong.

4 min read · 1990s–present

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Bill Ackman runs Pershing Square Capital with a highly concentrated, activist approach: he holds only a handful of positions, takes large stakes in companies he believes are mispriced or badly run, and often campaigns publicly for change. His career is a study in extremes — spectacular wins like a roughly $2.6 billion gain from a pandemic hedge in early 2020, and painful public losses like his multi-year short of Herbalife and a costly bet on Valeant.

Ackman is unusually transparent, laying out detailed theses in long public presentations and letters, and willing to stake his reputation on a view. He is a study in the power and peril of concentration, in the activist's leverage to force change rather than just predict it, and in the resilience required to be publicly wrong and keep going.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Concentrate on your best ideas

Ackman holds only a handful of deeply researched positions.

Do this: Rather than diversifying thinly, put real weight behind the few ideas you understand best.
Principle 2

Be the catalyst, not just the predictor

As an activist he forces the change he's betting on.

Do this: Where you can, act to make your thesis come true rather than merely waiting for it to.
Principle 3

Show your work publicly

He publishes detailed theses and defends them openly.

Do this: Laying out your reasoning in full sharpens it and builds credibility — but be ready to own it when wrong.

In their words

Experience is making mistakes and learning from them.
I'm not afraid of being wrong; I'm afraid of staying wrong.
Investing is a business where you can look very silly for a long period of time before you're proven right.

Frequently asked questions

Who is Bill Ackman?

The founder of Pershing Square Capital, known for highly concentrated, activist investing — taking large stakes in a few companies and campaigning publicly for change, with both spectacular wins and painful public losses.

What is activist investing?

Taking a large enough stake in a company to push for changes — in strategy, management or capital allocation — rather than passively holding, aiming to unlock value yourself rather than wait for it.
