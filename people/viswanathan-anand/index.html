Viswanathan Anand

The 'Lightning Kid' from Chennai became world champion — and lit the fuse for India's chess explosion.

4 min read · 1980s–present

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Viswanathan Anand became India's first chess grandmaster and went on to be a five-time World Chess Champion, one of the greatest players in the game's history. Famed in his youth for his astonishing speed of calculation — the 'Lightning Kid' — he rose from a country with almost no chess infrastructure to beat the world's best, holding the world title across the classical, knockout and tournament formats and remaining elite for over three decades.

Anand's calm temperament, adaptability across chess's changing eras (including the rise of computer preparation), and humility made him a beloved ambassador who inspired a whole generation of Indian players. He is a study in rising from nothing to the summit of a global discipline, in adapting your craft as its tools transform, and in lifting a nation's standard by going first.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Go first and light the way

Anand pioneered chess for a whole nation.

Do this: Being first from your context to reach the top opens the door for everyone behind you; the trail itself is a legacy.
Principle 2

Adapt as the tools change

He evolved through the computer-preparation era.

Do this: When technology transforms your field, adapt your method rather than clinging to the old way; longevity requires it.
Principle 3

Stay calm and humble at the top

His temperament sustained a 30-year career.

Do this: Composure and humility keep you learning and durable; they're often what separate a long reign from a brief one.

Frequently asked questions

Why is Viswanathan Anand significant?

He was India's first chess grandmaster and a five-time World Chess Champion, rising from a country with little chess infrastructure to the summit of the game and inspiring India's subsequent chess boom.

What made Anand a great champion?

Astonishing calculation speed in his youth, a calm temperament, remarkable adaptability across chess's changing eras including computer preparation, and the longevity to stay elite for over three decades.
