Galileo Galilei

He pointed a telescope at the heavens, trusted what he saw over what he was told — and launched modern science.

4 min read · 1600s

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Galileo Galilei transformed how humans investigate nature by insisting on observation and experiment over inherited authority. Turning an improved telescope to the sky, he discovered Jupiter's moons, the phases of Venus and the craters of the Moon — evidence that supported the Copernican view that the Earth orbits the Sun, not the reverse. He studied motion with careful experiments, laying groundwork Newton would build on, and championed mathematics as the language of nature.

His defence of heliocentrism brought him before the Inquisition, which forced him to recant and placed him under house arrest for the rest of his life. He is a study in trusting evidence over authority, in the experimental method as the engine of knowledge, and in the courage — and cost — of asserting an inconvenient truth.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Trust evidence over authority

Galileo believed what he observed, not what he was told.

Do this: When careful observation contradicts received wisdom, follow the evidence; that shift is the root of all science.
Principle 2

Experiment to find the law

He studied motion through deliberate experiments.

Do this: Design experiments to isolate and measure what you want to understand; nature answers questions you test it with.
Principle 3

Speak truth despite the cost

He defended heliocentrism before a hostile Inquisition.

Do this: Asserting an inconvenient truth can carry real cost; weigh it, but don't let authority alone override evidence.

Frequently asked questions

Why is Galileo called the father of modern science?

He insisted on observation and experiment over inherited authority, used the telescope to gather evidence for heliocentrism, and pioneered the experimental study of motion that Newton would build on.

What happened to Galileo for defending heliocentrism?

The Inquisition tried him, forced him to recant the view that the Earth orbits the Sun, and placed him under house arrest for the rest of his life.
