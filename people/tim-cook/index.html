Tim Cook

The operations master who followed a legend — and made Apple the most valuable company on earth through supply-chain genius.

4 min read · 1998–present

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Tim Cook took over as Apple's CEO in 2011, following the most revered founder in modern business — a succession most thought impossible to survive. Rather than imitate Steve Jobs, Cook played to his own distinctive strength: operational and supply-chain mastery. He had joined Apple in 1998 and transformed its operations, slashing inventory and building a supply chain so precise it became a weapon, letting Apple manufacture at enormous scale with extraordinary efficiency and margins.

Under Cook, Apple grew into the most valuable company in the world, expanding services, wearables, and its installed base into a vast, sticky ecosystem. A calm, disciplined, principled leader, he proved that following a visionary doesn't require being one — that world-class execution, operational excellence, and steady stewardship can create staggering value. As of 2026 he is transitioning the role to hardware chief John Ternus. He is a study in succeeding a legend by playing to your own strengths, and in operations as a genuine competitive weapon.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Follow a legend by being yourself

Cook didn't imitate Jobs; he led through his own operational strength.

Do this: Succeeding a visionary means playing to your own strengths, not copying theirs.
Principle 2

Make operations a weapon

His supply-chain mastery gave Apple scale, efficiency and margin rivals couldn't match.

Do this: Treat operational excellence as a genuine competitive advantage, not back-office plumbing.
Principle 3

Deepen the ecosystem

He grew services and wearables into a sticky, compounding installed base.

Do this: Extend a loyal base into an ecosystem whose switching costs compound over time.

Frequently asked questions

How did Tim Cook succeed Steve Jobs?

By not imitating him — Cook led through his own strength, operational and supply-chain mastery, growing Apple into the most valuable company in the world rather than trying to be a second Jobs.

Is Tim Cook still Apple's CEO?

He led Apple from 2011 and, as of 2026, is transitioning the role to hardware chief John Ternus — after one of the most successful CEO tenures in business history.
