Vince Lombardi

He turned a losing team into champions on a creed of relentless fundamentals, discipline and will.

4 min read · 1950s–1970

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Vince Lombardi took over a struggling Green Bay Packers team in 1959 and built one of the great dynasties in American sport, winning five NFL championships in seven years, including the first two Super Bowls. The Super Bowl trophy is named after him. His coaching was built not on trick plays but on obsessive mastery of fundamentals, physical and mental discipline, and an almost evangelical belief in effort, preparation and the pursuit of excellence.

Lombardi demanded total commitment and preached that the price of success is hard work and dedication. He is a study in winning through fundamentals rather than gimmicks, in discipline and preparation as the foundation of victory, and in the motivational power of an uncompromising standard of excellence.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Master the fundamentals

Lombardi won on basics executed perfectly, not tricks.

Do this: Relentlessly drill the fundamentals; flawless execution of the basics beats clever complexity under pressure.
Principle 2

Demand total commitment

He required full effort and preparation from everyone.

Do this: Set an uncompromising standard of effort and preparation; excellence is a habit demanded, not hoped for.
Principle 3

Preach the price of success

He made clear winning costs hard work and dedication.

Do this: Be honest that success requires sacrifice; people rise to a leader who names the real price and pays it too.

In their words

Winning is not everything, but the will to win is.
The quality of a person's life is in direct proportion to their commitment to excellence.
Perfection is not attainable, but if we chase perfection we can catch excellence.
