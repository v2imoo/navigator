Chuck Feeney

The billionaire who secretly gave away his entire fortune — and died with almost nothing, by design.

4 min read · 1960–2020

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Chuck Feeney co-founded Duty Free Shoppers, the airport-retail giant, and became a billionaire — then spent the rest of his life secretly giving almost all of it away. He pioneered 'Giving While Living': the conviction that the wealthy should donate their fortunes during their lifetimes, where they can direct and see the impact, rather than leaving it to bequests.

Through Atlantic Philanthropies, mostly anonymously for decades, Feeney gave away around $8 billion to universities, health and human rights, deliberately winding the foundation down to zero. He lived famously modestly — no car, no luxury home, flying economy — and achieved his goal of dying with almost nothing left to give.

What he aimed to — and did — leave

Feeney inspired the modern generation of philanthropists, including the Giving Pledge. His life is a radical answer to the question of what wealth is for — and a model of giving with humility and impact rather than for recognition.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Give while living

Feeney gave during his lifetime to direct and see the impact.

Do this: If you intend to give, do it while you can guide it and witness the results.
Principle 2

Detach identity from wealth

He lived modestly and gave mostly anonymously.

Do this: Separate your sense of self from your net worth; wealth is a tool, not a scoreboard.
Principle 3

Give with intention and focus

He directed funds to specific causes for maximum effect.

Do this: Concentrate giving on causes where it can achieve measurable impact.

How much did Chuck Feeney give away?
