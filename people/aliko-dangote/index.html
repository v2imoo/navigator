Aliko Dangote

He bet that Africa should make what it imports — and built cement, sugar and a giant refinery to prove it.

4 min read · 1980s–present

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Aliko Dangote started as a trader in Nigeria and built the Dangote Group into Africa's largest industrial conglomerate, spanning cement, sugar, salt and flour. His defining conviction was import substitution: instead of importing finished goods, Africa should manufacture them locally. He poured capital into cement plants across the continent and, most ambitiously, built one of the world's largest oil refineries in Nigeria — a country that long exported crude only to import refined fuel.

Dangote bet on manufacturing at massive scale in markets others considered too difficult, backing long-horizon industrial projects where quick-return trading was the norm. He is a study in import substitution as a strategy, in the courage to build hard physical infrastructure in tough markets, and in patient, vertically integrated industrialisation.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Make what you import

Dangote's whole strategy was local manufacturing over imports.

Do this: Where a market imports what it could make, building local production is both a business and a nation-building bet.
Principle 2

Build the hard infrastructure

He invested in plants and refineries others avoided.

Do this: The difficult, capital-heavy physical projects others shy from can become the deepest, most defensible businesses.
Principle 3

Take the long horizon

His industrial bets pay off over decades, not quarters.

Do this: Industrial value is built patiently; commit to long-horizon projects when quick-return competitors won't.

What is Dangote's core strategy?
