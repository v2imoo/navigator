Bernie Marcus

The retailer who, fired at 49, built the big-box home-improvement giant on empowering customers and staff.

4 min read · 1978–2002

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Bernie Marcus co-founded The Home Depot at age 49, after being fired from a home-improvement retailer — turning a career setback into the launch of the world's largest home-improvement chain. With Arthur Blank and financier Ken Langone, he built the 'big-box' format: cavernous stores stocking a vast range at low prices, aimed at both do-it-yourselfers and contractors.

Marcus's edge was combining scale-driven low prices with genuine expertise: Home Depot staff were trained to teach customers how to do their projects, turning intimidating home improvement into something ordinary people felt equipped to tackle. That service-plus-price combination, and a strong employee culture, drove explosive growth.

Marcus led Home Depot as CEO through its rapid rise and later became a major philanthropist. His story is a lesson in second acts, and in pairing low-cost scale with the expertise and culture that make customers loyal.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Pair low price with real expertise

Home Depot undercut on price and taught customers how to do the job.

Do this: Combine a price advantage with genuine expertise customers can't get elsewhere.
Principle 2

Empower the customer

Marcus made intimidating projects feel achievable.

Do this: Give customers the knowledge and confidence to succeed with your product.
Principle 3

Turn setbacks into launches

Fired at 49, he built a giant — a classic second act.

Do this: Treat a career setback as the freedom to build something of your own.

In their words

The most important thing is to take care of your people.
Give great service and the sales will follow.

Frequently asked questions

How did Home Depot succeed?

By pioneering the big-box format — huge stores, vast range, low prices — combined with trained staff who taught customers how to do their projects, plus a strong employee culture.

What's notable about Marcus's story?

He co-founded Home Depot at 49 after being fired, a classic second act, and built the world's largest home-improvement chain before becoming a major philanthropist.
