Hayao Miyazaki

The animation master who refused every shortcut — proving that obsessive craft and heart outlast any trend.

4 min read · 1979–present

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Hayao Miyazaki, co-founder of Studio Ghibli, is one of the greatest filmmakers in the history of animation — creator of Spirited Away, My Neighbor Totoro, Princess Mononoke and other works of extraordinary imagination, beauty, and emotional depth that have moved audiences worldwide and won the highest honours. He elevated animation into profound art, with richly drawn worlds, moral complexity, and a humane sensibility rare in the medium.

Miyazaki is famous for an almost fanatical devotion to craft and integrity. He insisted on hand-drawn animation long after the industry turned to computers, personally reviewing countless frames, and refused to compromise his artistic vision for commercial pressure or speed. A perfectionist who cares about getting every detail emotionally true, he built Ghibli as a place where that uncompromising standard could endure. He is a study in craft and integrity as a competitive moat, in building beloved and enduring work, and in refusing shortcuts the market rewards.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Make craft and integrity the moat

Miyazaki's uncompromising quality is what makes Ghibli's work endure.

Do this: Refuse to compromise on craft; uncompromising quality is a moat rivals can't shortcut.
Principle 2

Refuse the market's shortcuts

He kept hand-drawing when the industry chose cheaper computers.

Do this: Don't take the shortcut the market rewards if it betrays the quality that matters.
Principle 3

Build work that endures

He cares about emotional truth that lasts, not trends.

Do this: Aim for work true and deep enough to be beloved for generations, not just a season.

Frequently asked questions

Why is Hayao Miyazaki so revered?

As co-founder of Studio Ghibli and creator of Spirited Away and other masterpieces, he elevated animation into profound art — with extraordinary imagination, beauty, and emotional depth — through fanatical devotion to craft.

What's Miyazaki's lesson for builders?

Craft and integrity as a competitive moat — refusing the shortcuts the market rewards (keeping hand-drawn animation over cheaper computers) to build work true and beloved enough to endure for generations.
