Ray Dalio

The founder of the world's largest hedge fund, who turned decision-making itself into written principles.

4 min read · 1975–present

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Ray Dalio built Bridgewater Associates into the world's largest hedge fund by treating investing — and management — as systems that could be understood, written down as principles, and improved. He sought the timeless cause-and-effect relationships that drive markets and economies, and encoded his decision rules so they could be tested and followed consistently rather than driven by emotion.

Inside Bridgewater he built a culture of 'radical transparency' and 'idea meritocracy', where the best argument wins regardless of hierarchy and everyone's reasoning is open to challenge. His book Principles laid out this approach and made him one of the most widely-read thinkers on decision-making.

Dalio stepped back from control of Bridgewater in 2022. His enduring contribution is the idea that you can systematise good judgement — turning hard-won lessons into explicit principles that compound over time.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Write down your principles

Dalio turned lessons into explicit rules he could test and reuse.

Do this: Codify your decision rules so they can be examined, tested and improved.
Principle 2

Seek cause-and-effect

He looked for the timeless relationships driving markets, not one-off tips.

Do this: Understand the underlying mechanisms, not just the surface outcomes.
Principle 3

Build an idea meritocracy

At Bridgewater the best argument wins, regardless of rank.

Do this: Let the strongest reasoning win, not the most senior person.

In their words

Frequently asked questions

What is Ray Dalio known for?

Founding Bridgewater Associates, the world's largest hedge fund, and for systematising decision-making into written 'principles' he shared in his best-selling book.

What is an 'idea meritocracy'?

Dalio's cultural model where the best-reasoned argument wins regardless of hierarchy, supported by radical transparency about everyone's thinking.
