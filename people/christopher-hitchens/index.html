Christopher Hitchens

He prized following an argument over loyalty to any side — a brilliant, combative writer who feared no sacred cow.

Part I — The story

The story

Christopher Hitchens was one of the most brilliant, combative and controversial essayists and public intellectuals of his era — a dazzling writer and debater who prized following an argument wherever it led over loyalty to any political tribe. He moved from the left and repeatedly broke with allies, took on revered figures, and became a leading voice of the 'New Atheism' with his book God Is Not Great. He was as admired for his wit and erudition as he was fiercely opposed on many of his positions.

Facing terminal cancer, he wrote unflinchingly about his own dying. In fair presentation: celebrated for his fearless independence and prose, contested for many of his stances (including his support for the Iraq war). He is a study in independent thought over tribal loyalty, in argument as a discipline, and in intellectual courage held to the very end.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Follow the argument, not the tribe

Hitchens broke with his own side when reason led elsewhere.

Do this: Loyalty to a conclusion over a tribe is intellectual integrity; follow the argument even when it costs you allies.
Principle 2

Fear no sacred cow

He took on revered figures and ideas.

Do this: Reserve nothing from scrutiny; the ideas and people a culture protects from criticism are exactly those worth examining.
Principle 3

Hold your courage to the end

He wrote clear-eyed about his own death.

Do this: Intellectual honesty is tested most at the hardest moments; the discipline of clear thought is worth keeping to the last.
