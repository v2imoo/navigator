Alan Mulally

He brought an aerospace engineer's discipline to Ford — and steered it through the crisis without taking a bailout.

4 min read · 2000s–2010s

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Alan Mulally spent his career at Boeing, leading its commercial-airplanes division through the post-9/11 crisis, before becoming CEO of a deeply troubled Ford in 2006. He borrowed heavily before the financial crisis hit — a prescient move that let Ford survive without the government bailout its Detroit rivals needed. He unified the company under 'One Ford', simplified a sprawling product line, and instilled a culture of radical transparency through his weekly Business Plan Review, where leaders had to show the true colour-coded status of their work.

Mulally's gift was making it safe to surface problems: when an executive first marked a project 'red', he applauded rather than punished, breaking a culture of hiding bad news. He is a study in transparency as a management tool, in simplifying a bloated organisation around one plan, and in the outsider-engineer's disciplined approach to a turnaround.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Make it safe to surface problems

Mulally applauded the first honest 'red' status.

Do this: People hide bad news when it's punished; reward honesty about problems so you see them while they're still fixable.
Principle 2

Unify around one plan

'One Ford' aligned a sprawling, fragmented company.

Do this: Replace competing agendas with a single, shared plan everyone can see and rally behind.
Principle 3

Prepare before the storm

He raised cash before the crisis hit.

Do this: Build your buffer while times are good; the capital or slack you secure early is what carries you through the crisis.

Frequently asked questions

Who is Alan Mulally?

The aerospace executive who, as CEO of Ford from 2006, led one of the great corporate turnarounds — steering Ford through the financial crisis without a government bailout via his 'One Ford' plan and a culture of radical transparency.

What was Mulally's management approach?

Radical transparency through a weekly Business Plan Review where leaders showed the honest colour-coded status of their work, plus unifying the company around a single shared plan and preparing financially before the crisis.
