J. Paul Getty

The oilman who became the world's richest private citizen — and famously frugal with it.

4 min read · 1914–1976

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

J. Paul Getty built one of the great oil fortunes of the twentieth century and, for a time, was reputed to be the richest private citizen in the world. His decisive move was securing oil concessions in the Middle East — particularly the Neutral Zone between Saudi Arabia and Kuwait — gambles that paid off enormously when the wells came in.

Getty combined bold, patient risk-taking on exploration with famous, sometimes extreme, personal frugality — he was said to have installed a payphone for guests at his English estate. He built a vertically integrated oil company and amassed an art collection that became the foundation of the Getty museums.

Getty's career shows the outsized payoff of patient, well-judged risk on scarce resources — and, in his personal contradictions, the odd relationship great wealth can have with the person who holds it.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Take bold risks on scarce resources

Getty's Middle East concessions were huge gambles that paid off.

Do this: Concentrate bold bets where a scarce resource could deliver outsized returns.
Principle 2

Integrate to control the value chain

He built a vertically integrated oil operation.

Do this: Own the chain from extraction to sale to capture and protect margin.
Principle 3

Be patient with big bets

His concessions took years to pay off spectacularly.

Do this: Give high-conviction resource bets the time they need to mature.

Frequently asked questions

How did J. Paul Getty make his fortune?

Chiefly through bold, patient bets on Middle Eastern oil concessions — notably the Neutral Zone — combined with a vertically integrated oil company.

What was Getty known for personally?

Extreme frugality despite immense wealth, and a great art collection that became the foundation of the Getty museums.
