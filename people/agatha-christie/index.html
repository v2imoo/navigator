Agatha Christie

The 'Queen of Crime' built the perfect puzzle-mystery — and became the best-selling novelist in history.

4 min read · 1920s–1970s

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Agatha Christie is, by most counts, the best-selling novelist of all time, with billions of copies sold across 66 detective novels and many short-story collections. She created two of fiction's most beloved detectives — the fastidious Belgian Hercule Poirot and the sharp spinster Miss Marple — and perfected the 'fair-play' puzzle mystery, in which the reader is given every clue needed to solve the crime. Her play The Mousetrap is the longest-running stage production in history.

Christie's genius was ingenious, tightly engineered plots with surprising yet logical twists, delivered with crisp readability. She is a study in mastering the mechanics of a genre, in the craft of the satisfying twist, and in the enormous reach of work that respects and delights its audience.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Master your genre's mechanics

Christie engineered the perfect fair-play mystery.

Do this: Deeply understand the rules and machinery of your form; mastery of craft is what lets you delight an audience reliably.
Principle 2

Play fair with your audience

She gave readers every clue to solve the puzzle.

Do this: Respect your audience's intelligence; a satisfying payoff is one they could, in principle, have reached themselves.
Principle 3

Engineer the surprising-but-logical twist

Her endings shocked yet made perfect sense.

Do this: The best twist feels inevitable in hindsight; plant the logic early so the surprise lands as truth, not trickery.

Frequently asked questions

Why is Agatha Christie significant?

She is the best-selling novelist of all time, with billions of copies sold, creator of Hercule Poirot and Miss Marple and master of the 'fair-play' puzzle mystery; her play The Mousetrap is the longest-running ever.

What is a 'fair-play' mystery?

A detective story, perfected by Christie, in which the reader is given every clue needed to solve the crime themselves — so the surprising solution is also perfectly logical in hindsight.
