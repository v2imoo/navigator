Stéphane Bancel

He bet a decade of his career on an unproven idea — and when the pandemic came, mRNA was ready.

4 min read · 2010s–present

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Stéphane Bancel left a secure career to become CEO of Moderna in 2011, when it was a tiny, unproven company betting everything on mRNA — the then-radical idea of using messenger RNA to instruct the body to make its own medicines. For years Moderna had no approved products and plenty of sceptics, as Bancel poured capital and talent into building the platform and manufacturing. When COVID-19 struck, that decade of preparation let Moderna design a vaccine in days and deliver it at scale.

Bancel's bet exemplifies building a capability before the moment that needs it. He is a study in long-horizon conviction on an unproven technology, in the value of building platforms and preparedness ahead of demand, and in the patience to endure years of doubt before vindication.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Build the capability before it's needed

Moderna's platform was ready when the pandemic hit.

Do this: Invest in deep capabilities ahead of the moment that demands them; preparedness turns a crisis into your opportunity.
Principle 2

Hold conviction on the unproven

He backed mRNA through years of doubt.

Do this: If the evidence and upside justify it, sustain conviction on an unproven technology through the long, sceptical years.
Principle 3

Pour into the platform, not just a product

He built a reusable mRNA platform.

Do this: A platform that can produce many products is worth more than any single one; invest in the engine, not just the output.

In their words

We were building for a moment we couldn't predict but knew would come.
Platforms beat products.
Conviction is what carries you through the years no one believes.
