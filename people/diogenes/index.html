Diogenes

He lived in a barrel, owned almost nothing, and mocked every convention — telling Alexander the Great to get out of his light.

4 min read · 4th century BC

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Diogenes of Sinope was the most famous of the Cynic philosophers, who taught that happiness comes from living simply and virtuously in accordance with nature, free of the artificial wants and conventions of society. He practised what he preached to an extreme — reportedly living in a large ceramic jar, owning almost nothing, begging for food, and using outrageous public behaviour to expose the hypocrisy and vanity of those around him. When Alexander the Great, the most powerful man alive, offered to grant him any wish, Diogenes reportedly replied only: 'Stand out of my light.'

He used shock and wit to puncture pretension and provoke thought. He is a study in radical freedom through wanting little, in questioning every social convention, and in the power of living your philosophy rather than merely professing it.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Find freedom in wanting little

Diogenes owned almost nothing and answered to no one.

Do this: The fewer artificial wants you have, the freer you are; reducing your needs can be the most radical independence.
Principle 2

Question every convention

He mocked the social norms others obeyed unthinkingly.

Do this: Examine the conventions everyone follows without thought; many exist only to be questioned.
Principle 3

Live your philosophy, don't just profess it

He embodied his ideas to an extreme.

Do this: Ideas carry weight when you actually live them; the example of a life is more persuasive than any argument.

Frequently asked questions

Who was Diogenes?

The most famous of the Cynic philosophers of ancient Greece, who taught that happiness comes from living simply and virtuously in accordance with nature — practising it to an extreme by owning almost nothing and mocking social convention.

What is the Alexander the Great story?

When Alexander, the most powerful man alive, offered to grant Diogenes any wish, the philosopher — unimpressed by power — reportedly asked only that Alexander 'stand out of my light'.
