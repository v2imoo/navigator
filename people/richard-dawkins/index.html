Richard Dawkins

He reframed evolution from the gene's point of view — and coined the word 'meme' decades before the internet.

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Richard Dawkins transformed how we understand evolution with his 1976 book The Selfish Gene, which reframed natural selection from the gene's point of view: organisms are, in a sense, vehicles that genes build to copy themselves. The idea was illuminating and provocative, and the same book coined 'meme' — a unit of cultural transmission that spreads and evolves like a gene, a word that later defined the internet age. A gifted writer, he became one of the most influential popularisers of science.

He is also a prominent and highly controversial public atheist, whose outspoken views on religion have drawn both admiration and sharp criticism. In fair presentation: celebrated as a lucid science communicator, contested for his polemical stance on religion. He is a study in a reframing that changes how a field thinks, in the power of a vivid metaphor, and in the double edge of an outspoken public intellectual.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Reframe to reveal

Dawkins's gene's-eye view illuminated evolution anew.

Do this: A fresh point of view on familiar facts can reveal what no new data would; reframing is a form of discovery.
Principle 2

Coin the vivid concept

His 'meme' gave the world a lasting idea.

Do this: A sharp, nameable concept can outlive its origin and reshape how people think; naming an idea well is powerful.
Principle 3

Expect a double-edged profile

His outspokenness drew admiration and criticism alike.

Do this: A forceful public stance wins devoted followers and sharp critics both; count the cost of speaking out before you do.
