Part I — The story

The story

Philip Kotler took marketing — then seen as little more than advertising and selling — and rebuilt it as a rigorous management discipline grounded in understanding and serving customer needs. His textbook Marketing Management, first published in 1967 and revised across many editions, became the field's defining text, teaching generations that marketing begins not with a product to push but with a customer need to meet, and systematised ideas like segmentation, targeting and positioning (STP) and the marketing mix.

Kotler extended marketing thinking far beyond business — into social causes, non-profits and nations. He is a study in elevating a practical craft into a teachable science, in putting the customer's need at the centre of every commercial decision, and in the enduring influence of writing the definitive text that organises a whole field.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Start with the customer's need

Kotler taught that marketing begins with need, not product.

Do this: Before building or promoting anything, define the customer need you're actually serving.
Principle 2

Segment, target, position

His STP framework structures who you serve and how you're seen.

Do this: Divide the market, choose the segment you can serve best, then define a clear position in their mind.
Principle 3

Systematise the craft

He turned scattered practice into a teachable discipline.

Do this: Organise what you know into a clear framework; structure is what makes expertise transferable.

In their words

Frequently asked questions

Why is Kotler called the father of modern marketing?

He transformed marketing from mere selling and advertising into a rigorous, customer-centred management science, and his textbook Marketing Management defined the field for generations.

What is STP?

Kotler's framework of Segmentation, Targeting and Positioning — divide the market, choose the segment you serve best, and establish a clear position in customers' minds.
