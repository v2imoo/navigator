Pierre Omidyar

The programmer who built a marketplace on a radical bet: that strangers, given the right system, would mostly trust each other.

4 min read · 1995–present

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Pierre Omidyar founded eBay in 1995 as a simple online auction site — reportedly the first item sold was a broken laser pointer — and it grew into one of the defining companies of the early internet, pioneering the online marketplace. His profound insight was about human nature and system design: that most people are basically trustworthy, and that with the right reputation system, strangers across the world would buy and sell with each other sight unseen.

eBay's feedback and rating system — letting buyers and sellers rate each other — solved the trust problem that made peer-to-peer commerce possible, and became the template for countless marketplaces since. The business was powered by a two-sided network effect: more buyers attracted more sellers, which attracted more buyers. Omidyar is a study in designing systems that harness trust at scale, and in the power of the marketplace model and its network effects — insights that underpin much of the modern internet economy.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Design systems that harness trust

Omidyar's reputation system let strangers transact safely at scale.

Do this: Build the system that makes trust possible, and peer-to-peer commerce follows.
Principle 2

Harness the two-sided network effect

More buyers drew sellers, which drew more buyers — a compounding loop.

Do this: Design the marketplace loop where each side draws the other, and protect it.
Principle 3

Bet on people's basic good faith

He built on the premise that most people are trustworthy.

Do this: Design for the trustworthy majority while guarding against the dishonest few.

In their words

People are basically good.
If you give people the benefit of the doubt, they will usually rise to the occasion.
