Swami Vivekananda

His 'Sisters and brothers of America' speech in 1893 introduced Vedanta and yoga to the West — and reawakened India.

4 min read · 1880s–1902

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Swami Vivekananda, a disciple of the mystic Ramakrishna, became the figure who introduced the Indian philosophies of Vedanta and yoga to the Western world, most famously at the 1893 Parliament of the World's Religions in Chicago, where his address beginning 'Sisters and brothers of America' drew a standing ovation and made him a sensation. He preached a muscular, confident spirituality — service to humanity as worship, the divinity within every person, and strength over weakness.

He founded the Ramakrishna Mission to combine spiritual practice with social service, and his call to self-belief and national pride helped reawaken a colonised India. He is a study in carrying your culture's deepest ideas to the world, in a spirituality of strength and service, and in inspiring the self-confidence of a people.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Carry your ideas to the world

Vivekananda took Vedanta to a global stage.

Do this: Your culture's deepest ideas can resonate far beyond home; have the confidence to carry them to the world.
Principle 2

Pair the spiritual with service

His mission joined practice to social work.

Do this: Inner development and outer service reinforce each other; the highest ideals express themselves as help to others.
Principle 3

Preach strength, not weakness

He called for self-belief over timidity.

Do this: Inspire people to their own strength and divinity; confidence and self-belief unlock what resignation never can.

In their words

Arise, awake, and stop not till the goal is reached.
Take up one idea. Make that one idea your life.
You cannot believe in God until you believe in yourself.

Who was Swami Vivekananda?

A disciple of Ramakrishna who introduced Vedanta and yoga to the West — famously at the 1893 Parliament of World's Religions in Chicago — founded the Ramakrishna Mission, and helped reawaken the self-confidence of colonised India.

What was Vivekananda's message?

A spirituality of strength and service — the divinity within every person, service to humanity as worship, and a call to self-belief over weakness and timidity.
