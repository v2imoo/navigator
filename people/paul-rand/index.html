Paul Rand

He created the logos of IBM, ABC and UPS — and taught that a great mark is simple, memorable, and timeless.

4 min read · 1940s–1990s

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Paul Rand brought European modernism to American graphic design and essentially invented the modern practice of corporate identity. He designed some of the most enduring logos in history — IBM, ABC, UPS, Westinghouse — and late in life, the logo for Steve Jobs's NeXT, famously presenting a single take-it-or-leave-it solution with a booklet explaining his reasoning. His marks are models of simplicity, wit and longevity.

Rand argued that a logo's job is not to describe a business but to identify it — to be simple, distinctive and memorable enough to work across every context and endure for decades. He is a study in the discipline of simplicity, in designing for timelessness over fashion, and in the confidence to present one well-reasoned solution rather than a menu of options.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Identify, don't describe

Rand said a logo should identify a company, not explain it.

Do this: A mark's job is recognition, not description; aim for distinctive and memorable, not literal.
Principle 2

Design for timelessness

His logos were built to outlast trends.

Do this: Favour simple, enduring solutions over fashionable ones; the best identity still works in fifty years.
Principle 3

Present one reasoned solution

He gave clients a single answer with his rationale.

Do this: Do the thinking, then present your best solution with confidence and reasoning rather than hedging with options.

In their words

Design is the silent ambassador of your brand.
Don't try to be original; just try to be good.
Simplicity is not the goal. It is the by-product of a good idea.

Frequently asked questions

Who was Paul Rand?

The American graphic designer who essentially invented modern corporate identity, creating enduring logos for IBM, ABC, UPS and NeXT, and bringing European modernism to American design.

What did Paul Rand believe about logos?

That a logo should identify rather than describe a company — simple, distinctive, memorable and timeless enough to work across every context and endure for decades.
