Charlie Munger

Buffett's partner and intellectual foil, who turned a latticework of mental models into an investing philosophy.

4 min read · 1962–2023

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Charlie Munger was Warren Buffett's business partner for over half a century and the intellectual force who reshaped Buffett's investing from bargain-hunting toward buying wonderful businesses at fair prices. A lawyer turned investor, Munger brought a ferocious, multidisciplinary mind and a bluntness that made him one of the most quoted thinkers in business.

His central idea was the 'latticework of mental models': that to think well you must carry the big ideas from many disciplines — psychology, economics, physics, biology — and use them together. He championed inversion (solve problems backward), studying the causes of human misjudgement, and staying rigorously within your circle of competence.

Much of Navigator's mental-models library descends from thinkers like Munger. His insistence that avoiding stupidity beats seeking brilliance, and that incentives and biases explain most behaviour, remains among the most practical wisdom in business.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Build a latticework of models

Munger drew on many disciplines so the right idea surfaced for each problem.

Do this: Learn the big models from several fields and use them together.
Principle 2

Invert the problem

He solved forward problems backward — study how to fail, then avoid it.

Do this: Ask how you'd guarantee the bad outcome, then steer clear of those causes.
Principle 3

Avoid stupidity over seeking brilliance

Munger stressed that dodging big errors beats chasing cleverness.

Do this: Focus first on not being stupid; consistency beats occasional brilliance.

In their words

Invert, always invert.
It is remarkable how much long-term advantage people like us have gotten by trying to be consistently not stupid.
Show me the incentive and I will show you the outcome.

Frequently asked questions

Who was Charlie Munger?

Warren Buffett's business partner and vice-chairman of Berkshire Hathaway for over fifty years, and a hugely influential thinker on mental models and rational decision-making.

What is the 'latticework of mental models'?

Munger's idea that clear thinking requires carrying the big ideas from many disciplines and using them together, rather than relying on one field's tools.
