Ilya Sutskever

His work lit the spark of modern deep learning — and he now leads a lab devoted solely to safe superintelligence.

4 min read · 2010s–present

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Ilya Sutskever is one of the central figures of the deep-learning revolution. As a student of Geoffrey Hinton, he co-created AlexNet in 2012 — the neural network whose stunning image-recognition results convinced the world that deep learning worked. He went on to co-found OpenAI in 2015 and served as its chief scientist, guiding the research that produced the GPT family of large language models.

In 2024 he left OpenAI to co-found Safe Superintelligence (SSI), a lab with a single, narrow mission: building a safe superintelligence and nothing else, insulated from commercial pressure; he became its CEO in 2025. Sutskever is known for an almost mystical conviction about scale and the trajectory of intelligence. He is a study in the leverage of a foundational technical breakthrough, in following a research conviction across institutions, and in narrowing a mission to its essence.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Prove the breakthrough undeniably

AlexNet's results silenced doubt about deep learning.

Do this: A single, decisive demonstration can convert a sceptical field faster than years of argument.
Principle 2

Follow the research conviction

Sutskever has moved institutions to pursue his view of intelligence.

Do this: Let your deepest technical conviction guide where you work, even when it means leaving success behind.
Principle 3

Narrow the mission to its essence

SSI does one thing: safe superintelligence.

Do this: Stripping an organisation to a single goal removes distraction and sharpens everything toward it.

In their words

If you value intelligence above all other human qualities, you're going to have a bad time.
The models just want to learn.
