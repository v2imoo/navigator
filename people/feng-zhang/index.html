Feng Zhang

He helped turn gene-editing from a bacterial curiosity into a working tool inside human cells.

4 min read · 2010s–present

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Feng Zhang, who immigrated to the United States as a child, became one of the leading figures in making CRISPR gene-editing work in the cells of plants and animals, including humans — a crucial step in turning the technology into a practical tool for medicine and research. A professor at MIT and the Broad Institute, he was also a co-developer of optogenetics, a method to control brain cells with light, and has co-founded several biotech companies.

Zhang is a prolific scientist-inventor who moves fast from discovery to application. He is a study in translating a breakthrough into a working technology, in the intense, fast-moving frontier of modern biotech, and in bridging academic science and the companies that bring it to patients.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Make the breakthrough work in practice

Zhang got CRISPR working in human cells.

Do this: Proving a technology works in the real, hard setting (not just in principle) is where much of the value is created.
Principle 2

Move fast from discovery to application

He translates science quickly into tools.

Do this: Speed in turning a discovery into something usable matters; the frontier rewards those who apply fast and carefully.
Principle 3

Bridge lab and company

He co-founds firms to bring science to patients.

Do this: Academic discovery needs a commercial vehicle to reach people; build or partner with the companies that carry it.

In their words

Why was Zhang's CRISPR work important?
