Alexander Hamilton

The immigrant orphan who built the financial architecture of a new nation from scratch.

4 min read · 1777–1804

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Alexander Hamilton rose from an impoverished, illegitimate orphan in the Caribbean to become one of the most consequential of America's Founding Fathers — the person who designed the financial architecture of the new United States. As the first Treasury Secretary, he built the systems of national credit, banking and industry that turned a bankrupt collection of former colonies into a viable economic power.

Hamilton's genius was systems design: he understood that a nation needed sound public credit, a central bank, and a manufacturing base, and he built the institutions to create them, often against fierce opposition. Astonishingly prolific and driven, he wrote much of the Federalist Papers and shaped the Constitution's interpretation. His life is a study in building enduring institutions from first principles — and in the raw ambition and industry that carried him from nothing to the centre of a nation's founding.
