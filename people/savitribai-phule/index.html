Savitribai Phule

She became India's first woman teacher and opened schools for girls and the oppressed — against ferocious resistance.

4 min read · 1840s–1897

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Savitribai Phule, with her husband Jyotirao, pioneered education for girls and oppressed-caste communities in 19th-century India, when both were forbidden them. She became one of India's first women teachers and ran some of the earliest schools for girls, enduring stones, dung and abuse thrown at her on the way to class by those who opposed educating women and lower castes. She also campaigned against child marriage, for widow remarriage, and opened a home for pregnant widows and a well for 'untouchables'.

A poet and relentless reformer, she laid foundations for women's education and social justice in India. She is a study in pioneering against ferocious social resistance, in education as the lever of liberation, and in persistence through daily humiliation for a cause whose time had not yet come.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Use education as the lever

Phule saw schooling as the route to liberation.

Do this: Education is among the most powerful levers for lifting the excluded; opening it to the shut-out changes everything downstream.
Principle 2

Persist through daily humiliation

She faced abuse on every walk to school.

Do this: Pioneering a cause ahead of its time means enduring sustained hostility; steady persistence is the price of being first.
Principle 3

Attack injustice on many fronts

She fought caste, child marriage and more together.

Do this: Deep social change needs pressure across linked injustices at once, not a single narrow campaign.

Who was Savitribai Phule?

A 19th-century Indian reformer who, with her husband Jyotirao, became one of India's first woman teachers and pioneered schools for girls and oppressed castes, enduring fierce abuse to do so.

What did Savitribai Phule fight for?

Education for girls and lower-caste communities, and against child marriage and for widow remarriage — using schooling as the lever of liberation against entrenched caste and gender injustice.
