R. K. Narayan

He found the whole of human comedy in one small South Indian town — written in prose of deceptive simplicity.

4 min read · 1930s–2001

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

R. K. Narayan was one of the pioneers of Indian literature in English, who created the fictional South Indian town of Malgudi and, across decades of novels and stories, populated it with ordinary people whose small lives revealed the whole range of human comedy and feeling. Encouraged early by Graham Greene, he wrote in a deceptively simple, gentle, ironic style that made the local universal — Swami and Friends, The Guide, Malgudi Days.

Narayan showed that profound literature could be made from the everyday life of ordinary Indians, without exoticism or grandeur. He is a study in finding the universal in the small and local, in the power of simple, unadorned prose, and in creating a complete fictional world from close observation of ordinary life.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Find the universal in the local

Narayan made one small town speak to the world.

Do this: You don't need grand settings; closely observed ordinary life, rendered truly, becomes universal.
Principle 2

Write with simple, clear prose

His gentle, unadorned style was his strength.

Do this: Simplicity is not lack of skill; clear, plain prose can carry more feeling and truth than ornate writing.
Principle 3

Build a world from observation

Malgudi grew from close attention to real life.

Do this: A rich fictional world can be built not from invention alone but from patient observation of the life around you.
