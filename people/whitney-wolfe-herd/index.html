Whitney Wolfe Herd

The founder who rebuilt dating around women making the first move — and took the company public.

4 min read · 2014–present

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Whitney Wolfe Herd founded Bumble on a single product decision that became its identity: on Bumble, women message first. Having co-founded Tinder and then left amid a high-profile dispute, she built a dating app designed to give women more control and a better experience — turning a pointed critique of the category into a differentiated product.

That 'women make the first move' mechanic was both a values statement and a smart product choice, shaping the network's culture and setting Bumble apart in a crowded market. Wolfe Herd took the company public in 2021, becoming one of the youngest women to lead a company to IPO. She stepped down as CEO in 2023, served as executive chair, and returned as CEO in March 2025.
