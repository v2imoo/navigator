Liang Wenfeng

A quant-fund founder who built a small Chinese lab that shocked the world by matching the giants for a fraction of the cost.

4 min read · 2010s–present

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Liang Wenfeng built his first fortune running High-Flyer, a quantitative hedge fund using AI to trade markets, which gave him both capital and a large cluster of chips. In 2023 he founded DeepSeek, a research lab that, in early 2025, stunned the AI world by releasing open models that rivalled the best from far larger, better-funded Western labs — reportedly trained at a small fraction of the usual cost. It forced a global reassessment of how much money and hardware state-of-the-art AI really requires.

Liang emphasised efficiency, open release and fundamental research over brute-force spending, drawing talent with a culture of curiosity rather than just pay. He is a study in doing more with less through engineering ingenuity, in the disruptive power of open models, and in challenging the assumption that only the richest can reach the frontier.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Do more with less

DeepSeek matched giants on a fraction of the budget.

Do this: Constraints breed ingenuity; a resource disadvantage can force the engineering creativity that becomes your edge.
Principle 2

Challenge the cost assumption

He questioned that the frontier requires the biggest budget.

Do this: Test the industry's assumption that only massive spending wins; efficiency can be a disruptive strategy.
Principle 3

Compete with openness

Releasing open models built reach and credibility fast.

Do this: Open release can win mindshare and momentum against incumbents guarding closed systems.

In their words

Innovation is expensive and wasteful, but real innovation needs the freedom to waste.
