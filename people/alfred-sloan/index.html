Alfred P. Sloan

The executive who out-organised Ford — inventing the decentralised corporation and the car for every purse and purpose.

4 min read · 1923–1956

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Alfred Sloan took a sprawling, chaotic General Motors and, through organisational genius, turned it into the company that overtook Ford to dominate the automobile industry for decades. Where Henry Ford offered one car in one colour, Sloan offered 'a car for every purse and purpose' — a ladder of brands from Chevrolet to Cadillac that let customers trade up as they prospered.

His deeper innovation was managerial: he created the decentralised, multi-division corporation, with autonomous operating divisions coordinated by a central office setting strategy and financial controls. This structure — letting big organisations be both large and responsive — became the template for the modern corporation and was studied by business schools for generations.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Segment the market

Sloan's brand ladder let customers trade up over their lives.

Do this: Offer a range that meets customers at every stage and lets them move up.
Principle 2

Decentralise with central control

Autonomous divisions plus a coordinating centre made GM big yet responsive.

Do this: Give units autonomy to act, with a centre setting strategy and financial discipline.
Principle 3

Manage by numbers

Sloan pioneered financial controls to steer a vast enterprise.

Do this: Use clear financial metrics to coordinate a large, decentralised organisation.

Frequently asked questions

How did Sloan's GM overtake Ford?

With 'a car for every purse and purpose' — a ladder of brands letting customers trade up — while Ford stuck to one model, and by inventing the decentralised, multi-division corporate structure.

Why is Alfred Sloan important to management?

He created the modern decentralised corporation — autonomous divisions coordinated by a central office with financial controls — the template studied by business schools for generations.
