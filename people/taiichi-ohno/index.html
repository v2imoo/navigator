Taiichi Ohno

The engineer who built the system behind Toyota — and taught the world to wage war on waste.

4 min read · 1943–1990

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Taiichi Ohno was the Toyota engineer who, over decades, architected the Toyota Production System — the manufacturing method that became the model for 'lean' production studied and copied by industries worldwide. Building on the ideas of Sakichi Toyoda and the realities of resource-scarce postwar Japan, he created a system of relentless efficiency that let Toyota out-compete far larger, better-resourced Western rivals.

Ohno's central obsession was the elimination of waste ('muda') — anything that doesn't add value for the customer, from excess inventory to waiting to overproduction. He pioneered 'just-in-time' production (make only what's needed, when it's needed), the pull system, and a culture where any worker could stop the line to fix a problem at its source. He championed going to see for yourself ('genchi genbutsu') and asking 'why' five times to reach root causes. He is a study in the relentless elimination of waste, in just-in-time systems, and in continuous, ground-level improvement.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Wage war on waste

Ohno relentlessly eliminated anything that didn't add customer value.

Do this: Hunt down and eliminate waste — excess inventory, waiting, overproduction — relentlessly.
Principle 2

Make only what's needed, when needed

Just-in-time slashed inventory and exposed problems.

Do this: Pull work through the system on demand rather than pushing and stockpiling.
Principle 3

Go and see for yourself

He insisted on observing problems at the source, not from reports.

Do this: Go to where the work happens and see the problem yourself; don't manage from reports.

In their words

Costs do not exist to be calculated. Costs exist to be reduced.
Having no problems is the biggest problem of all.
Where there is no standard, there can be no improvement.

Frequently asked questions

What did Taiichi Ohno create?

The Toyota Production System — the manufacturing method that became the global model for 'lean' production — built on relentless elimination of waste, just-in-time production, and continuous ground-level improvement.

What is 'just-in-time' production?

Ohno's principle of making only what's needed, when it's needed, in the amount needed — slashing inventory, exposing problems quickly, and pulling work through the system on demand.
