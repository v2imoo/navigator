Pelé

The boy from poverty who became football's first global icon — and the embodiment of joy in sport.

4 min read · 1956–1977

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Pelé rose from a childhood of poverty in Brazil — practising with a sock stuffed with rags because he could not afford a ball — to become the most famous footballer in history and the only player ever to win three World Cups. His extraordinary skill, vision, and prolific scoring made him Brazil's greatest sporting hero, but his larger achievement was helping transform football into a truly global game and becoming its first worldwide icon.

Pelé combined supreme talent with an infectious joy and sportsmanship that made him beloved far beyond Brazil, and later, by joining the New York Cosmos, he did more than anyone to popularise the sport in the United States. He turned his fame into a lifelong role as football's global ambassador. He is a study in rising from nothing through sheer talent and dedication, in embodying joy and sportsmanship as part of greatness, and in a single figure's power to carry a sport to the world.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Rise from nothing through dedication

Pelé went from a rag ball in poverty to the world's greatest player.

Do this: Talent plus relentless dedication can carry you from the hardest start to the top.
Principle 2

Embody joy and sportsmanship

His infectious joy made him beloved far beyond his talent.

Do this: How you carry yourself — joy, grace, sportsmanship — is part of lasting greatness.
Principle 3

Carry your field to the world

He became football's global ambassador, growing the whole sport.

Do this: Use your standing to grow the whole field, not just your own legend.

Frequently asked questions

Why is Pelé so revered in football?

He is the only player to win three World Cups, Brazil's greatest sporting hero, whose skill and prolific scoring — combined with infectious joy — made him the sport's first truly global icon.

How did Pelé grow the sport?

Through his worldwide fame and later by joining the New York Cosmos, doing more than anyone to popularise football in the United States, and serving as the game's lifelong global ambassador.
