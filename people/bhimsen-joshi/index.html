Bhimsen Joshi

He ran away from home as a boy to find a guru — and became the towering voice of Indian classical music.

4 min read · 1940s–2011

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Bhimsen Joshi was one of the greatest Hindustani classical vocalists India has produced. As a boy, so captivated by music that he ran away from home in search of a guru who could teach him, he underwent years of rigorous traditional training (gurukul) before emerging as a master of the Kirana gharana, renowned for his powerful voice, breath control and deeply emotive, improvisational renderings of ragas. He brought classical music to vast audiences and received the Bharat Ratna, India's highest civilian honour.

His art rested on extraordinary discipline and devotion to his guru and craft. He is a study in single-minded devotion to a calling, in the years of rigorous apprenticeship behind apparent genius, and in carrying a demanding classical tradition to a wide public.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Devote yourself single-mindedly

Joshi ran away as a child to pursue music.

Do this: A calling pursued with total devotion, at real cost, is what produces mastery; half-commitment rarely reaches the heights.
Principle 2

Honour the apprenticeship

Years of gurukul training preceded his genius.

Do this: Behind apparent genius lies years of rigorous apprenticeship; submit to the discipline before expecting mastery.
Principle 3

Carry the demanding tradition to many

He brought classical music to vast audiences.

Do this: You can take a demanding art to a wide public without cheapening it; mastery itself draws people in.
