Jyotirao Phule

With his wife Savitribai he opened India's first schools for girls and lower castes — and named caste injustice head-on.

4 min read · 1840s–1890

Part I — The story

The story

Jyotirao Phule was one of India's most radical and important social reformers, who with his wife Savitribai opened some of the country's first schools for girls and for children of oppressed castes, at a time when both were forbidden education. He attacked the caste system and Brahminical dominance directly, campaigned against untouchability and for widow remarriage and women's rights, and founded the Satyashodhak Samaj ('Society of Truth-Seekers') to pursue social equality and justice for the marginalised.

His writing, including Gulamgiri ('Slavery'), named caste oppression bluntly and influenced later leaders like Ambedkar. He is a study in confronting entrenched hierarchy directly, in education as the weapon against oppression, and in building institutions and a partnership dedicated to equality.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Name the injustice directly

Phule attacked caste oppression bluntly.

Do this: Don't soften or euphemise injustice; naming it plainly is the first step to dismantling it.
Principle 2

Wield education against oppression

He opened schools for the excluded.

Do this: Education is the most powerful weapon against entrenched hierarchy; open its doors to those shut out.
Principle 3

Build in partnership

He and Savitribai reformed together.

Do this: A committed partnership can multiply reforming power; shared conviction sustains the work against fierce resistance.

Frequently asked questions

Who was Jyotirao Phule?

A radical 19th-century Indian social reformer who, with his wife Savitribai, opened India's first schools for girls and oppressed castes, attacked the caste system directly, and founded the Satyashodhak Samaj for social equality.

What was Phule's influence?

His blunt critique of caste oppression, including in his book Gulamgiri, and his pioneering work for education and equality influenced later leaders like B. R. Ambedkar and the broader anti-caste movement.
