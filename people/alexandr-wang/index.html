Alexandr Wang

He built the data-labelling backbone that modern AI is trained on — a teenage dropout turned AI-infrastructure force.

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Alexandr Wang dropped out of MIT at 19 to found Scale AI in 2016, recognising that the AI revolution would run on vast amounts of high-quality labelled data — and that providing that data infrastructure could be an enormous business. Scale became a key supplier of the training data behind many leading AI systems and self-driving programmes, making Wang one of the youngest self-made billionaires. In 2025, Meta took a large stake in Scale and Wang joined Meta to help lead its superintelligence effort, with Scale continuing under new day-to-day leadership.

Wang saw that the unglamorous 'picks and shovels' layer — data — was where much of AI's value would accrue. He is a study in spotting the essential infrastructure beneath a boom, in the leverage of serving every player rather than competing, and in moving early and fast on a foundational need.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Sell picks and shovels

Wang built the data layer every AI needs.

Do this: In a boom, the infrastructure everyone depends on can be a better business than competing in the gold rush itself.
Principle 2

Serve every player

Scale supplies data to many AI companies.

Do this: Being the neutral supplier to a whole industry is high-leverage; you win whoever wins.
Principle 3

Move early on the foundational need

He saw data mattered before most did.

Do this: Spot the essential, under-appreciated need beneath an emerging wave and move on it early and fast.

In their words

Data is the fuel of AI.
The unsexy infrastructure is where the real value often sits.
Move early, move fast, and serve everyone building the future.
