Daniel Kahneman

The psychologist who proved the human mind is predictably irrational — and won a Nobel in economics for it.

4 min read · 1971–2024

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Daniel Kahneman, with his collaborator Amos Tversky, overturned the foundational economic assumption that humans are rational decision-makers. Through decades of ingenious experiments, they mapped the systematic biases and mental shortcuts — loss aversion, anchoring, availability, overconfidence — that lead people to make predictably irrational choices, founding the field of behavioural economics and earning Kahneman the 2002 Nobel Prize in economics (a psychologist winning the economics prize).

His book Thinking, Fast and Slow distilled a career into one framework: the mind has two systems — System 1, fast, intuitive, and emotional; System 2, slow, deliberate, and logical — and most of our errors come from trusting fast intuition where slow reasoning is needed. Understanding these biases is foundational to better decisions, and underpins much of the mental-models thinking on this site. He is a study in the systematic flaws of human judgement, and in the humility and tools required to think more clearly.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Know your own biases

Kahneman mapped the systematic errors that fool everyone, including experts.

Do this: Learn the common biases — loss aversion, anchoring, overconfidence — and watch for them in yourself.
Principle 2

Slow down for important decisions

He showed errors come from trusting fast intuition where slow reasoning is needed.

Do this: For important judgements, engage slow, deliberate thinking rather than snap intuition.
Principle 3

Distrust confident intuition

He found confidence is a poor guide to accuracy.

Do this: Treat your own confidence with suspicion; it's often unrelated to being right.

In their words

Nothing in life is as important as you think it is while you are thinking about it.
We can be blind to the obvious, and we are also blind to our blindness.
The confidence people have in their beliefs is not a measure of the quality of evidence.

Frequently asked questions

What did Daniel Kahneman prove?

That humans are predictably irrational — with his collaborator Amos Tversky he mapped the systematic biases and mental shortcuts that distort judgement, founding behavioural economics and winning the 2002 Nobel Prize in economics.

What are System 1 and System 2?

Kahneman's framework from Thinking, Fast and Slow: System 1 is fast, intuitive and emotional; System 2 is slow, deliberate and logical — and most errors come from trusting fast intuition where slow reasoning is needed.
