Akio Morita

The founder who made 'Made in Japan' a mark of quality and put a personal stereo in every pocket.

4 min read · 1946–1994

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Akio Morita co-founded Sony in the rubble of postwar Japan and turned it into a global byword for innovative, well-designed electronics — transforming 'Made in Japan' from a synonym for cheap goods into a mark of quality. He paired an engineer's product instinct with a marketer's global vision, insisting Sony build its own brand worldwide rather than manufacture anonymously for others.

Morita championed products the market hadn't asked for but came to love: the pocket transistor radio, the Trinitron television, and above all the Walkman, which created the entire category of personal portable music. He understood, like Steve Jobs after him, that customers can't always tell you what they want — sometimes you must show them.

Morita's global outlook — living in America to understand its market, building Sony into a truly international company — made him one of the most influential businessmen of the twentieth century and a bridge between Japanese manufacturing and Western consumers.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Build your own brand

Morita refused to be an anonymous manufacturer, investing in the Sony name globally.

Do this: Own your brand and customer relationship rather than making anonymously for others.
Principle 2

Lead demand, don't just follow it

The Walkman created a market no survey predicted.

Do this: Have the conviction to build what customers will love before they can ask for it.
Principle 3

Think globally from the start

Morita immersed himself in foreign markets to build a world company.

Do this: Understand your key markets first-hand; don't manage them from a distance.

In their words

Curiosity is the key to creativity.
You can be totally rational with a machine. But if you work with people, logic often has to take a back seat to understanding.
Never follow the others.

Frequently asked questions

What was Akio Morita's biggest contribution?

Building Sony into a global brand and pioneering products like the Walkman — and, in doing so, transforming the world's perception of Japanese manufacturing into a mark of quality.

How did Morita view customer research?

Like Jobs, he believed customers can't always articulate what they want, so a great company must sometimes lead demand by showing them something new.
