Rabindranath Tagore

Part I — The story

The story

Rabindranath Tagore was a towering polymath of the Bengal Renaissance — poet, novelist, playwright, composer, painter, philosopher and educator — who in 1913 became the first non-European and first Asian to win the Nobel Prize in Literature, for his collection of poems Gitanjali. He reshaped Bengali literature and music, wrote some 2,000 songs (Rabindra Sangeet), and composed the national anthems of both India and Bangladesh.

Tagore founded an experimental university, Visva-Bharati at Santiniketan, built on his ideals of education in harmony with nature and across cultures. He is a study in creative range across many arts, in blending the traditions of East and West, and in pairing artistic genius with an original philosophy of education and human freedom.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Create across many forms

Tagore mastered poetry, music, painting and more.

Do this: Don't confine yourself to one medium if several call to you; cross-pollination across arts deepens each.
Principle 2

Bridge traditions

He blended Indian and Western thought and forms.

Do this: Draw on more than one tradition; synthesis across cultures can produce something richer than either alone.
Principle 3

Build institutions for your ideals

He founded a university embodying his philosophy.

Do this: Give your ideals a lasting form by building the institution that embodies them, not just the works.
