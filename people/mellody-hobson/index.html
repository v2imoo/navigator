Mellody Hobson

The investor who rose from poverty to lead a major fund — preaching patience, candour, and being 'color brave'.

4 min read · 1991–present

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Mellody Hobson grew up the youngest of six children raised by a single mother in financial precarity — evictions and disconnected utilities were part of her childhood — an experience that gave her an early, visceral understanding of money and a lifelong mission around financial literacy. She rose to lead Ariel Investments, one of the largest Black-owned asset-management firms, becoming one of the most respected voices in American finance and a prominent corporate director.

Ariel's philosophy is patient, long-term value investing — its symbol is the tortoise, 'slow and steady wins the race' — buying quality companies at good prices and holding through volatility. Hobson is also known for candour about difficult subjects, coining the phrase 'color brave' to urge people to confront rather than avoid conversations about race. She is a study in rising from hardship through financial literacy, in the discipline of patient long-term investing, and in the leadership value of direct, courageous candour.
