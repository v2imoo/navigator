Lou Gerstner

He saved a dying giant by rejecting the one thing everyone demanded: breaking it up.

4 min read · 1990s–2000s

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

When Lou Gerstner became CEO of IBM in 1993, the company was haemorrhaging billions and widely considered doomed; the consensus, even inside IBM, was to break it into pieces. Gerstner — an outsider from the world of consumer goods and consulting, with no tech background — made the contrarian call to keep IBM whole, betting that its unique value was precisely its ability to integrate complex technology for large customers. He refocused the company on services and solutions, overhauled its insular culture, and engineered one of the great corporate turnarounds.

Gerstner showed that the famous line 'culture is everything' was not soft talk but the hardest problem in the turnaround. He is a study in the value of an outsider's perspective, in making the contrarian strategic bet when it's right, and in the primacy of culture in changing an organisation.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Question the consensus solution

Gerstner rejected the universal call to break up IBM.

Do this: When everyone agrees on the fix, test it hardest; the contrarian call, if right, can be the whole turnaround.
Principle 2

Use the outsider's clarity

His lack of tech baggage let him see IBM's real value.

Do this: An outsider's perspective can spot the strategic truth insiders are too close to see; use fresh eyes deliberately.
Principle 3

Treat culture as the hard problem

He found changing IBM's culture was the core challenge.

Do this: Strategy is easy to write and hard to live; the real work of change is almost always cultural.

In their words

Culture isn't just one aspect of the game — it is the game.

Frequently asked questions

Who is Lou Gerstner?

The outsider CEO who turned around IBM in the 1990s when it was near collapse — rejecting the consensus to break it up, refocusing it on services and integration, and overhauling its culture.

What was Gerstner's key decision at IBM?

To keep IBM whole rather than split it into pieces as nearly everyone advised, betting that its unique value was its ability to integrate complex technology for large customers.
