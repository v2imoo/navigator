Jeff Bezos

The founder who weaponised long-term thinking and customer obsession to build the everything store.

4 min read · 1994–present

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Jeff Bezos built Amazon from an online bookstore run out of a garage into one of the most valuable companies on earth by relentlessly optimising for two things almost everyone else was willing to trade away: the long term, and the customer. Where rivals chased quarterly profit, Bezos reinvested everything and told shareholders, plainly, to expect it.

A Wall Street quant before he was a founder, Bezos spotted the explosive growth of the early web in 1994, made a list of products that could sell online, and chose books — a huge catalogue no physical store could match. He left a comfortable job, drove west, and wrote the business plan on the way. Amazon's culture of customer obsession, high standards, and 'Day 1' urgency flowed from him.

The flywheel was his real invention: lower prices bring more customers, which bring more sellers, which bring more selection and scale, which lower costs and prices again. Amazon Web Services then turned Amazon's own infrastructure into the backbone of the internet and its profit engine. Bezos stepped down as CEO in 2021 to focus on Blue Origin, his space company, remaining executive chairman.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Obsess over customers, not competitors

Bezos anchored every decision on the customer, working backward from their needs — a compass that outlasts any rival's move.

Do this: Start from the customer's need and work backward to the product.
Principle 2

Optimise for the long term

He accepted years of low profit to build durable advantage, and told shareholders so from the first letter.

Do this: Judge decisions on their long-term payoff, even at short-term cost.
Principle 3

Build a flywheel

Lower prices → more customers → more sellers → more scale → lower costs. Each turn powered the next.

Do this: Design a loop where each success makes the next one cheaper and easier.

Frequently asked questions

What was Bezos's key insight with Amazon?

That optimising relentlessly for customers and the long term — even at the cost of short-term profit — would build a durable flywheel of growth rivals couldn't match.

Is Jeff Bezos still Amazon's CEO?

No — he stepped down as CEO in 2021, handing the role to Andy Jassy, and became executive chairman while focusing on Blue Origin.
