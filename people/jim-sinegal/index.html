Jim Sinegal

He built a retail giant by deliberately capping its own profit margins — and letting customers keep the difference.

4 min read · 1980s–2010s

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Jim Sinegal co-founded Costco in 1983 and built it into one of the world's most admired retailers on a deceptively simple model: sell a limited selection of goods in bulk at razor-thin margins, make the profit from membership fees, and never mark anything up beyond a strict cap even when you could. He treated low prices as a sacred promise to members and paid his workers unusually well, betting that loyal customers and low staff turnover would compound into a durable advantage.

Sinegal resisted Wall Street pressure to raise margins, insisting that short-term greed would betray the flywheel that made Costco work. He is a study in the discipline of self-restraint on margin, in a business model where you make money by saving customers money, and in treating both customers and employees as the engine of long-term value.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Make low price a sacred promise

Sinegal capped markups even when he could charge more.

Do this: Commit to a value promise you won't break for short-term gain; the trust it builds compounds into loyalty.
Principle 2

Change where you make the money

Costco profits from membership, not markup.

Do this: Rethink your profit model so your incentives align with the customer's; make money when they win.
Principle 3

Treat employees as the engine

He paid well to earn loyalty and low turnover.

Do this: Invest in your people; loyal, low-turnover staff quietly power the customer experience and the economics.

In their words

Culture is not the most important thing — it's the only thing.
We would rather have 10 cents less margin and keep the customer for life.
If you pay the best wages, you get the best people.

Frequently asked questions

Who is Jim Sinegal?

The co-founder and long-time CEO of Costco, who built it into one of the world's most admired retailers on razor-thin margins, membership-fee profits, strict markup caps and unusually high worker pay.

Why does Costco cap its markups?

Sinegal insisted that keeping prices low — even when the company could charge more — was a sacred promise to members that built the loyalty and volume the whole model depends on.
