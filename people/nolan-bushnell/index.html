Nolan Bushnell

The founder who launched the video-game industry with Pong — and once employed a young Steve Jobs.

4 min read · 1972–present

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Nolan Bushnell founded Atari and effectively launched the commercial video-game industry, first with the arcade hit Pong and then with the Atari home console that brought gaming into living rooms. He turned a novel technology into mass entertainment and created an entirely new industry from close to nothing.

Atari's freewheeling, creative culture became legendary, and Bushnell famously gave an early job to a young Steve Jobs, who absorbed lessons he later applied at Apple. Bushnell went on to found the Chuck E. Cheese entertainment-restaurant chain, applying the same instinct for combining technology with fun. His career had dramatic ups and downs, but his role as a father of the video-game industry is secure.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Create a new category

Bushnell turned a novel technology into a whole industry.

Do this: Turn an emerging technology into a new form of product or entertainment.
Principle 2

Cultivate a creative culture

Atari's freewheeling culture bred talent and ideas.

Do this: Foster a playful, creative culture that attracts and develops original people.
Principle 3

Combine technology with fun

From Atari to Chuck E. Cheese, he merged tech and entertainment.

Do this: Look for ways to fuse new technology with genuine fun and delight.
