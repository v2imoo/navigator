Hetty Green

The 19th-century woman who out-invested Wall Street's men — by being greedy when they were fearful.

4 min read · 1865–1916

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Hetty Green was one of the wealthiest and most successful investors of the nineteenth century — a woman who dominated finance in an era that barred women from it almost entirely, dubbed 'the Witch of Wall Street' by men unsettled by her success. She practised a disciplined value-investing philosophy decades before it had a name: buying quality assets — railroad bonds, real estate, government debt — when panics made them cheap, and holding with iron patience.

Green's edge was counter-cyclical conservatism. She kept large cash reserves so that when financial panics struck and others were forced to sell, she had the liquidity to buy distressed assets at bargain prices — even lending to the city of New York during the Panic of 1907. Famously, obsessively frugal, she avoided debt and speculation and focused on steady, compounding returns. She is a study in disciplined value investing, in the power of liquidity and patience through crises, and in succeeding against overwhelming barriers.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Keep liquidity for the panic

Green held cash so she could buy distressed assets when others were forced to sell.

Do this: Keep reserves so that when panic strikes, you can buy what others must dump cheaply.
Principle 2

Be greedy when others are fearful

She bought quality in crises and held with iron patience.

Do this: Buy quality assets when fear makes them cheap, then hold patiently.
Principle 3

Avoid debt and speculation

Her frugal, conservative discipline compounded steadily.

Do this: Avoid leverage and speculation; steady, compounding discipline wins over time.

In their words

I believe in getting in at the bottom and out at the top.
When I see a good thing going cheap because nobody wants it, I buy a lot of it and tuck it away.
There is no great secret in fortune making. All you have to do is buy cheap and sell dear, act with thrift and shrewdness, and be persistent.
