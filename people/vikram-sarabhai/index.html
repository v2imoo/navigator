Vikram Sarabhai

He argued a poor nation needed space most of all — and founded the programme that became ISRO.

4 min read · 1940s–1971

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Vikram Sarabhai was the visionary who founded India's space programme — the precursor to ISRO — against the common objection that a poor, developing nation had no business in space. His counter-argument was decisive: India needed space technology precisely to leapfrog in solving real problems like communication, education, weather and resource mapping for its vast, dispersed population. A physicist and institution-builder, he also co-founded the Indian Institute of Management Ahmedabad and the Physical Research Laboratory.

Sarabhai set India on a path to becoming a serious space power, focused on development rather than prestige. He is a study in reframing a 'luxury' as a necessity for leapfrogging, in building the institutions a nation needs, and in aiming advanced technology at concrete human problems.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Reframe the 'luxury' as a necessity

Sarabhai argued a poor nation needed space most.

Do this: A capability dismissed as a luxury may be exactly what lets you leapfrog; reframe it around the real problems it solves.
Principle 2

Aim advanced tech at human problems

He targeted communication, education, weather.

Do this: Point frontier technology at concrete needs of ordinary people; development, not prestige, is the worthier goal.
Principle 3

Build the enabling institutions

He founded ISRO's precursor, IIM-A and more.

Do this: Lasting progress needs institutions; build the organisations that will carry the mission for generations.

In their words

There are some who question the relevance of space activities in a developing nation. To us, there is no ambiguity of purpose.
We must be second to none in the application of advanced technologies to the real problems of man.
The real test is whether it helps the common person.

Frequently asked questions

Who was Vikram Sarabhai?

The father of India's space programme — founder of ISRO's precursor — who argued a developing nation needed space technology precisely to leapfrog in communication, education and resource mapping, and co-founded IIM Ahmedabad and the Physical Research Laboratory.

What was Sarabhai's argument for a space programme?

That a poor nation needed space most of all — not for prestige, but to leapfrog in solving real problems like communication, education, weather and resource mapping for its vast, dispersed population.
