Zhang Yiming

He built the recommendation engine that learned what the world wanted to watch before the world knew itself.

4 min read · 2010s–present

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Zhang Yiming founded ByteDance in 2012 on a bet that artificial intelligence, not human editors or social graphs, should decide what content people see. His apps — the news aggregator Toutiao and especially the short-video platform TikTok (Douyin in China) — used powerful recommendation algorithms to serve each user an endlessly personalised feed, learning from behaviour rather than relying on who you follow. TikTok became one of the fastest-growing and most-used apps in the world, a rare Chinese product to achieve global consumer dominance.

Zhang's edge was treating recommendation as the core product and the algorithm as the moat. He is a study in building a company around a single technical capability, in letting machine learning replace human curation at scale, and in reaching global consumers from an unexpected origin.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Make the algorithm the product

Zhang built ByteDance around recommendation itself.

Do this: Identify the single technical capability that is your real product, and pour everything into making it the best.
Principle 2

Let the machine learn the user

TikTok reads behaviour instead of relying on follows.

Do this: Where you can, let data and learning replace manual curation; the system that adapts to each user wins attention.
Principle 3

Design for discovery, not just social graph

His feed surfaces content by interest, not connections.

Do this: Don't assume people only want what their network shares; engines that surface relevance can out-engage social feeds.

Delay gratification; focus on the long term.
Be a cool-headed optimist.

Frequently asked questions

Who is Zhang Yiming?

The founder of ByteDance (2012), the company behind TikTok and Toutiao, who built one of the world's biggest technology companies on AI-driven content recommendation rather than social graphs or human editors.

Why was ByteDance's approach distinctive?

It treated the recommendation algorithm as the core product — serving each user a personalised feed learned from behaviour rather than from who they follow — which powered TikTok's explosive global growth.
