Satyajit Ray

With almost no money and a first-time crew, he made 'Pather Panchali' — and brought Indian cinema to the world.

4 min read · 1950s–1992

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Satyajit Ray is widely regarded as one of the greatest filmmakers in the history of cinema. A commercial artist with no filmmaking experience, he made his debut Pather Panchali (1955) on a shoestring budget with an amateur crew, financing it partly by pawning his wife's jewellery — and created a work of such humane, lyrical realism that it, and the Apu Trilogy it began, won acclaim around the world and put Indian cinema on the global map. He went on to make dozens of films, and also wrote, illustrated and composed.

Ray's films found the profound in ordinary lives, rendered with restraint and deep humanity. He received an honorary Academy Award shortly before his death. He is a study in creating world-class art with almost no resources, in finding universal truth in ordinary life, and in mastering many crafts in service of one vision.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Create with almost nothing

Ray made a masterpiece on a shoestring.

Do this: A lack of resources need not stop great work; constraint can force the ingenuity that defines a masterpiece.
Principle 2

Find the profound in the ordinary

His films elevated everyday lives.

Do this: The deepest art often lies in ordinary life rendered truthfully; you don't need grand subjects to reach the universal.
Principle 3

Master many crafts for one vision

He wrote, composed and illustrated his films.

Do this: Deep control across the crafts your work needs lets you realise a singular vision without compromise.

Frequently asked questions

Who was Satyajit Ray?

One of the greatest filmmakers in cinema history, who made his debut Pather Panchali (1955) on a shoestring with an amateur crew, created the acclaimed Apu Trilogy, and brought Indian cinema to global recognition.

How did Ray make his first film?

With no filmmaking experience and almost no money — financing Pather Panchali partly by pawning his wife's jewellery and using an amateur crew — yet producing a work of humane, lyrical realism that won worldwide acclaim.
