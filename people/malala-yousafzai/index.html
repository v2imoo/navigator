Malala Yousafzai

Shot for going to school, she survived and became the world's most powerful voice for girls' education.

4 min read · 2010s–present

Part I — The story

The story

Malala Yousafzai grew up in Pakistan's Swat Valley, where the Taliban banned girls from school. As a child she spoke out for her right to an education, writing an anonymous blog and campaigning publicly. In 2012, at 15, she was shot in the head by a Taliban gunman for her activism — and survived. Rather than being silenced, she became the world's most prominent advocate for girls' education, and in 2014, at 17, the youngest person ever to win the Nobel Peace Prize.

She co-founded the Malala Fund and continues to campaign globally for every girl's right to twelve years of free, quality education. She is a study in courage against lethal intimidation, in turning an attempt to silence you into a louder voice, and in the moral force of a single young person standing on principle.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Refuse to be silenced

An attempt to kill Malala made her voice louder.

Do this: Intimidation aimed at silencing you can, met with courage, amplify your cause instead; don't let fear win.
Principle 2

Speak from lived principle

Her authority came from her own fight for school.

Do this: Advocacy grounded in your own lived stake carries a moral force that detached commentary can't match.
Principle 3

Turn survival into mission

She built a global cause from her ordeal.

Do this: Channel what you survived into a mission larger than yourself; purpose transforms trauma into power.
