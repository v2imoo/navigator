Joel Greenblatt

He posted one of history's great investing records — then distilled it into a formula a teenager could follow.

4 min read · 1980s–present

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Joel Greenblatt ran Gotham Capital to a reported compound return of around 40% a year for two decades — one of the finest records in investing history — largely through special situations: spin-offs, restructurings, mergers and other overlooked corners where mispricing hides. He then did something unusual for a great investor: he wrote simple, witty books distilling his thinking for ordinary readers, most famously The Little Book That Beats the Market.

That book introduced the 'Magic Formula' — rank companies by a combination of high return on capital (good businesses) and high earnings yield (cheap prices), then buy the top of the list — a mechanical version of buying good businesses at bargain prices. He is a study in hunting where others don't look, in reducing a complex edge to a teachable rule, and in the generosity of explaining your method plainly.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Hunt in overlooked corners

Greenblatt made fortunes in spin-offs and special situations.

Do this: Look where institutional money can't or won't go — forced selling and complexity create mispricing.
Principle 2

Buy good businesses cheaply

His formula combines quality (ROIC) with value (earnings yield).

Do this: Seek the intersection of a genuinely good business and a genuinely cheap price — both, not either.
Principle 3

Reduce your edge to a rule

He turned his method into a formula anyone could follow.

Do this: If you can distil your approach into a simple, repeatable rule, you'll apply it more consistently.

In their words

Choosing individual stocks without any idea of what you're looking for is like running through a dynamite factory with a burning match.
The secret to investing is to figure out the value of something — and then pay a lot less.
Mr. Market is there to serve you, not to guide you.

What is the Magic Formula?
