Mahatma Gandhi

He fought an empire with the one weapon it couldn't answer: disciplined, mass non-violence.

4 min read · 1900s–1948

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Mohandas 'Mahatma' Gandhi led India's struggle for independence from British rule through satyagraha — the disciplined practice of non-violent resistance and civil disobedience. From organising Indian labourers in South Africa to the Salt March of 1930, when he walked some 240 miles to make salt in defiance of British law, he mobilised hundreds of millions of ordinary people against one of the most powerful empires in history, and India won its independence in 1947.

Gandhi's method was to refuse cooperation with injustice while refusing to answer violence with violence — turning the moral asymmetry into a force the empire could not suppress without exposing itself. He lived with extreme simplicity and insisted means and ends must match. He was assassinated in 1948. He is a study in moral leverage over brute force, in the discipline required for mass non-violence, and in the power of embodying the change you demand.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Refuse cooperation with injustice

Gandhi made non-cooperation a weapon against the empire.

Do this: Power often depends on your participation; withdrawing it, in disciplined concert with others, is itself force.
Principle 2

Match means to ends

He insisted the methods must embody the world you want.

Do this: Don't pursue a just end by unjust means; the way you win shapes what you actually get.
Principle 3

Embody the change you demand

His personal simplicity gave his message unanswerable authority.

Do this: Live the standard you ask of others; moral authority comes from practice, not pronouncement.

In their words

Be the change you wish to see in the world.
First they ignore you, then they laugh at you, then they fight you, then you win.
Strength does not come from physical capacity. It comes from an indomitable will.

Frequently asked questions

What is satyagraha?

Gandhi's method of non-violent resistance — holding firmly to truth by refusing to cooperate with injustice while refusing to answer it with violence, turning moral asymmetry into a force that could not be suppressed without self-exposure.

What was the Salt March?

Gandhi's 1930 protest in which he walked roughly 240 miles to the sea to make salt in defiance of the British salt monopoly — a galvanising act of mass civil disobedience in India's independence struggle.
