Ferdinand Magellan

He led the first expedition to sail around the world — though he died before it finished, proving the Earth could be encircled.

4 min read · 16th century

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Ferdinand Magellan, a Portuguese explorer sailing for Spain, led the expedition that achieved the first circumnavigation of the Earth. Setting out in 1519 with five ships, he found the strait at South America's tip that still bears his name, crossed the vast Pacific (which he named for its calm), and reached the Philippines — where he was killed in a local conflict in 1521. One ship and a handful of survivors completed the voyage home, proving definitively that the world could be sailed around and grasping its true scale.

The achievement came at enormous cost in lives and hardship, driven by Magellan's conviction and resolve. He is a study in pursuing an audacious goal against the odds, in a vision completed by others after the leader falls, and in the courage to venture into the genuinely unknown.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Pursue the audacious goal

Magellan attempted what no one had done.

Do this: Genuinely audacious goals, pursued with conviction, expand what humanity knows is possible — even at great cost.
Principle 2

Venture into the unknown

He sailed waters no European had charted.

Do this: Real discovery requires going where there is no map; prepare as you can, then accept irreducible uncertainty.
Principle 3

Let the mission outlive you

His crew completed what he began.

Do this: A goal worth pursuing can be finished by others if you fall; build a mission bigger than any one life.

Frequently asked questions

Who was Ferdinand Magellan?

A Portuguese explorer sailing for Spain who led the first expedition to circumnavigate the Earth, setting out in 1519; he was killed in the Philippines in 1521, but survivors completed the voyage, proving the world could be sailed around.

Did Magellan complete the circumnavigation himself?

No — he was killed in a conflict in the Philippines in 1521; one ship and a handful of his crew completed the journey home, achieving the first circumnavigation of the globe.
