Steve Wozniak

The engineering genius behind Apple's first computers — proof that the builder and the visionary are different gifts.

4 min read · 1976–present

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Steve Wozniak was the engineering genius who designed and largely single-handedly built the Apple I and Apple II computers — the machines that launched Apple and helped ignite the personal-computer revolution. A brilliant, playful hardware and software engineer, 'Woz' created elegant designs of remarkable ingenuity, squeezing maximum capability from minimal components in ways that amazed other engineers, driven by the sheer joy of building rather than by money or power.

His partnership with Steve Jobs is the archetype of a founding pair: Wozniak the technical genius who could build almost anything, Jobs the visionary who saw what it could become and how to sell it. Woz has been candid that he had no interest in running a company and simply loved to engineer. He is a study in the distinct gift of the builder versus the visionary, in engineering elegance and ingenuity, and in doing great work for the love of the craft itself.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Know whether you're the builder or visionary

Woz was the genius builder; Jobs the visionary — different gifts.

Do this: Know which gift is yours — building or vision — and pair with someone who has the other.
Principle 2

Pursue elegance and ingenuity

His designs did more with less, amazing other engineers.

Do this: Pursue elegant solutions that do more with less; ingenuity is its own advantage.
Principle 3

Do great work for love of the craft

Woz engineered for joy, not money or power.

Do this: Doing work you genuinely love for its own sake often produces your best.

In their words

Never trust a computer you can't throw out a window.
I designed it to do more with fewer parts — that was the fun of it.
If you love what you do and are willing to do what it takes, it's within your reach.
