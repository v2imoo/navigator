I. M. Pei

He fused clean modern geometry with cultural grace — and put a glass pyramid in front of the Louvre.

4 min read · 1950s–2010s

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

I. M. Pei, a Chinese-American architect, built a career of precise, light-filled modernism grounded in geometry — bold forms in stone, concrete and glass that nonetheless felt graceful and humane. His most famous work, the glass pyramid at the entrance of the Louvre in Paris, was fiercely controversial when proposed but became a beloved icon, a model of placing something boldly modern in dialogue with the historic. He won the Pritzker Prize in 1983.

Pei was known for diplomacy and patience in realising difficult projects across cultures, and for believing architecture should serve people and endure. He is a study in marrying modern form with cultural sensitivity, in winning over fierce opposition through conviction and tact, and in a long career of quiet, geometric mastery.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Marry the modern with the historic

Pei's Louvre Pyramid dialogued with the old palace.

Do this: The boldest modern statement can honour, rather than erase, its historic setting; design in dialogue, not defiance.
Principle 2

Win opposition through conviction and tact

He saw controversial projects through.

Do this: Fierce opposition to a bold idea can be overcome with patient conviction and diplomacy, not just being right.
Principle 3

Build to endure and serve

He believed architecture should last and serve people.

Do this: Design for permanence and human use; work that endures and serves beats work that merely impresses briefly.

Frequently asked questions

Who was I. M. Pei?

A Chinese-American architect celebrated for precise, light-filled geometric modernism — most famously the glass pyramid at the Louvre — who won the Pritzker Prize in 1983 and worked into old age.

Why was the Louvre Pyramid significant?

Fiercely controversial when proposed, Pei's glass pyramid became a beloved icon and a model of placing something boldly modern in graceful dialogue with a historic setting.
