Katharine Graham

The publisher who found her voice under fire — and staked her paper on Watergate and the Pentagon Papers.

4 min read · 1963–1991

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Katharine Graham became publisher of The Washington Post after her husband's death thrust her, unprepared and doubting herself, into leadership of a family newspaper. She grew into one of the most consequential figures in American media, guiding the Post through the defining press battles of the era — publishing the Pentagon Papers and backing the Watergate investigation that ultimately led to a president's resignation.

Those decisions carried enormous risk — legal, financial and political — and Graham made them with courage that transformed the Post into a great national newspaper and defended press freedom itself. She also built it into a strong business and public company, and later wrote a candid memoir about finding confidence and leadership late and under pressure.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Stand by principle under pressure

Graham published and investigated despite grave risk.

Do this: When principle and risk collide, have the courage to stand by what's right.
Principle 2

Grow into the role

Thrust into leadership unprepared, she learned and rose.

Do this: Don't wait to feel ready; capability grows by meeting the role's demands.
Principle 3

Back your best people

She supported her editors and reporters against enormous pressure.

Do this: Give your best people cover to do difficult, important work.
