Benjamin Graham

He turned investing from speculation into a discipline — and taught Warren Buffett to buy a dollar for fifty cents.

4 min read · 1920s–1970s

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Benjamin Graham built the intellectual foundation of value investing. In the aftermath of crashes and manias, he argued that a stock is not a ticker to be gambled on but a fractional ownership of a real business with an intrinsic value that can be estimated — and that the investor's job is to buy when the price sits well below that value, leaving a 'margin of safety'. His two books, Security Analysis and The Intelligent Investor, remain the canon.

Graham gave investors two enduring ideas: the parable of 'Mr. Market', a manic-depressive business partner who offers you wild prices every day that you are free to ignore; and the margin of safety, the discount that protects you when your analysis is wrong. His most famous student, Warren Buffett, called The Intelligent Investor the best book on investing ever written. He is a study in treating price and value as different things, in building in protection against your own errors, and in temperament as the investor's true edge.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Separate price from value

Graham insisted intrinsic value is distinct from the day's quote.

Do this: Estimate what a business is worth independently, then compare it to the price — never let the quote define value.
Principle 2

Demand a margin of safety

He bought only at a large discount to intrinsic value.

Do this: Build in a cushion so that even if your analysis is wrong, you don't lose much — protection first, upside second.
Principle 3

Treat Mr. Market as a servant

Graham said the market exists to serve you, not instruct you.

Do this: Use the market's mood swings to transact on your terms; ignore its prices when they're irrational.

In their words

In the short run the market is a voting machine, but in the long run it is a weighing machine.
The margin of safety is always dependent on the price paid.
The investor's chief problem — and even his worst enemy — is likely to be himself.

Frequently asked questions

Who was Benjamin Graham?

The father of value investing — he taught that a stock is a share of a real business with an intrinsic value, to be bought only at a discount (a 'margin of safety'), and wrote Security Analysis and The Intelligent Investor.

What is 'Mr. Market'?

Graham's parable of the market as a manic-depressive partner who offers you wildly varying prices each day; a wise investor transacts only when the price is favourable and otherwise ignores his moods.
