Niccolò Machiavelli

The thinker who described power as it actually is, not as it ought to be.

4 min read · 1498–1527

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Niccolò Machiavelli, a Florentine diplomat and writer, produced in The Prince the founding work of modern political realism — an unflinching analysis of how power is actually gained, held and lost, rather than how it ought to be in an ideal moral world. His willingness to describe politics as it really works, stripped of comforting illusions, made his name a byword for cunning, often unfairly.

Machiavelli's real contribution was intellectual honesty about power and human nature: that leaders must deal with the world as it is, that being feared can be safer than being loved, that outcomes sometimes justify hard means, and that fortune favours the bold and adaptable. Whether or not one accepts his conclusions, his clear-eyed realism about incentives, power and human behaviour remains sharply relevant to leadership and strategy.

A central theme

Machiavelli is a foundational figure in thinking clearly about power, incentives and human nature. His unflinching realism — seeing the world as it is rather than as we wish it — is a discipline as valuable in business and strategy as in politics, however one judges his specific advice.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

See the world as it is

Machiavelli analysed power without comforting illusions.

Do this: Face reality and incentives as they actually are, not as you wish them to be.
Principle 2

Understand human nature

He reasoned clearly about how people really behave.

Do this: Base your plans on how people actually behave, not how they should.
Principle 3

Favour boldness and adaptability

He argued fortune favours the bold and flexible.

Do this: Act boldly and adapt to circumstance rather than clinging to rigid plans.

In their words

It is better to be feared than loved, if you cannot be both.
Never was anything great achieved without danger.
The wise man does at once what the fool does finally.

Frequently asked questions

What is The Prince about?

Machiavelli's founding work of modern political realism — an unflinching analysis of how power is actually gained, held and lost, describing politics as it really works rather than as it morally ought to be.

Is Machiavelli just about being ruthless?

No — his real contribution is intellectual honesty about power and human nature: seeing the world clearly, reasoning about incentives, and dealing with reality as it is, a discipline valuable well beyond his specific advice.
