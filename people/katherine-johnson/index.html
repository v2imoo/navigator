Katherine Johnson

The mathematician whose calculations sent Americans to space — trusted above the computers themselves.

4 min read · 1953–1986

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Katherine Johnson was a brilliant mathematician whose calculations of orbital mechanics at NASA were critical to the success of the first American crewed spaceflights — including the mission that first put an American in orbit and the Apollo missions to the Moon. A Black woman working in the segregated America of the 1950s and 60s, she overcame profound racial and gender barriers through undeniable excellence, becoming indispensable to the space programme at a time when people like her were systematically excluded.

Her trustworthiness was legendary: when NASA first used electronic computers to calculate John Glenn's orbital flight, Glenn reportedly refused to fly until Johnson personally verified the machine's numbers by hand — 'get the girl to check the numbers'. Her story, long overlooked, became widely known through the book and film Hidden Figures. She is a study in overcoming systemic barriers through sheer excellence, in becoming indispensable through trusted mastery, and in the human judgement that even the best machines must earn their way past.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Become indispensable through excellence

Johnson's undeniable skill made her essential despite every barrier.

Do this: Make your excellence so undeniable it overcomes the barriers in your way.
Principle 2

Earn trust above the machine

Glenn trusted her hand calculations over the computer itself.

Do this: Build a reputation for accuracy so trusted that your judgement is sought above all.
Principle 3

Let mastery break the barrier

Her work forced open doors prejudice tried to keep shut.

Do this: Sustained mastery can break down barriers that argument alone cannot.
