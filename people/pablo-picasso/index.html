Pablo Picasso

The prolific master who reinvented himself again and again — and understood the business of being an artist.

4 min read · 1901–1973

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Pablo Picasso was among the most influential and prolific artists in history, co-founding Cubism and repeatedly reinventing modern art across a career spanning seven decades. He produced tens of thousands of works and moved through distinct creative periods, refusing to settle into a single successful style — a restlessness that kept him at the frontier of art for a lifetime.

Picasso was also, unusually for a great artist, commercially astute: he understood the value of his own brand, cultivated his reputation, and managed his market shrewdly, dying enormously wealthy. His famous remark that 'good artists copy, great artists steal' captures a creative philosophy of absorbing influences and transforming them into something wholly your own — a lesson as relevant to founders and creators as to painters.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Reinvent, don't settle

Picasso kept changing style rather than repeating success.

Do this: Keep reinventing; don't get trapped repeating what worked before.
Principle 2

Produce at volume

His enormous output was part of his greatness.

Do this: Create prolifically; volume produces both mastery and the occasional masterpiece.
Principle 3

Transform your influences

'Great artists steal' — absorb and remake others' ideas as your own.

Do this: Absorb influences widely and transform them into something distinctly yours.

In their words

Good artists copy, great artists steal.
Every act of creation is first an act of destruction.
Inspiration exists, but it has to find you working.

Frequently asked questions

What did Picasso mean by 'great artists steal'?

That true creativity comes from absorbing influences widely and transforming them into something wholly your own — not timid copying but bold appropriation and reinvention.

What made Picasso so influential?

His relentless reinvention (co-founding Cubism and moving through many styles), his enormous prolific output over seven decades, and his shrewd understanding of his own brand and market.
