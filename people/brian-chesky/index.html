Brian Chesky

The designer-founder who turned air mattresses into a global marketplace by obsessing over experience and trust.

4 min read · 2008–present

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Brian Chesky co-founded Airbnb after he and his roommate, unable to make rent, put air mattresses on their floor and rented them to strangers attending a conference. From that improvised beginning they built a marketplace that let anyone rent out their home, growing into a company that reshaped travel and hospitality worldwide. Chesky, trained as a designer rather than an engineer, brought an obsessive focus on the quality of the end-to-end experience.

Airbnb's central challenge was trust — persuading strangers to sleep in each other's homes — and Chesky solved it through design: reviews, verified profiles, secure payments, and a relentless attention to how every step felt. Famously, he urged his team to build something people 'love', not merely like, even if it meant delighting a small number of users in unscalable ways first. He is a study in design-led thinking, in building trust into a marketplace, and in the power of creating an experience people adore.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Design the whole experience

Chesky, a designer, obsessed over how every step of the journey felt.

Do this: Treat the end-to-end experience as the product, and design every detail of it.
Principle 2

Build trust into the marketplace

Reviews, verification and secure payments let strangers transact safely.

Do this: In a marketplace of strangers, engineer trust deliberately — it's the real product.
Principle 3

Make something people love

He pushed to delight users, even unscalably, rather than merely satisfy them.

Do this: Aim for products people love, not just like — even if you must delight a few by hand first.

In their words

Build something 100 people love, not something a million people kind of like.
If we tried to think of a good idea, we wouldn't have been able to think of a good idea. You just have to find the solution for a problem in your own life.
Design is not just what it looks like. Design is how it works for people.
