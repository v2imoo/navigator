Part I — The story

The story

Simon Sinek rose to prominence with a simple, powerful idea captured in his book Start With Why and one of the most-watched TED talks of all time. His 'Golden Circle' argues that great leaders and organisations communicate from the inside out — starting with why they do what they do (their purpose), then how, then what — because people don't buy what you do, they buy why you do it. He used Apple and Martin Luther King as illustrations of leading with purpose.

Sinek has extended his work into trust, safety and long-term thinking with books like Leaders Eat Last and The Infinite Game. He is a study in the clarifying power of purpose, in communicating from the why outward, and in making a single idea memorable enough to change how people lead.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Start with why

Sinek says purpose must come before product.

Do this: Lead and communicate from your why — your purpose — before the what; people connect to the reason, not the feature.
Principle 2

Communicate inside-out

His Golden Circle runs why → how → what.

Do this: Order your message from purpose outward; the why gives the what its meaning and pull.
Principle 3

Play the infinite game

He urges a long-term, purpose-led mindset over short-term wins.

Do this: Treat leadership as an infinite game — the goal is to keep playing and advancing the purpose, not to 'win' a quarter.

In their words

People don't buy what you do; they buy why you do it.
Leadership is not about being in charge. It is about taking care of those in your charge.
Working hard for something we don't care about is stress; for something we love is passion.

Frequently asked questions

Who is Simon Sinek?

A leadership author and speaker known for Start With Why and one of the most-watched TED talks ever, who argues that great leaders inspire by communicating their purpose — their 'why' — first.

What is the Golden Circle?

Sinek's model that effective leaders and organisations communicate from the inside out — why (purpose), then how, then what — because 'people don't buy what you do, they buy why you do it'.
