Tiger Woods

He remade golf into a sport of athletic dominance — then authored one of the great comebacks after it all fell apart.

4 min read · 1990s–present

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Tiger Woods transformed golf. Bursting onto the scene with a record-breaking 1997 Masters win, he brought athleticism, intensity and a global, diverse audience to a staid sport, winning 15 major championships and dominating for over a decade. His relentless practice, mental fortitude and willingness to completely rebuild his swing — even at the height of his success — set a new standard for the game.

After a very public personal collapse and a series of career-threatening back surgeries, he authored one of sport's greatest comebacks, winning the 2019 Masters when almost no one thought he could. He is a study in redefining a discipline through intensity, in the courage to rebuild what already works in pursuit of better, and in resilience through failure both professional and personal.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Raise the whole game's standard

Woods brought athleticism golf hadn't seen.

Do this: You can redefine a field's norms; bring an intensity or approach the discipline hasn't demanded before.
Principle 2

Rebuild even what works

He reconstructed his swing at the top.

Do this: Willingness to tear down and rebuild a winning method in pursuit of better is how the great keep improving.
Principle 3

Author the comeback

He returned from collapse to win again.

Do this: A fall, however public, need not be the end; disciplined, patient rebuilding can produce a defining second act.
