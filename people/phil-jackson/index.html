Phil Jackson

He won 11 NBA titles by managing egos and minds as much as plays — coaching the whole person, not just the player.

Part I — The story

The story

Phil Jackson won 11 NBA championships as a head coach — more than anyone in league history — guiding the Michael Jordan–led Chicago Bulls and the Shaquille O'Neal–Kobe Bryant Los Angeles Lakers to their dynasties. His approach blended the 'triangle offense', a system that shared the ball and empowered every player, with a distinctive philosophy drawn from Zen Buddhism and Native American spirituality: mindfulness, selflessness, and coaching the whole person.

Jackson's genius was managing egos and minds — turning collections of stars into cohesive teams by subordinating individual glory to the group. He is a study in leading through psychology and culture rather than tactics alone, in systems that empower everyone over hero-ball, and in the art of managing talented, difficult individuals toward a shared goal.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Manage minds, not just plays

Jackson coached psychology and culture as much as tactics.

Do this: The hardest part of leading talent is the inner game; manage attention, ego and cohesion, not only strategy.
Principle 2

Build systems that empower everyone

The triangle offense shared the ball and the glory.

Do this: Design systems where every member contributes and benefits; shared ownership beats dependence on one star.
Principle 3

Subordinate ego to the group

He turned collections of stars into teams.

Do this: Great teams require stars to sacrifice for the whole; make selflessness the culture, modelled from the top.

In their words

The strength of the team is each individual member. The strength of each member is the team.
Wisdom is always an overmatch for strength.
Approach the game with no preset agendas and you'll probably come away surprised.

Why is Phil Jackson a legendary coach?

He won a record 11 NBA championships coaching the Michael Jordan Bulls and the Shaq–Kobe Lakers dynasties, blending the team-first triangle offense with a mindfulness-based philosophy of coaching the whole person.

What was distinctive about Jackson's coaching?

He managed minds and egos as much as tactics — drawing on Zen Buddhism and mindfulness, using the triangle offense to empower every player, and turning collections of stars into cohesive teams.
