Albert Einstein

The physicist who remade our understanding of reality through pure thought experiments.

4 min read · 1905–1955

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Albert Einstein transformed our understanding of space, time, gravity and light, and became the twentieth century's symbol of genius. Remarkably, he did much of his most revolutionary work not in a laboratory but through 'thought experiments' — imagining riding alongside a beam of light, or falling in an elevator — reasoning from first principles to conclusions that overturned centuries of physics.

Einstein's method is a masterclass in thinking: he questioned assumptions everyone else treated as fixed, followed logic to counter-intuitive but inescapable conclusions, and prized imagination as much as knowledge. His 1905 'miracle year' produced multiple field-defining papers. His willingness to trust reason over convention — and to persist against a sceptical establishment — changed science forever.

Beyond physics, Einstein is a model of how to think: reason from fundamentals, question inherited assumptions, and value imagination. His thought experiments are the purest demonstration that clear reasoning, not just data, can reveal the deepest truths.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Reason from first principles

Einstein questioned assumptions everyone treated as fixed.

Do this: Question the assumptions others accept, and reason up from fundamentals.
Principle 2

Use thought experiments

He tested ideas by imagining scenarios in his mind.

Do this: Work through problems by imagining the scenario fully and following the logic.
Principle 3

Value imagination

He prized imagination as highly as knowledge.

Do this: Cultivate imagination — it reaches where accumulated knowledge cannot.

In their words

Frequently asked questions

How did Einstein make his breakthroughs?

Largely through 'thought experiments' — imagining scenarios like riding a beam of light — reasoning from first principles and questioning assumptions everyone else treated as fixed.

What can non-physicists learn from Einstein?

A method of thinking: reason from fundamentals, question inherited assumptions, use imagination as much as knowledge, and follow logic to its conclusions even when they defy convention.
