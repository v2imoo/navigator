Eliyahu Goldratt

A physicist who taught the business world one ruthless idea: a system is only as fast as its single slowest step.

4 min read · 1980s–2000s

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Eliyahu Goldratt, a physicist by training, applied the logic of science to business operations and arrived at the Theory of Constraints: every system has one bottleneck that limits its entire output, and improving anything other than that constraint is wasted effort. He taught it not through a textbook but through The Goal, a 1984 business novel about a plant manager saving his factory — one of the best-selling and most influential management books ever written.

Goldratt's method is a disciplined loop: identify the constraint, exploit it fully, subordinate everything else to it, elevate it, then repeat as the bottleneck moves. He is a study in finding the single leverage point in any system, in the clarifying power of teaching through story, and in the counterintuitive truth that local efficiency everywhere can still leave the whole system slow.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Find the one constraint

Goldratt showed that a single bottleneck caps total output.

Do this: Locate the one step that limits the whole system; it's the only place improvement actually helps.
Principle 2

Subordinate everything to it

Non-bottleneck improvements are wasted or harmful.

Do this: Once you know the constraint, align the entire system to keep it fully fed and never idle.
Principle 3

Teach through story

The Goal taught via a novel, not a lecture.

Do this: To make a hard idea stick, embed it in a narrative people can feel their way through.

Frequently asked questions

What is the Theory of Constraints?

Eliyahu Goldratt's principle that every system is limited by a single bottleneck, so the only improvements that raise total output are those made at the constraint; everything else should be subordinated to it.

What is 'The Goal' about?

Goldratt's 1984 business novel in which a plant manager saves his failing factory by discovering the Theory of Constraints — one of the most influential management books ever written, teaching through story rather than lecture.
