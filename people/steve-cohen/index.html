Steve Cohen

He built a trading powerhouse on speed and information — and rebuilt his firm after a landmark insider-trading case.

4 min read · 1990s–present

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Steve Cohen built SAC Capital into one of the most successful and feared hedge funds in the world, reportedly compounding around 30% annually for years through rapid, information-intensive trading across many portfolio managers. In 2013 SAC pleaded guilty to insider-trading charges and paid a record $1.8 billion penalty; Cohen himself was not criminally charged but was barred from managing outside money for a period. He later returned, building Point72 into a major firm and buying the New York Mets.

Cohen's edge was built on speed, breadth of information, aggressive risk-taking and an intense trading culture. His story is inseparable from the compliance questions that shadowed SAC. He is a study in the power of an information edge and a trading culture, in the hard line between aggressive research and illegal information, and in rebuilding after a reputational collapse.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Build an information edge

Cohen's firm won on speed and breadth of research.

Do this: Compete where you can gather and act on information faster and more completely than rivals — within the rules.
Principle 2

Stay inside the hard line

SAC's downfall came from crossing the line into illegal information.

Do this: An aggressive research edge is legitimate; material non-public information is not — the line is absolute, and crossing it ruins everything.
Principle 3

Rebuild after collapse

He came back from a landmark case to build Point72.

Do this: A reputational blow need not be terminal if you rebuild with discipline and a clear record afterward.
