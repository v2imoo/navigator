Benjamin Franklin

The printer who made himself into a scientist, statesman and sage through relentless self-improvement.

4 min read · 1726–1790

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Benjamin Franklin was the ultimate self-made polymath — a printer who built a media and publishing business, then reinvented himself as a scientist, inventor, writer, diplomat and Founding Father of the United States. Starting with little formal education, he made himself successful enough to retire from business young, then devoted his life to science, civic institutions and statecraft.

Franklin was a master of self-improvement, famously tracking his progress against a list of thirteen virtues, and of practical wisdom, distilled in Poor Richard's Almanack. As a businessman he understood branding and industriousness; as a scientist he made real discoveries about electricity; as a diplomat he secured the French alliance that won American independence. His life is a manual for lifelong learning and reinvention.

Franklin embodies self-directed improvement and reinvention — building himself, through discipline and curiosity, into a master of business, science and statecraft. His practical wisdom about industry, thrift, learning and character remains among the most useful ever written.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Improve yourself systematically

Franklin tracked his progress against a list of virtues.

Do this: Make self-improvement a deliberate, tracked practice, not a vague intention.
Principle 2

Reinvent across fields

He moved from printer to scientist to statesman.

Do this: Use success in one field as a base to reinvent yourself in another.
Principle 3

Build civic and personal capital

He founded institutions and cultivated a trusted reputation.

Do this: Invest in institutions and reputation; they compound into influence.

In their words

An investment in knowledge pays the best interest.
Well done is better than well said.
Either write something worth reading or do something worth writing.

Frequently asked questions

What made Benjamin Franklin remarkable?

His self-made, polymathic reinvention — from printer and businessman to scientist, inventor, writer, and Founding Father — all achieved through relentless self-improvement and curiosity with little formal education.

What was Franklin's approach to self-improvement?

Deliberate and systematic — he famously tracked his progress against a list of thirteen virtues and distilled practical wisdom about industry, thrift and character in Poor Richard's Almanack.
