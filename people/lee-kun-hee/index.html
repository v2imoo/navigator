Lee Kun-hee

The chairman who demanded Samsung 'change everything except your wife and children' — and turned a cheap imitator into a world leader.

4 min read · Contemporary

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Lee Kun-hee, son of Samsung's founder, took over leadership of the group and drove the transformation that turned it from a maker of cheap, imitative electronics into a genuine world leader. His defining conviction was that competing forever on price as a fast-follower was a dead end, and that Samsung had to compete on quality, design and innovation at the highest level. In the 1990s he forced a wrenching cultural change on a company that was then succeeding, famously exhorting his managers to 'change everything except your wife and children' to capture the depth of the shift he demanded.

Under his leadership, Samsung invested massively in quality, design, research and manufacturing excellence, and climbed from imitator to leader across multiple categories — becoming a top maker of smartphones and televisions, and, most strategically, the world's leading maker of critical components like memory chips and displays. He built Samsung's distinctive position as a vertically integrated giant controlling the whole electronics stack, from the chip to the finished device.

His legacy is one of the great corporate transformations — proof that a company can deliberately remake itself from cheap follower into world leader through sustained investment and leadership conviction. His tenure was not without controversy, including the governance and legal issues that surround the Korean chaebol, but his role in transforming Samsung into a global technology power is central to its story.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Escape the fast-follower trap deliberately

Lee concluded that competing forever on price as a follower was a dead end, and drove Samsung to compete on quality and innovation.

Do this: Recognise the fast-follower trap and leap out of it before cheaper rivals squeeze you — it can be the key transformation.
Principle 2

Force transformation with conviction

His 'change everything except your wife and children' forced a wrenching shift on a succeeding company.

Do this: Deep transformation of a working company usually requires uncompromising conviction from the top, because organisations won't abandon a working formula willingly.
Principle 3

Invest to move up to quality and innovation

Samsung's leap required massive investment in quality, design, R&D and manufacturing.

Do this: Moving up from cheapness to genuine quality and innovation demands sustained investment, not just a change of ambition.
Principle 4

Build control of the whole stack

Lee built Samsung's leadership in critical components and its control of the whole electronics stack.

Do this: Owning the critical components and more of your stack can be a formidable, distinctive source of advantage.

In their words

Change everything except your wife and children.
Quality is the matter of survival; abandon the obsession with quantity.
