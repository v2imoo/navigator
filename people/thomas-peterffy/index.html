Thomas Peterffy

The refugee programmer who computerised the trading floor — and built a fortune automating what humans did by hand.

4 min read · 1977–present

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Thomas Peterffy fled communist Hungary with nothing, arrived in America speaking no English, taught himself computer programming, and became a pioneer who automated financial trading — building one of the great self-made technology fortunes. At a time when trading was done by shouting humans on chaotic exchange floors, he saw that computers could price options and execute trades far faster and more accurately than people, and he methodically replaced human judgement with algorithms.

Peterffy famously built handheld computers to use on the trading floor when exchanges banned them, and relentlessly automated every step, founding Interactive Brokers to bring low-cost electronic trading to the world. He was years ahead of an industry that eventually followed him entirely. He is a study in seeing automation's potential before others, in methodically replacing manual processes with technology, and in rising from nothing through self-taught skill and relentless logic.
