Vinod Khosla

The founder-turned-VC who backs audacious moonshots others call impossible.

4 min read · 1982–present

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Vinod Khosla co-founded Sun Microsystems, a landmark computing company, before becoming a venture capitalist known for backing the boldest, most ambitious bets in technology — clean energy, AI, biotech, food science — areas others consider too risky or too far out. He founded Khosla Ventures explicitly to fund audacious ideas with the potential to transform whole industries.

Khosla embraces a portfolio philosophy suited to moonshots: most bold bets will fail, but the rare success can be so enormous that it more than compensates. He argues that meaningful innovation comes from being willing to fail unconventionally, and that the biggest problems — and rewards — lie where others fear to invest.

Khosla represents venture capital's appetite for asymmetric bets — funding the improbable because the payoff from the rare success can reshape the world, and because the biggest opportunities live where the risk scares others away.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Pursue asymmetric bets

Khosla funds moonshots where rare wins dwarf many losses.

Do this: Seek bets where the downside is capped but the upside could be enormous.
Principle 2

Fund what others fear

He invests in areas others consider too risky or far-out.

Do this: The biggest opportunities often lie where conventional investors won't go.
Principle 3

Accept unconventional failure

Most bold bets fail; that's the price of transformative wins.

Do this: Be willing to fail unconventionally in pursuit of transformative success.

In their words

Most people won't try because they're afraid to fail.
The bigger the problem, the bigger the opportunity.
Failure doesn't matter; the magnitude of success does.
