Part I — The story

The story

Patrick Lencioni writes business fables — short, story-driven books that teach a single idea through narrative. His best-known, The Five Dysfunctions of a Team, lays out a pyramid of what undermines teams: absence of trust, fear of conflict, lack of commitment, avoidance of accountability, and inattention to results. His central thesis, developed in The Advantage, is that 'organisational health' — a cohesive, trusting, aligned leadership team — is the greatest untapped competitive advantage, more decisive than strategy, finance or technology.

Lencioni makes the human, interpersonal side of organisations practical and actionable. He is a study in teaching through story, in the foundational role of trust and healthy conflict in teams, and in the overlooked power of organisational health.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Build trust first

Lencioni's pyramid starts with vulnerability-based trust.

Do this: Everything else on a team rests on trust; without it, there's no honest conflict, commitment or accountability.
Principle 2

Embrace healthy conflict

He says fear of conflict quietly kills teams.

Do this: Encourage open, productive disagreement; teams that can't argue well can't commit to real decisions.
Principle 3

Prize organisational health

He calls it the greatest untapped advantage.

Do this: A cohesive, aligned, trusting leadership team beats clever strategy; invest in health as a competitive edge.

In their words

Not finance. Not strategy. Not technology. It is teamwork that remains the ultimate competitive advantage.
If you could get all the people in an organisation rowing in the same direction, you could dominate any industry.
Trust is the foundation of real teamwork.

Frequently asked questions

Who is Patrick Lencioni?

A business author known for teaching through fables, best known for The Five Dysfunctions of a Team, and a leading champion of 'organisational health' as the greatest untapped competitive advantage.

What are the five dysfunctions of a team?

Absence of trust, fear of conflict, lack of commitment, avoidance of accountability, and inattention to results — a pyramid in which each dysfunction builds on the one below it, starting with a lack of trust.
