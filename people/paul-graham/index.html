Paul Graham

The essayist-programmer who built the startup accelerator that reshaped how companies are funded.

4 min read · 1995–present

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Paul Graham co-founded Y Combinator, the accelerator that reinvented how early-stage startups are funded and launched — backing large batches of young companies with small cheques, intense mentorship, and a network that became one of the most valuable in technology. YC helped launch Airbnb, Stripe, Dropbox and hundreds of others.

A programmer who sold his first company to Yahoo, Graham became as influential through his essays as through YC. Written in plain, contrarian prose, they crystallised startup wisdom — 'make something people want', focus on growth, do things that don't scale — into ideas a generation of founders internalised.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Make something people want

Graham's core startup maxim, and YC's unofficial motto.

Do this: Before anything else, build something people genuinely want.
Principle 2

Do things that don't scale

Early on, founders should do unscalable things to win first users.

Do this: Manually delight your first users, even in ways that can't scale yet.
Principle 3

Focus on growth

Graham taught startups to orient around a single growth metric.

Do this: Pick the one growth number that matters and organise everything around it.

In their words

Make something people want.
Do things that don't scale.
A startup is a company designed to grow fast.
