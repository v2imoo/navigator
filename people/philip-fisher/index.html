Philip Fisher

He taught investors to buy great growing businesses and hold them for decades — and to do their homework by asking everyone.

4 min read · 1930s–1990s

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Philip Fisher was the great counterpoint and complement to Benjamin Graham: where Graham sought cheap statistical bargains, Fisher sought exceptional growing companies to own for the very long term. His 1958 book Common Stocks and Uncommon Profits laid out how to identify businesses with durable competitive advantages, able management and long runways — and his influence shows plainly in Warren Buffett, who described himself as '85% Graham and 15% Fisher'.

Fisher's signature method was 'scuttlebutt': gathering a mosaic of information by talking to a company's customers, suppliers, competitors and former employees, not just reading its reports. He held his best positions for decades. He is a study in buying quality over cheapness, in doing primary research beyond the filings, and in the patience to let a great business compound over a lifetime.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Buy quality, not just cheapness

Fisher sought outstanding growing businesses over statistical bargains.

Do this: Pay up for a genuinely superior business with a long runway; quality compounds in ways cheapness cannot.
Principle 2

Do the scuttlebutt

He built a research mosaic from customers, rivals and ex-staff.

Do this: Go beyond the filings — talk to the people around a business to learn what the numbers don't show.
Principle 3

Hold for the long term

His best investments were held for decades.

Do this: Once you own a great business, sit still; the biggest gains come from patience, not activity.

In their words

The stock market is filled with individuals who know the price of everything, but the value of nothing.
If the job has been correctly done, the time to sell is almost never.
I don't want a lot of good investments; I want a few outstanding ones.

Frequently asked questions

What is Philip Fisher known for?

Pioneering growth investing — buying exceptional growing companies to hold for the very long term — and the 'scuttlebutt' method of researching a business by talking to its customers, suppliers, competitors and former employees.

How did Fisher influence Buffett?

Buffett called himself '85% Graham and 15% Fisher'; Fisher's emphasis on buying wonderful growing businesses and holding them shaped Buffett's shift from pure bargain-hunting to quality investing.
