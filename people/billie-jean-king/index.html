Billie Jean King

She won the 'Battle of the Sexes' and then won the bigger fight — equal pay and respect for women in sport.

4 min read · 1960s–present

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Billie Jean King won 39 Grand Slam titles across singles, doubles and mixed, but her greatest impact came off the court. In 1973 she beat Bobby Riggs in the nationally televised 'Battle of the Sexes', a symbolic victory watched by 90 million that struck a blow against the idea that women's sport was inferior. That same year she founded the Women's Tennis Association and threatened to boycott the US Open until it offered equal prize money — which it became the first major to do.

King used her platform relentlessly for gender equality and LGBTQ rights, reshaping the economics and status of women's sport. She is a study in using athletic excellence as a platform for change, in organising collective power to force fairness, and in fighting battles whose stakes reach far beyond the game.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Use your platform for the bigger fight

King leveraged tennis fame for equality.

Do this: Excellence earns a platform; use it to change the system, not just to win within it.
Principle 2

Organise collective power

She founded the WTA to give players leverage.

Do this: Individual stardom is fragile; organising peers into collective power forces lasting change.
Principle 3

Win the symbolic battles

The Battle of the Sexes shifted public belief.

Do this: Some contests matter for what they prove, not the prize; win the ones that change how people think.

Frequently asked questions

Who is Billie Jean King?

A tennis champion with 39 Grand Slam titles who became a pioneering advocate for gender equality — winning the 1973 'Battle of the Sexes', founding the Women's Tennis Association, and securing equal prize money at the US Open.

What was the 'Battle of the Sexes'?

A 1973 televised match in which King beat Bobby Riggs before 90 million viewers — a symbolic victory against the notion that women's sport was inferior, and a landmark moment for gender equality.
