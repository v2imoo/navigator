Boyan Slat

At 18 he set out to rid the oceans of plastic — and turned a teenage TED talk into a global cleanup system.

4 min read · 2010s–present

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Boyan Slat, as an 18-year-old Dutch student, gave a TED talk proposing a passive system to let ocean currents concentrate floating plastic for collection, rather than chasing it with nets. He dropped out of university to found The Ocean Cleanup in 2013, raised funds, endured early engineering failures and public scepticism, and iterated through designs until systems were actually extracting plastic from the Great Pacific Garbage Patch — while also deploying 'Interceptors' in rivers to stop plastic before it reaches the sea.

Slat paired youthful audacity with hard engineering iteration and a systems view of the problem. He is a study in attacking a vast problem others called impossible, in persisting through visible early failure, and in tackling a crisis at both its symptom (ocean plastic) and its source (rivers).

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Attack the 'impossible' problem

Slat took on ocean plastic others wrote off.

Do this: Vast problems everyone calls impossible are wide open; youthful audacity plus engineering can make real dents.
Principle 2

Iterate through public failure

Early designs failed visibly; he kept improving.

Do this: Expect early, public failure on a hard moonshot; treat each as data and iterate rather than retreat.
Principle 3

Address symptom and source

He cleans the ocean and stops plastic in rivers.

Do this: Tackle both the symptom and the upstream source; solving only one leaves the problem regenerating.
