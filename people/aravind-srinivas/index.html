Aravind Srinivas

He bet that people would rather get answers than ten blue links — and built an AI to deliver them.

4 min read · 2020s–present

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Aravind Srinivas, an Indian-born AI researcher who worked at OpenAI and DeepMind, co-founded Perplexity AI in 2022 on a simple bet: that large language models could reinvent search by giving people direct, cited answers to their questions rather than a list of links to sift through. Perplexity grew rapidly into one of the most prominent AI-native products, positioning itself as an 'answer engine' and taking on the established giants of search.

Srinivas is known for emphasising accuracy and citations — grounding answers in sources to fight the hallucination problem — and for building fast in an intensely competitive field. He is a study in attacking an entrenched incumbent by reframing the problem (answers, not links), in making trust a feature through citation, and in the speed and focus required to carve out space against far larger players.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Reframe the incumbent's problem

Srinivas sells answers where search sells links.

Do this: To attack a dominant incumbent, change what the product is fundamentally for, rather than competing on their terms.
Principle 2

Make trust a feature

Perplexity cites its sources to combat hallucination.

Do this: When your category has a credibility problem, build verifiability into the product as a core differentiator.
Principle 3

Move fast against giants

He builds quickly in a field crowded with larger rivals.

Do this: Against bigger competitors, speed and focus on one sharp use case are among your few real advantages.
