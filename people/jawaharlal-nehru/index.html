Jawaharlal Nehru

He inherited a newborn, divided nation and gave it the institutions, science and democracy to endure.

4 min read · 1940s–1964

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Jawaharlal Nehru became independent India's first prime minister in 1947 and held the office until his death in 1964, shaping the character of the world's largest democracy at its most fragile moment. He championed parliamentary democracy, secularism, and a 'scientific temper', and built the foundational institutions — universities, research laboratories, dams, the Planning Commission and heavy industry — that he believed a modern nation required. He steered India through partition, integration and non-alignment in the Cold War.

Nehru's choices — a mixed economy, state-led industrialisation, democratic institutions — defined India for generations and remain debated. He is a study in institution-building as the real work of nation-founding, in holding to democratic and pluralist principles under enormous strain, and in planting the long-term foundations (science, education, infrastructure) whose payoff outlives the planter.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Build institutions, not just policies

Nehru's lasting legacy was the institutions he founded.

Do this: Policies change with leaders; institutions endure — invest your energy in building the structures that outlast you.
Principle 2

Plant for the long term

He funded science and education whose payoff came decades later.

Do this: Commit resources to foundations — education, research, infrastructure — that won't pay off on your watch but define the future.
Principle 3

Hold principles under strain

He defended democracy and pluralism through crisis.

Do this: The test of a principle is whether you keep it when it's costly; founding values must survive the hard early years.

Frequently asked questions

Who was Jawaharlal Nehru?

Independent India's first prime minister (1947–64), who shaped the world's largest democracy at its founding — championing parliamentary democracy, secularism and science, and building the institutions, industry and infrastructure of a modern nation.

What was Nehru's economic approach?

A mixed economy with state-led industrialisation and central planning, building heavy industry, dams, universities and research institutions — a model that defined India for generations and remains debated.
