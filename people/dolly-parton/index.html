Dolly Parton

The 'backwoods Barbie' who was underestimated all the way to an empire — owning her songs, her brand, and her terms.

4 min read · 1964–present

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Dolly Parton grew up one of twelve children in deep poverty in rural Tennessee and became one of the most beloved and successful entertainers in American history — but behind the flamboyant, down-home persona that led many to underestimate her is one of the shrewdest businesspeople in music. She wrote her own songs and fought to own them: famously, she declined to let Elvis Presley record 'I Will Always Love You' because it would have required giving up half the publishing rights — a decision that later earned her a fortune when Whitney Houston's version became a global hit.

Parton built a diversified empire — her song catalogue, the Dollywood theme park, production companies — and used her wealth for major philanthropy, including the Imagination Library that has given hundreds of millions of books to children, and a donation that helped fund a COVID vaccine. She is a study in being underestimated and using it, in the long-term value of owning your intellectual property, and in building a diversified business behind a beloved brand.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Own your intellectual property

Parton kept her song rights even when it meant turning down Elvis — and it made a fortune.

Do this: Hold onto the rights to what you create; ownership can be worth far more than a one-off deal.
Principle 2

Let them underestimate you

Her down-home persona hid a shrewd operator — to her advantage.

Do this: Being underestimated can be an advantage; let it work for you.
Principle 3

Diversify behind a beloved brand

She built a catalogue, a theme park, and production companies.

Do this: Extend a beloved brand into diversified, owned businesses.

Frequently asked questions

Why did Dolly Parton turn down Elvis?

Recording 'I Will Always Love You' would have required giving Elvis's management half the publishing rights. She said no to keep ownership — a decision that earned her a fortune when Whitney Houston's version became a global hit.

What makes Dolly Parton a shrewd businessperson?

Behind a flamboyant persona that led many to underestimate her, she wrote and owned her songs, built a diversified empire (catalogue, Dollywood, production), and used her wealth for major philanthropy.
