Charles Schwab

The founder who broke Wall Street's grip on investing — by cutting the commissions and respecting the customer.

4 min read · 1971–present

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Charles Schwab founded his brokerage in 1971 and, when the US deregulated stock-trading commissions in 1975, seized the moment to build the discount brokerage — letting ordinary people buy and sell stocks for a fraction of what full-service Wall Street firms charged. He stripped out the expensive, conflicted advice and high commissions, betting that millions of self-directed investors wanted low cost and respect rather than salesmanship.

Schwab relentlessly used technology — from early automated phone systems to one of the first major online trading platforms — to cut costs and empower customers, and he championed transparency and the customer's interest in an industry rife with conflicts. His firm helped bring investing to the masses and pressured the whole industry toward lower costs. He is a study in democratising a gatekept service, in competing on low cost and customer trust, and in riding deregulation and technology to upend incumbents.

The playbook

Do this: When rules change in your favour, move decisively before incumbents adapt.
Principle 3

Win on cost and trust
