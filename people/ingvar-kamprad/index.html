Ingvar Kamprad

The founder who made design affordable by rethinking every cost — including asking the customer to build the furniture.

4 min read · 1943–2018

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Ingvar Kamprad built IKEA into the world's largest furniture retailer by attacking cost at every point in the chain and passing the savings to customers as strikingly low prices. His most famous innovation — flat-pack furniture the customer assembles — came from the simple observation that shipping air is expensive; disassembled furniture ships cheaply and stores densely.

Raised frugal in rural Sweden, Kamprad made thrift a religion and a competitive weapon. He designed for cost from the start, sourced globally, and built vast self-service warehouse stores that turned shopping into an efficient, low-labour experience.

Kamprad's frugality was legendary — flying economy, reusing tea bags — and it set the culture. Every krona saved in operations became a lower price, which drove the volume that lowered costs further: a flywheel dressed in Swedish plainness.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Design for cost from the start

Kamprad designed products around a target price, engineering cost out at the source.

Do this: Set the price first, then design the product and process to hit it.
Principle 2

Shift work to the customer

Flat-pack and self-service cut shipping and labour — and customers accepted it for lower prices.

Do this: Where customers will trade effort for savings, let them do part of the work.
Principle 3

Make frugality the culture

Relentless thrift throughout operations funded the low prices that drove volume.

Do this: Build a cost-conscious culture so savings compound into lower prices and growth.

Frequently asked questions

Why does IKEA sell flat-pack furniture?

Because shipping assembled furniture means shipping air — expensive and bulky. Flat-pack ships cheaply and stores densely, cutting costs that become lower prices.

What drove IKEA's low prices?

Designing for cost from the start, shifting assembly and transport to customers, global sourcing, and a deeply frugal culture.
