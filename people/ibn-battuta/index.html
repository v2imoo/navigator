Ibn Battuta

Over 30 years he journeyed some 75,000 miles across the medieval world — and wrote down the whole of it.

4 min read · 14th century

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Ibn Battuta, a Moroccan scholar, set out at 21 on a pilgrimage to Mecca and did not stop travelling for some three decades — journeying an estimated 75,000 miles across North and West Africa, the Middle East, Central Asia, India, Southeast Asia and China, far more than any traveller of his age. He served as a judge, met rulers and ordinary people, and dictated his experiences in a famous travelogue, the Rihla, one of history's richest firsthand accounts of the 14th-century world.

His curiosity, adaptability and sheer endurance opened a vast, interconnected medieval world to later readers. He is a study in insatiable curiosity, in adaptability across wildly different cultures, and in the value of recording what you witness for those who come after.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Follow insatiable curiosity

Ibn Battuta kept going for three decades.

Do this: Deep curiosity, followed without limit, can take you further and teach you more than any fixed plan.
Principle 2

Adapt across cultures

He thrived among wildly different societies.

Do this: The ability to adapt, respect local customs and make yourself useful anywhere opens doors a rigid traveller never finds.
Principle 3

Record what you witness

His Rihla preserved a whole world.

Do this: Writing down what you observe turns personal experience into lasting knowledge for everyone who follows.

In their words

Travelling leaves you speechless, then turns you into a storyteller.

Frequently asked questions

Who was Ibn Battuta?

A 14th-century Moroccan scholar and the greatest traveller of the medieval world, who journeyed an estimated 75,000 miles across Africa, Asia and Europe over three decades and recorded it in his travelogue, the Rihla.

What is the Rihla?

Ibn Battuta's famous travelogue, dictated from his decades of journeys — one of history's richest firsthand accounts of the 14th-century world, its rulers, peoples and places.
