Indira Gandhi

A commanding, divisive leader who won a war and the Green Revolution — and imposed the Emergency.

4 min read · 1960s–1984

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Indira Gandhi, daughter of Jawaharlal Nehru, became India's first and so far only woman prime minister, serving across most of two decades and dominating its politics. Her record is powerful and deeply contested: she led India to a decisive victory in the 1971 war that created Bangladesh, oversaw the Green Revolution's gains in food self-sufficiency and India's first nuclear test — but also imposed the 1975–77 Emergency, suspending civil liberties and jailing opponents, the darkest chapter for Indian democracy. She was assassinated in 1984.

In the spirit of fair presentation: admirers credit her with decisiveness, strength and major achievements, while critics hold the Emergency and her centralising, authoritarian turn against her. She is a study in commanding political leadership, in the perils of concentrating power, and in a legacy that resists simple judgement.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Act decisively at the pivotal moment

Her 1971 war leadership was swift and resolute.

Do this: At genuine turning points, decisiveness and resolve can be decisive; hesitation can forfeit the moment.
Principle 2

Beware concentrating power

The Emergency is the cautionary core of her record.

Do this: Concentrating power, even with good intentions, risks grave abuse; strong institutions and checks matter most under strong leaders.
Principle 3

Expect a contested legacy

Her record mixes real achievement and real damage.

Do this: Powerful leaders rarely leave simple legacies; weigh the whole ledger honestly rather than flattening it either way.

Frequently asked questions

Who was Indira Gandhi?

India's first and only woman prime minister, who dominated its politics for nearly two decades — leading the decisive 1971 war that created Bangladesh, but also imposing the controversial 1975–77 Emergency; she was assassinated in 1984.

Why is Indira Gandhi's legacy contested?

Admirers credit her decisiveness and achievements like the 1971 victory and food self-sufficiency, while critics condemn the Emergency's suspension of civil liberties and her centralising, authoritarian turn.
