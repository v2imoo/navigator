Jony Ive

The designer who shaped the iPhone and iPod — proving design can be a company's deepest competitive edge.

4 min read · 1992–present

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Jony Ive, as Apple's chief design officer, shaped the products that defined modern technology — the iMac, iPod, iPhone, iPad and MacBook — in one of the great creative partnerships in business history alongside Steve Jobs. His obsessive pursuit of simplicity, craft and the marriage of form and function made industrial design a core source of Apple's value, not a decorative afterthought.

Ive's philosophy centres on reduction — stripping away until only the essential, beautiful and intuitive remains — and on caring deeply about details customers may never consciously notice. After leaving Apple in 2019 he founded the design firm LoveFrom, and in 2025 his AI-hardware venture io was acquired by OpenAI, with Ive taking on design leadership across the company as he and Sam Altman build a new family of AI devices — a striking echo of his partnership with Jobs, now at the frontier of AI.

Ive demonstrates that design can be a company's deepest competitive advantage — that obsessive craft, simplicity and care about unseen details create products people love and rivals struggle to match. His move to shape AI hardware with OpenAI puts that philosophy at the centre of the next technology wave.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Make design a core advantage

Ive proved design can be a company's deepest edge, not decoration.

Do this: Treat design and craft as a core source of value, not a finishing touch.
Principle 2

Reduce to the essential

His method is stripping away until only the essential remains.

Do this: Simplify relentlessly; remove until only what's essential and beautiful is left.
Principle 3

Care about unseen details

He obsessed over details customers may never consciously notice.

Do this: Sweat the details others ignore; the care shows even when it's invisible.
