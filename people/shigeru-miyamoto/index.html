Shigeru Miyamoto

He turned video games into an art form of pure joy — the Walt Disney of a new medium.

4 min read · 1980s–present

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Shigeru Miyamoto is the most celebrated video-game designer in history, the creative force at Nintendo behind Donkey Kong, Super Mario Bros., The Legend of Zelda and more — franchises that have sold hundreds of millions of copies and shaped the entire medium. He approached game design as the crafting of joy and discovery, drawing on childhood memories of exploring caves and countryside around his home to create worlds that invite wonder and play.

Miyamoto is famous for prioritising fun and feel above technology or spectacle, for endless iteration until a game 'feels' right, and for his belief that a delayed game can eventually be good but a rushed game is bad forever. He is a study in designing for emotion and delight, in relentless iteration toward the right feel, and in drawing creative fuel from one's own sense of wonder.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Design for the feeling

Miyamoto chases fun and delight above all.

Do this: Optimise for how the experience feels to the user, not for technical specs or spectacle; emotion is the product.
Principle 2

Iterate until it feels right

He refines endlessly until the play is perfect.

Do this: Prototype and tune relentlessly; the gap between good and magical is closed by iteration, not by the first idea.
Principle 3

Don't ship it rushed

He'd rather delay than release something not ready.

Do this: A delayed product can become great; a rushed one stays flawed forever — protect quality over the deadline when it counts.

Frequently asked questions

Who is Shigeru Miyamoto?

The legendary Nintendo designer behind Donkey Kong, Super Mario Bros. and The Legend of Zelda — among the most influential video-game creators in history, often called the medium's Walt Disney.

What is Miyamoto's design philosophy?

Prioritise fun and feeling above technology and spectacle, iterate endlessly until a game feels right, and never ship something rushed — 'a delayed game is eventually good, but a rushed game is forever bad'.
