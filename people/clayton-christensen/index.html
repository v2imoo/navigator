Clayton Christensen

The scholar who explained why great companies fail — by doing everything right.

Part I — The story

The story

Clayton Christensen answered one of business's deepest puzzles: why do successful, well-managed companies, doing everything the textbooks say, so often get destroyed by inferior newcomers? His theory of 'disruptive innovation', laid out in The Innovator's Dilemma, showed that incumbents fail precisely because they listen to their best customers and focus on their most profitable products — ignoring cheap, simple, 'inferior' innovations that start at the bottom of the market and then improve until they upend everything.

The insight was that good management — serving existing customers, protecting margins — is exactly what blinds incumbents to disruptive threats. Christensen also championed 'jobs to be done' — understanding the real job a customer hires a product to do. Late in life, in How Will You Measure Your Life?, he applied the same rigorous thinking to living well. He is a study in why success breeds vulnerability, in the mechanics of disruption, and in applying clear theory to both business and life.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Watch the bottom of the market

Christensen showed disruption starts with cheap, 'inferior' products at the low end.

Do this: Watch cheap, simple entrants at the bottom; they improve and rise to upend you.
Principle 2

Beware your own best customers

Listening only to top customers blinds incumbents to disruption.

Do this: Serving your most profitable customers can blind you to the threat that will kill you.
Principle 3

Find the real job to be done

Understand the job a customer actually hires your product to do.

Do this: Ask what job the customer is really hiring your product to do, not just its features.

In their words

Disruptive technologies look like bad ideas to the leaders of incumbent firms.
Customers don't buy products; they hire them to do a job.
It's easier to hold your principles 100% of the time than 98% of the time.

Frequently asked questions

What is disruptive innovation?

Christensen's theory of how inferior, cheap, simple newcomers start at the bottom of a market and improve until they upend established companies — which fail precisely because good management keeps them focused on their best customers and margins.

What is 'jobs to be done'?

Christensen's framework for understanding the real job a customer 'hires' a product to do — focusing on the underlying need rather than the product's features or the customer's demographics.
