Lee Kuan Yew

The founder of modern Singapore, who turned a tiny, resource-poor island into a global powerhouse.

4 min read · 1959–2015

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Lee Kuan Yew took Singapore — a tiny island with no natural resources, ejected from Malaysia in 1965 and surrounded by larger neighbours — and, in a single generation, transformed it into one of the wealthiest, cleanest, safest and most efficient nations on earth. It is one of the most remarkable feats of nation-building in modern history, and he approached it much as a founder approaches a startup.

Lee governed with ruthless pragmatism and long-term thinking: he prioritised what worked over ideology, invested obsessively in education and clean governance, attracted foreign investment, and built institutions and a meritocracy that made Singapore a hub. His methods were sometimes authoritarian and controversial, but his clarity of strategy, incorruptibility and relentless focus on results made him one of the most studied leaders by founders and policymakers alike.

Lee Kuan Yew is a case study in strategic, long-term leadership — building an enduring institution from almost nothing through pragmatism, obsessive investment in people and governance, and a relentless focus on what actually produces results rather than what sounds good.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Choose what works over ideology

Lee prioritised results and pragmatism above any doctrine.

Do this: Judge decisions by what actually works, not by ideology or fashion.
Principle 2

Think in generations

He built for the long term, investing in education and institutions.

Do this: Make decisions on a decades-long horizon, not the next cycle.
Principle 3

Build clean, meritocratic institutions

Incorruptible governance and merit underpinned Singapore's rise.

Do this: Build institutions on merit and integrity; they compound into lasting advantage.
