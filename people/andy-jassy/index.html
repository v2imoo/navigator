Andy Jassy

The operator who built the cloud business that powers the internet — then took the helm of all Amazon.

4 min read · 1997–present

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Andy Jassy built Amazon Web Services from an idea into the dominant force in cloud computing — the infrastructure that runs a vast share of the internet and, for years, the engine of nearly all of Amazon's profits. Starting as Jeff Bezos's technical assistant (a 'shadow' role), he grasped early that Amazon's own hard-won infrastructure could be rented to others, and built AWS into a business so large and profitable it transformed the company. In 2021 he succeeded Bezos as CEO of all of Amazon.

Jassy's achievement with AWS is a landmark case of recognising that a capability you built for yourself can become a bigger business than your original one — selling the 'picks and shovels' of the digital economy. As CEO he has had to steward Amazon's enormous, diverse empire and its culture of customer obsession and long-term thinking. He is a study in turning internal infrastructure into a dominant external business, and in deep operational stewardship at vast scale.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Sell what you built for yourself

Jassy saw Amazon's own infrastructure could be rented to others — and built AWS.

Do this: A capability you built internally may be a bigger business sold externally.
Principle 2

Own the picks and shovels

AWS provides the infrastructure the digital economy runs on.

Do this: Owning the essential infrastructure others build on is a dominant, durable position.
Principle 3

Steward at scale

As CEO he maintains Amazon's customer obsession across a vast empire.

Do this: At huge scale, disciplined stewardship of culture and principles is the core job.

In their words

There is no compression algorithm for experience.
If you're going to invent, you're going to disrupt.
You have to be willing to be misunderstood for long periods of time.

Frequently asked questions

What did Andy Jassy build?

Amazon Web Services (AWS) — the dominant force in cloud computing that runs much of the internet and became the engine of nearly all of Amazon's profits — by recognising that Amazon's own infrastructure could be rented to others.

What's the lesson of AWS?

That a capability you build for yourself can become a bigger business than your original one — selling the 'picks and shovels' of the digital economy, an infrastructure position that's dominant and durable.
