David Swensen

He reinvented how institutions invest — and turned Yale's endowment into the model the whole world copied.

4 min read · 1980s–2021

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

David Swensen managed Yale University's endowment for over three decades and, in doing so, rewrote the rules of institutional investing. He pioneered the 'Yale Model': diversifying heavily away from domestic stocks and bonds into illiquid, inefficient asset classes — private equity, venture capital, real assets, absolute return — where skilled managers and a long time horizon could earn superior returns. Yale's endowment grew from about $1 billion to over $30 billion, one of the best institutional records anywhere.

Swensen was also a generous teacher; he trained a generation of chief investment officers who went on to run other great endowments, and wrote Unconventional Success advising ordinary investors to do the opposite of what he did institutionally — keep it simple, low-cost and index-based. He is a study in exploiting a genuine structural edge (a long horizon), in diversification done with conviction, and in giving different advice to those without your advantages.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Exploit your real structural edge

Swensen used Yale's permanent horizon to own illiquid assets.

Do this: Identify the structural advantage only you have — time, scale, mandate — and build your strategy around it.
Principle 2

Diversify with conviction

He moved boldly into unconventional asset classes.

Do this: Diversification isn't timidity; concentrate into several genuinely different return sources you understand.
Principle 3

Give advice fit to the audience

He told ordinary investors to index, not to copy Yale.

Do this: What works with your advantages may ruin someone without them; tailor advice to the other person's situation.

In their words

Establishing a well-diversified portfolio of risky assets provides the foundation for superior returns.
The fiduciary's job is to serve the institution, not the ego.
Investors who stick to low-cost index funds will beat the vast majority of professionals.

Frequently asked questions

What is the 'Yale Model'?

David Swensen's approach of diversifying an endowment away from domestic stocks and bonds into illiquid, inefficient asset classes — private equity, venture, real assets — exploiting a long time horizon to earn superior returns.

What did Swensen advise ordinary investors?

The opposite of what he did at Yale — keep it simple with low-cost index funds, since individuals lack the institutional advantages that make the Yale Model work.
