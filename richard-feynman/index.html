Richard Feynman

The physicist who insisted on understanding from first principles — and never fooling yourself.

4 min read · 1942–1988

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Richard Feynman won a Nobel Prize for quantum electrodynamics, but became legendary for something broader: a way of thinking. He insisted on understanding things from first principles, distrusted jargon and authority, and had an unmatched gift for cutting through complexity to the simple heart of a problem — famously demonstrating the cause of the Challenger disaster with a glass of ice water and a rubber ring.

Feynman's principles — that you must not fool yourself, and you are the easiest person to fool; that if you can't explain something simply you don't really understand it; that real knowledge means grasping the thing, not just its name — are as valuable to any thinker as to a physicist. His curiosity, playfulness and intellectual honesty made him a model of how to think clearly.

Feynman is a patron saint of clear thinking. His insistence on genuine understanding over the appearance of it, on intellectual honesty above all, and on the joy of figuring things out, is wisdom that reaches far beyond physics into every field of decision and discovery.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Don't fool yourself

Feynman warned you are the easiest person to fool — demand real understanding.

Do this: Test whether you truly understand, not whether you feel you do.
Principle 2

Explain it simply

If you can't teach it plainly, you don't really grasp it.

Do this: Explain an idea in simple terms; the gaps reveal what you don't understand.
Principle 3

Know the thing, not the name

He distinguished knowing a name from understanding a thing.

Do this: Seek to understand how something actually works, not just its label.

Frequently asked questions

What is the 'Feynman Technique'?

A method for genuine understanding: explain a concept in simple terms as if teaching it; wherever you get stuck or vague reveals what you don't actually understand and must revisit.

Why is Feynman valuable beyond physics?

His principles — don't fool yourself, understand from first principles, know the thing rather than its name, prize intellectual honesty — are a model of clear thinking useful in any field.
