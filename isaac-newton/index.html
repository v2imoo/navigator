Isaac Newton

The scientist who unified the heavens and earth under a few universal laws — and invented the mathematics to prove it.

4 min read · 1665–1727

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Isaac Newton is among the most influential scientists in history. He formulated the three laws of motion and the law of universal gravitation, showing that the same simple rules govern a falling apple and the orbits of planets — unifying the heavens and the earth under a handful of universal principles. To do it, he co-invented calculus, the mathematics required to describe change itself.

Newton's power was reductive genius: finding the few underlying laws from which enormous complexity follows. His Principia Mathematica set the template for all of physical science. Famously reclusive and difficult, he also spent years on alchemy and theology, a reminder that even the greatest minds are human. His acknowledgement of 'standing on the shoulders of giants' captures how knowledge compounds across generations.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Find the few underlying laws

Newton reduced vast complexity to a handful of universal principles.

Do this: Search for the small set of fundamental rules that generate the complexity you see.
Principle 2

Build the tools you need

He co-invented calculus because the problem demanded it.

Do this: When existing tools can't solve the problem, build the ones that can.
Principle 3

Stand on giants' shoulders

Newton credited those before him; knowledge compounds.

Do this: Build on the accumulated work of others rather than starting from nothing.

In their words

Frequently asked questions

What did Isaac Newton discover?

The three laws of motion and universal gravitation — showing the same rules govern a falling apple and planetary orbits — and he co-invented calculus, the mathematics needed to describe change.

What made Newton's approach so powerful?

Reductive genius: finding the few fundamental laws from which enormous complexity follows, and building the mathematical tools required to prove them — the template for all physical science.
