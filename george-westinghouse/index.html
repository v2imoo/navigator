George Westinghouse

The inventor-industrialist who made railroads safe and, with Tesla, brought AC power to the world.

4 min read · 1869–1914

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

George Westinghouse was both a prolific inventor and a capable industrialist — the combination Tesla lacked. His railway air brake made trains dramatically safer and became universal, and he went on to champion alternating current, backing Nikola Tesla and defeating Edison's direct current in the 'War of the Currents' to electrify the modern world.

Where many inventors failed at business, Westinghouse built enduring companies around his inventions, held hundreds of patents, and treated his workers unusually well for the era. He had the rare ability to both create breakthrough technology and build the organisations to manufacture, sell and sustain it — turning invention into lasting industry.

Westinghouse is the model of the inventor who is also a builder — the counterpoint to Tesla. He proves that capturing the value of invention requires the discipline to build and run the enterprise around it, not just the genius to create.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Be inventor and builder

Westinghouse both created technology and built the companies to deliver it.

Do this: Pair invention with the organisation-building that turns it into lasting industry.
Principle 2

Back the right technology

He championed AC when its superiority was still contested.

Do this: Have the conviction to back the technically superior option through the fight.
Principle 3

Build durable institutions

His companies outlasted him, unlike many inventors' ventures.

Do this: Build organisations designed to sustain and scale the invention over time.

Frequently asked questions

What did George Westinghouse invent?

Most famously the railway air brake, which made trains safe and became universal — and he held over 300 patents while championing alternating-current (AC) power with Tesla.

How did Westinghouse differ from Tesla?

He was both inventor and industrialist — he built enduring companies to manufacture and sustain his inventions, capturing their value, where the brilliant Tesla repeatedly failed at business.
