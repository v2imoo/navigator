Licensing

Own the intellectual property; let others pay to use it.

Contents

Overview

The licensing model earns revenue by granting others the right to use intellectual property — a patent, brand, character, technology or design — in exchange for fees or royalties. The owner doesn't manufacture or sell the end product; it monetises the IP itself, at very high margin.

Because the marginal cost of licensing IP again is near zero, the model scales cleanly. The value depends entirely on the strength and defensibility of the underlying IP.

How it works

Step 1

Create or acquire valuable, defensible intellectual property.

Step 2

Grant others the right to use it under licence, for a fee or royalty.

Step 3

Earn recurring, high-margin income while others bear the cost of production.

Where you see it

Brand & character licensing

Owners earn royalties as others make the actual products.

Technology IP

Chip and software designs licensed to manufacturers.

When it works

  • The IP is genuinely valuable and legally defensible.
  • Others need it and can't easily replicate or design around it.
  • Licensing scales without proportional cost to the owner.

When it fails

  • The IP is weak, expiring, or easy to design around.
  • Enforcement is costly and infringement is rampant.
  • Licensees build their own alternative and stop paying.

Frequently asked questions

How is licensing different from franchising?

Franchising licenses a whole business system and brand to run outlets; licensing grants the right to use a specific piece of IP — a patent, brand or design — in someone else's product.

Why can licensing be so profitable?

Because the marginal cost of licensing the same IP again is near zero, so revenue scales with almost no added cost — as long as the IP stays defensible.
