Edwin Land

The inventor of instant photography whose fusion of science and art shaped Steve Jobs.

4 min read · 1932–1982

Contents

Part I — The story

The story

Edwin Land invented instant photography and built Polaroid into one of the most admired technology companies of its era — a rare fusion of deep science and consumer magic. Holding hundreds of patents, Land believed in pursuing 'the impossible' and in the idea that great products live at the intersection of science and art, a philosophy that directly inspired Steve Jobs.

Land ran Polaroid as an inventor's company, betting on breakthrough products that delighted people rather than incremental improvements, and refusing to be limited by market research — he believed you had to show people what they didn't yet know they wanted. His instant camera was pure wonder made practical. His fortunes later faded, but his influence on how visionary technologists think endures.

Land is the bridge between the inventor and the modern product visionary. His belief that the best products marry science and art, and that you lead demand rather than follow it, became core to Steve Jobs and the culture of Apple.

Part II — The playbook

The playbook

Principle 1

Marry science and art

Land built products at the intersection of deep technology and human delight.

Do this: Combine genuine technical breakthrough with design that delights — not one alone.
Principle 2

Lead demand

He believed you show people what they don't yet know they want.

Do this: Have the conviction to create products people can't yet ask for.
Principle 3

Pursue the 'impossible'

Land bet on breakthroughs, not incremental improvement.

Do this: Aim for breakthrough products rather than safe, incremental ones.

In their words

Don't undertake a project unless it is manifestly important and nearly impossible.
The bottom line is in heaven.
Marketing is what you do when your product is no good.

Frequently asked questions

What did Edwin Land invent?

Instant photography — the Polaroid camera — along with hundreds of other patented innovations in optics and light, building Polaroid into a celebrated technology company.

How did Land influence Steve Jobs?

Jobs cited Land as a hero for his belief that the best products live at the intersection of science and art, and that you lead demand by showing people what they don't yet know they want.
