Chip War

How semiconductors became the most critical technology on earth — and the fight to control them.

by Chris Miller · 2022

Contents

Summary

Chris Miller tells the history of the semiconductor — the tiny chip that powers everything — as a story of technology, business and geopolitics. It explains how a handful of companies came to control choke points in the most important supply chain in the world, and why nations now treat chips as a matter of national security.

It's essential context for understanding NVIDIA's rise and the stakes of the AI era: whoever controls advanced chips and the tools to make them holds extraordinary leverage.

Big ideas

Choke points

A few firms control indispensable steps in the chip supply chain.

Compounding complexity

Chipmaking grew so hard that only a handful can do it at the frontier.

Geopolitics of technology

Chips are now central to national security and trade conflict.

Ecosystems win

Advantage comes from controlling tools and ecosystems, not just products.

Key lessons

  • Control of a choke point confers enormous leverage.
  • Extreme complexity is itself a barrier to entry.
  • Technology and geopolitics are now inseparable.
  • Ecosystems and tools out-compete standalone products.

Connections

  • NVIDIA
  • Cornered resource
  • Network economies

Frequently asked questions

Why are semiconductors so strategic?

Because nearly all modern technology depends on them, and only a few firms and nations can make the most advanced ones — giving them huge economic and military leverage.

How does it relate to AI?

Advanced AI depends on cutting-edge chips; controlling that supply chain, as NVIDIA does on the design side, is central to the AI era.
