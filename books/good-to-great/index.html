Good to Great

What separates companies that make the leap to greatness from those that stay merely good.

by Jim Collins · 2001

Contents

Summary

Jim Collins and his team studied companies that transformed from good to sustained great performance and asked what they shared. The answer wasn't charisma or luck but a set of disciplined patterns — humble but fiercely determined leaders, getting the right people first, and confronting brutal facts.

Its famous concepts — Level 5 Leadership, First Who Then What, the Hedgehog Concept, and the Flywheel — have become part of the standard business vocabulary, though the book invites healthy debate about survivorship and causation.

Big ideas

Level 5 leadership

The best leaders blend deep humility with intense professional will.

First who, then what

Get the right people on board before deciding the strategy.

The Hedgehog Concept

Focus on the intersection of what you're best at, passionate about, and can be paid for.

The flywheel

Greatness comes from consistent pushes that compound, not one dramatic move.

Key lessons

  • Great leadership is humble and relentless, not flashy.
  • People decisions come before strategy.
  • Focus on your hedgehog: the one thing you can be best at.
  • Momentum compounds from disciplined, consistent effort.

Frequently asked questions

What's the Hedgehog Concept?

The intersection of what you can be best in the world at, what drives your economics, and what you're deeply passionate about — focus there.

Are the book's conclusions proven?

They're influential but debated — critics note survivorship bias, so treat the concepts as useful lenses rather than laws.
