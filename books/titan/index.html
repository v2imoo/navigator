Biography

Titan

The life of John D. Rockefeller — how one man organised chaos into a monopoly, then reinvented philanthropy.

by Ron Chernow · 1998

Contents

Summary

Ron Chernow's definitive biography of John D. Rockefeller portrays a man of contradictions: a devout, abstemious bookkeeper who built the most ruthless monopoly in American history, and then became its most consequential philanthropist. It's a study of how discipline, cost obsession and cold rationality can build — and how the same traits can justify questionable means.

The book traces Standard Oil's rise through relentless efficiency and hardball tactics, the antitrust reckoning that broke it up, and Rockefeller's second act giving away his fortune with the same systematic mind he used to earn it.

Big ideas

Order out of chaos

Rockefeller won by imposing stability and scale on a volatile industry.

Cost obsession

Driving costs below every rival's let Standard Oil outlast and absorb them.

The ruthless means

Secret rebates and predatory pricing built the monopoly — and provoked antitrust law.

Systematic philanthropy

He gave at scale to root causes and institutions, inventing the modern foundation.

Key lessons

  • Control costs and the chokepoints of an industry.
  • Turn volatility into a chance to consolidate.
  • Great success and questionable means can coexist — judge both.
  • Give like an investor: fund causes and institutions, measure results.

Connections

Frequently asked questions

Is Titan critical or admiring?

Both, honestly — Chernow neither excuses the monopolistic tactics nor dismisses the genuine achievements and later philanthropy.

Why read it today?

For a masterclass in cost advantage, industry consolidation, and the ethics of great business power.
