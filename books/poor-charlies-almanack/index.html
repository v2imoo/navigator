Investing

Poor Charlie's Almanack

Charlie Munger's worldly wisdom — a latticework of mental models for thinking clearly.

by Charlie Munger (ed. Peter Kaufman) · 2005

Contents

Summary

A collection of Charlie Munger's speeches and talks, this book is less about investing than about thinking. Munger's central idea is that to make good decisions you need a 'latticework' of mental models drawn from many disciplines — psychology, economics, physics, biology — rather than the single hammer of your own specialism.

It's also a catalogue of the ways human judgement goes wrong. Munger's talk on the psychology of misjudgement is a tour of the biases that lead smart people to foolish conclusions, and a manual for avoiding them, largely through inversion: figure out how you'd fail, and don't do that.

Big ideas

Latticework of models

Carry a few dozen big models from different fields and use them together.

Psychology of misjudgement

Learn the standard causes of human error — incentives, bias, social proof — to sidestep them.

Inversion

Solve problems backward: study how to guarantee failure, then avoid it.

Circle of competence

Know the edge of what you understand and stay within it.

Key lessons

  • Draw models from many disciplines, not just your own.
  • Study the biases that fool clever people.
  • Invert problems to find what to avoid.
  • Incentives quietly shape almost all behaviour.

Connections

  • Warren Buffett
  • Inversion
  • Incentives
  • Circle of competence

Frequently asked questions

What is Munger's 'latticework'?

A set of mental models from many disciplines held well enough that the right one comes to mind for a given problem — the opposite of one-track thinking.

Do I need to invest to benefit?

No — most of the book is about thinking and avoiding error, useful in any field.
