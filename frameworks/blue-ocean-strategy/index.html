Blue Ocean Strategy

Stop fighting in bloody 'red oceans' — create uncontested new market space.

Contents

Overview

Blue Ocean Strategy distinguishes 'red oceans' — existing, crowded markets where rivals fight over the same demand until the water turns bloody — from 'blue oceans', new uncontested market space created by redefining the offering. Instead of beating the competition, you make it irrelevant by serving demand no one else is.

The move usually comes from breaking the industry's assumed trade-off between value and cost: raising the factors the industry under-serves while eliminating the ones customers don't actually value, so you offer more of what matters at a lower cost.

When to use it

A market is saturated and competing head-on is a race to the bottom.

How to use it

Step 1

Map the red ocean

Identify the factors your industry competes on and where everyone clusters.

Step 2

Question the trade-offs

Challenge the assumed choice between differentiation and low cost.

Step 3

Eliminate & reduce

Cut or reduce factors the industry over-invests in but customers don't value.

Step 4

Raise & create

Boost under-served factors and create new ones the industry never offered.

Step 5

Open the blue ocean

The new combination attracts non-customers and sidesteps rivalry.

Worked example

A budget airline ignores the industry's assumptions — no assigned seats, no meals, no hubs, one aircraft type — eliminating cost while raising frequency and price simplicity. It doesn't win the existing air-travel fight; it converts people who'd have driven or not travelled at all, creating demand competitors weren't serving.

Common pitfalls

  • Assuming a blue ocean stays uncontested — success invites imitators, turning it red.
  • Creating 'new' space no one actually wants (novelty without demand).
  • Neglecting the cost side — differentiation alone isn't the strategy.

Frequently asked questions

What's a red ocean vs a blue ocean?

A red ocean is an existing, crowded market where rivals fight over the same demand. A blue ocean is new, uncontested space you create by redefining what you offer.

How do you create a blue ocean?

By breaking the industry's assumed value-cost trade-off — eliminating and reducing factors customers don't value, while raising and creating ones they do, to reach non-customers.
