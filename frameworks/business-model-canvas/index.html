Business Model Canvas

Sketch an entire business model on one page across nine building blocks.

Contents

Overview

The Business Model Canvas captures how a business creates, delivers and captures value on a single page of nine blocks: customer segments, value propositions, channels, customer relationships, revenue streams, key resources, key activities, key partnerships, and cost structure. It replaces a long business plan with one shared visual anyone can grasp and critique.

Its strength is systems thinking made visible: change one block and you can see the knock-on effects on the others, so it's as much a tool for stress-testing a model as for describing one.

When to use it

Designing, describing, or challenging a business model — for a startup or an existing company.

How to use it

Step 1

Customer segments & value proposition

Who you serve, and the specific value you offer each group — the heart of the model.

Step 2

Channels & relationships

How you reach customers and the kind of relationship you keep with them.

Step 3

Revenue streams

How and for what the customer actually pays.

Step 4

Key resources, activities & partners

What you need, do, and rely on others for to deliver the value.

Step 5

Cost structure

The major costs of running it — then check revenue exceeds them.

Worked example

A founder maps a meal-kit idea on the canvas. Filling in 'key partnerships' and 'cost structure' exposes that logistics and food waste will dominate costs — a problem the value proposition box never revealed. Seeing the whole model on one page turns a vague idea into a set of concrete, testable assumptions before a cent is spent.

Common pitfalls

  • Filling every box with optimism instead of testable assumptions.
  • Treating it as a one-off document rather than a living, revised model.
  • Neglecting the fit between blocks — a great value prop with a broken cost structure still fails.

Frequently asked questions

What are the nine blocks?

Customer segments, value propositions, channels, customer relationships, revenue streams, key resources, key activities, key partnerships, and cost structure.

Why use a canvas instead of a business plan?

It fits on one page, makes the whole model visible at once, and lets you see how changing one part affects the others — far faster to iterate than a long document.
