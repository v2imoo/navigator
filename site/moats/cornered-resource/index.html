Moat

Cornered Resource

A business has a cornered resource when it has preferential access to a coveted asset — a patent, a person, a deposit, a location, an exclusive deal — that others want but cannot obtain on equal terms. The asset itself does the defending.

The core idea

Sometimes the advantage is simply having the thing no one else can get. It might be a patent that legally excludes rivals, a mineral deposit or piece of land, an exclusive supply or distribution agreement, or a uniquely talented individual or team. The test is strict: the resource must be genuinely coveted (it produces real value), and access must be preferential and enduring — obtained on terms competitors can't match, and not easily bid away.

Why it's durable

A true cornered resource is protected by something outside ordinary competition: law (patents, licenses), geography (a location or deposit), or a binding relationship (an exclusive contract). Because rivals can't simply pay to acquire the same access, the advantage holds — for as long as the protection holds.

The hard cases

  • Talent is the trickiest: a brilliant person can be a cornered resource, but only if their value is truly non-transferable and they stay. If they can be hired away, the resource wasn't cornered.
  • Deals and licenses count only while exclusive and hard to replicate; a contract everyone can sign is not a moat.
  • Patents corner a resource until they expire or are designed around.

How to build and test it

  • Secure exclusivity, then durability. Getting the asset is half the job; keeping preferential access is the other half.
  • Ask whether it's really cornered: could a competitor acquire the same access by paying market price? If yes, it isn't a moat.
  • Convert it while it lasts. Cornered resources with expiry dates (patents, contracts) should fund a more permanent power before they lapse.

The limit

Most cornered resources are time-bounded — patents expire, contracts renew, star talent leaves, deposits deplete. The rare exceptions are those protected by permanent structural facts. Treat a cornered resource as a valuable head start to be converted, not an eternal fortress.
