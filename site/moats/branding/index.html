Moat

Branding

A business has a branding moat when customers will pay more for an objectively identical product because of who makes it. The brand carries meaning — trust, status, reassurance — that the product alone does not, and that meaning commands a durable premium.

The core idea

Two products can be materially the same and yet sell for very different prices, because one carries a brand the customer values. Branding as a moat is specifically this: the ability to charge more for the same thing, sustained over time. It works through two channels — affinity (the good feelings, identity, or status a brand confers) and certainty (the reassurance of a known quantity, which lowers the customer's perceived risk). A luxury house sells affinity; a trusted staple sells certainty; the strongest brands sell both.

Why it's durable

A brand is built slowly, through years of consistent delivery and association, and that history can't be bought or copied quickly. A competitor can match your product overnight but cannot manufacture decades of trust or a hard-won identity. The premium persists as long as the brand keeps its promise — which is why consistency, not cleverness, is the discipline that protects it.

Where it shows up

Luxury goods, consumer staples, and any category where the customer is uncertain and the cost of a bad choice feels high. It's strongest where the purchase is emotional or risky and weakest where buyers are coldly price-rational and fully informed.

How to build and test it

  • Pick one meaning and hold it for decades. Brand power comes from relentless consistency of identity, not from chasing trends.
  • Protect scarcity and quality. Every cheapened product or off-brand extension spends down the premium.
  • Test it: would customers pay more for your product than for an identical unbranded one? By how much, and would they still next year? That premium is the moat.

The limit

Brand is powerful but fragile: it erodes quickly if the promise is broken, if quality slips, or if the brand is over-extended into places it doesn't belong. And a premium that rests on nothing real — style without substance — collapses the moment customers notice.
