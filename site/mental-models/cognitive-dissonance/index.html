Cognitive Dissonance

CD
Psychology & Behavior

between beliefs and actions drive rationalisation over change.

Overview

between beliefs and actions drive rationalisation over change.

Why it matters

Good thinking is mostly a matter of reaching for the right lens at the right moment. Cognitive Dissonance earns its place in the toolkit because it changes what you notice: it reframes a familiar situation so the important part becomes obvious and the noise falls away.

How to apply it

  1. Name the situation. Write down the decision or problem where Cognitive Dissonance might apply.
  2. Run it through the lens. Ask the one question this idea forces you to ask.
  3. Act on what changes. If the lens changed your answer, change your plan — that’s the whole point.

Common pitfalls

  • Using it as a label instead of a tool — naming the model isn’t the same as applying it.
  • Reaching for it everywhere; every lens has a domain where it stops being useful.
  • Stopping at insight instead of changing a decision.
