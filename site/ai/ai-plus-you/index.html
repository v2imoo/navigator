Artificial Intelligence

AI + You

You and AI as a team — agents, projects, and agentic workflows that hold more context, move faster, and tackle complex research and output together.

The idea

The second arm is collaboration, not delegation. You bring judgment, taste, intent, and accountability; AI brings breadth, speed, and tirelessness. Working together — through agents, structured projects, and agentic workflows — you can hold a larger context and reach outcomes neither of you could reach alone. This is less “ask a question, get an answer” and more “run a process, supervise the work.”

From prompts to agents

A prompt is a single turn. An agent is a system that plans, uses tools, checks its own work, and carries a task across many steps toward a goal. The shift changes your job: you stop typing every instruction and start setting direction and guarding checkpoints. You define the objective, the constraints, and the moments where a human must sign off — and the agent does the legwork in between.

Projects & accumulating context

The biggest unlock is structuring work as an ongoing project rather than a cold chat. When context accumulates — your documents, data, decisions, and house style — the collaboration compounds: it stops re-explaining and starts building on what came before. Treat context like an asset you invest in, not something you retype each session.

Complex research & output

This is where the pairing changes what's possible for one person or a small team: cross-reference dozens of sources, synthesise them, model scenarios, and produce first-draft deliverables at a scale that used to require a department. Your role is to frame the question sharply, judge the quality, and own the result — the leverage is enormous, but so is the cost of shipping something you didn't check.

The operating rules

  1. Human on the loop, not out of it. The more autonomous the system, the more deliberate your checkpoints must be.
  2. Make the objective and the constraints explicit. Agents drift when the goal is fuzzy.
  3. Verify at the seams. Check where one step hands off to the next — that's where errors compound.
  4. Keep a kill switch. Know how to stop, inspect, and correct a run.

How to start

Pick one multi-step task you understand well enough to check — a research memo, a data pull, a content pipeline. Set it up as a project, define the checkpoints, run it, and grade the output honestly. Expand autonomy only as far as your ability to verify keeps up. Pair this with Vertical AI Agents and The Bitter Lesson.
