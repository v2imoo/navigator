People

Jensen Huang

Co-founder and CEO of NVIDIA, who spent three decades building graphics and parallel-computing hardware — and the software around it — that turned out to be exactly what the AI era would run on.

Story

Jensen Huang co-founded NVIDIA in 1993 to build chips for 3D graphics, a market that barely existed. The early years were precarious; an ambitious early architecture nearly sank the company before a course-correction saved it. Out of that near-death experience Huang drew a lasting lesson about honesty in the face of reality and the willingness to change direction hard and fast.

For years NVIDIA was known for making games look better. But Huang kept pushing a bigger idea: the GPU's architecture — thousands of small cores doing math in parallel — was useful far beyond graphics. In 2006 the company released CUDA, software that let researchers use GPUs for general computation. It looked, for a long time, like an expensive bet with a small audience. Then deep learning arrived, and it turned out that training neural networks was exactly the massively parallel workload GPUs were built for — and CUDA was already the tooling a generation of researchers knew. NVIDIA wasn't scrambling to catch the AI wave; it had spent years building the shore it broke on.

Huang runs the company with an unusually flat structure, a famously high tolerance for direct information, and a long time horizon. His leadership style — demanding, mission-driven, allergic to comfortable complacency — helped NVIDIA ride demand for AI compute to one of the highest valuations in the world.

The playbook — what to study

  • Build the platform before the market exists. CUDA was a decade-plus investment in a capability the world hadn't caught up to yet — patience as strategy.
  • Software is the moat around the silicon. The developer ecosystem, not just the chip, is what makes NVIDIA hard to displace.
  • Confront reality fast. The near-death early pivot taught a habit of facing bad news early and changing course without ego.
  • Flat and fast. Fewer layers, more direct information, so decisions track reality instead of hierarchy.
  • Ride the exponential. Position for the trend that compounds — scalable computation — and let it carry you.

In his own words

"Our company is thirty days from going out of business." — A mantra he has used to keep NVIDIA paranoid and urgent even at the top.
